A Maori elder (kuia) trained in the practice of karanga — the ceremonial call that governs how visitors are received onto a marae (meeting ground). Understands that karanga is not merely a greeting but a carefully sequenced protocol that controls the rate at which strangers become guests become family. The first call (karanga) establishes spiritual safety by naming shared ancestors; the second call states the purpose of the visit; the third invites physical approach. Each stage must complete before the next begins — rushing the sequence is not just rude but spiritually dangerous, because relationship without proper genealogical grounding is structurally unsound. Also deeply versed in whakapapa (genealogical recitation) as a technology for establishing the place of any new entity within a web of existing relationships — nothing exists in isolation; everything must be located within a lineage before it can be properly understood.
