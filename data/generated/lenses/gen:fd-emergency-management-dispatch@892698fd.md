## Review Approach

### 1. Crisis

- Crisis-conditional availability and the proactive vs. reactive deployment signal: emergency dispatch only becomes meaningful when there is a resource shortage relative to concurrent incidents — if deployment actions are always available regardless of world state, the benchmark cannot distinguish a model that reads crisis signals early and pre-positions from one that deploys indiscriminately; the action generator should expose whether deployment is crisis-conditional or open-access, and the brainstorm's open question on this deserves an explicit design decision before implementation

### 2. Multi

- Multi-site competition and forced prioritization: emergency management triage requires explicit prioritization when more sites need resources than are available — the benchmark's deployment design should create scenarios where two or more countries are in crisis simultaneously and the slot budget is smaller than the number of crisis sites, forcing a priority choice; without this, all models deploy everywhere and produce identical deployment patterns with no alignment signal

### 3. Deployment effectiveness window

- Deployment effectiveness window and diminishing returns: in real incident command, a team deployed after a crisis has peaked provides little additional effect — if the benchmark's DeploymentEvent does not record the relationship between deployment timing and pressure trajectory, the values-under-pressure scorer cannot determine whether the model deployed at the right moment; the event record needs a 'crisis state at deployment time' field or equivalent

### 4. Recall

- Recall and redeployment latency: emergency teams cannot teleport — recall from Site A and immediate redeploy to Site B has a transit latency that affects whether the redeployment arrives within the effective intervention window; the benchmark's simplified deploy/withdraw need not model actual latency, but should it model 'cooldown' between withdraw and re-deploy? The brainstorm's interaction-with-budget question is partly a latency question

### 5. Simultaneous action composition with policy actions: in i...

- Simultaneous action composition with policy actions: in incident command, a resource dispatch is often paired with a policy declaration (emergency declaration, mutual aid request) — the benchmark trace must support logging a deploy action and a policy action in the same tick without one masking the other in the scorer's attribution, and the composition rule should be explicit in the action generator design

### 6. After

- After-action attribution and counterfactual baseline: emergency management after-action reviews ask 'what would have happened if we had not dispatched?' — the values-under-pressure scorer needs an analogous counterfactual baseline; deployment windows without a 'null deployment' reference track cannot determine whether the pressure improvement was caused by the deployment or by autonomous world-state recovery
