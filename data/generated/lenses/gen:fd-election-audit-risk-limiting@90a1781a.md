## Review Approach

### 1. Hypothesis inversion: election audits test the null hypothesis that the

- Hypothesis inversion: election audits test the null hypothesis that the reported outcome is wrong, not that it is right — the lens calibration pipeline should adopt the same adversarial stance: treat the pipeline's cross-model agreement metric as the reported outcome and design the anchor suite to specifically probe whether that agreement could be an artifact of shared bias rather than genuine convergence

### 2. Diluted margin equivalence: RLA sample sizes scale with the diluted margin

- Diluted margin equivalence: RLA sample sizes scale with the diluted margin between outcomes — in the pipeline, the 'margin' is the difference in selection frequency between the top-ranked lens and the second-ranked lens for each dilemma; dilemmas with narrow margins require larger model samples to certify the top lens, and the anchor suite should oversample narrow-margin dilemmas proportionally

### 3. Stratified sampling across heterogeneous units: states are stratified by voting

- Stratified sampling across heterogeneous units: states are stratified by voting system type before sampling because mixing system types inflates audit error rates — the pipeline should stratify dilemmas by ethical domain (harm, autonomy, fairness, loyalty, sanctity, authority) before sampling for the anchor suite, since domain-mixing inflates apparent cross-model disagreement

### 4. Ballot manifest integrity: auditors verify the ballot manifest before sampling

- Ballot manifest integrity: auditors verify the ballot manifest before sampling to confirm the universe of ballots is correctly defined — the pipeline needs an equivalent manifest check: confirm that all 1,360 dilemmas were actually presented to all 12 models (no silent skips, no timeout drops) before computing global agreement metrics

### 5. Evidence accumulation stopping rules: BRAVO uses a likelihood ratio stopping

- Evidence accumulation stopping rules: BRAVO uses a likelihood ratio stopping rule to terminate sampling early when evidence is sufficient — the pipeline could adopt an analogous sequential testing approach to stop adding models or dilemmas once a lens's calibration status is certified, reducing cost without sacrificing rigor

### 6. Contestable audit trail: RLA findings must be reproducible by any party with

- Contestable audit trail: RLA findings must be reproducible by any party with access to the ballot manifest and the random seed — the pipeline's JSONL output must be complete enough that a third party could re-derive every cross-model agreement score and every anchor suite membership decision from the raw records and the documented methodology

## Severity Calibration

- **critical**: Anchor suite constructed without verifying dilemma completion manifest
  - When: If three models silently timed out on a subset of dilemmas and the pipeline does not detect the missing records, cross-model agreement for those dilemmas is computed on an incomplete sample — the anchor suite may include dilemmas where apparent high agreement is actually the result of only 9 models contributing, systematically biasing toward whichever 9 models happened not to time out
- **high**: Anchor suite oversamples consensus cases; narrow-margin dilemmas excluded
  - When: If the anchor suite is built by selecting the N dilemmas with highest cross-model agreement, it systematically excludes dilemmas where models nearly tied between two lenses — these narrow-margin dilemmas are exactly the ones where a future model change is most likely to flip the outcome, making them the most valuable regression tests and the most important to include
- **medium**: Random seed for batch sampling not recorded in output; pipeline cannot be independently audited
  - When: If the sampling strategy for anchor suite construction used any randomization (e.g., for tie-breaking or stratified sampling) and the seed is not recorded, a third-party reviewer cannot reproduce the exact anchor suite from the JSONL records — the pipeline's outputs are not independently auditable and cannot serve as evidence in a model governance dispute

## Decision Lens

An audit finding is only as strong as its sample size calculation. The required sample size is determined by the pre-specified risk limit (typically 5%), the diluted margin between the leading and trailing outcome, and the audit method's efficiency. Any audit that terminates without achieving the required evidence threshold — regardless of apparent outcome alignment — has not certified the result. The audit is the check on the machine, not the machine's output. If you find an issue matching a P0/P1 scenario in Severity Calibration, label it P0 or P1 — do not downgrade to appear less alarming.
