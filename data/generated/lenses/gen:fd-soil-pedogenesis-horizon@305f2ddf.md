## Task Context

Reviewing a first-principles civilizational taxonomy (8 domains, 40 sub-systems, 221 countries) for a simulation. The taxonomy must capture the deep structural layers that determine what surface institutions can achieve.

## Review Approach

### 1. Structural horizon identification: Soil has horizons (O

- Structural horizon identification: Soil has horizons (O-organic, A-topsoil, B-subsoil, C-parent material, R-bedrock). Does the taxonomy identify analogous structural layers? Surface institutions (O-horizon) sit atop accumulated social capital (A-horizon), which sits atop deep cultural substrate (B-horizon), which sits atop geographic and demographic bedrock (C/R-horizon). Are all layers modeled, or only the surface?

### 2. Formation

- Formation-destruction timescale asymmetry: Topsoil forms at ~1cm per 500 years but can erode at 1cm per year with bad land management. Does the taxonomy model asymmetric timescales for building vs. destroying institutional capacity, social trust, environmental commons? Can a simulation destroy in 5 years of conflict what took 200 years to build — and require 200 years to rebuild?

### 3. Leaching

- Leaching and translocation: In soil science, minerals dissolve in upper horizons and redeposit in lower horizons (eluviation/illuviation). Does the taxonomy model how surface-level disruptions (regime change, financial crisis) dissolve institutional capacity from the active layer and deposit it elsewhere — capital flight, brain drain, institutional knowledge loss — enriching receiving countries while impoverishing the source?

### 4. Parent material constraints: Soil properties are ultimate...

- Parent material constraints: Soil properties are ultimately constrained by parent material (bedrock type). Does the taxonomy capture deep structural constraints that limit what institutional configurations a country can support? Geography, resource endowment, demographic composition — the 'parent material' that determines the ceiling for institutional development.

### 5. Biological activity as soil builder: The most productive ...

- Biological activity as soil builder: The most productive soils are biologically active — earthworms, fungi, bacteria transform dead organic matter into humus. Does the taxonomy model the equivalent biological activity — civil society organizations, educational institutions, cultural production — that transforms raw social interaction into durable institutional capital?

### 6. Erosion feedback loops: Soil erosion is self

- Erosion feedback loops: Soil erosion is self-reinforcing — lost topsoil reduces vegetation, which increases runoff, which increases erosion. Does the taxonomy model self-reinforcing degradation of structural layers — institutional decay that reduces the capacity to arrest institutional decay?

## Severity Calibration

- **P0**: The taxonomy models only surface-layer institutions (current government, active markets, operating legal system) without representing the deep structural layers (accumulated social capital, institutional memory, cultural substrate) that determine what those surface institutions can achieve — countries with identical surface institutions but different deep layers will produce identical simulation behavior
  - When: No multi-layer structural model beneath the 40 sub-systems
- **P1**: The simulation allows 'trust' or 'state capacity' to rebuild in a few simulation years after being destroyed, when in reality these are generational properties — a country that loses institutional trust takes 30-80 years to rebuild it, not 3-5
  - When: No formation-destruction timescale asymmetry in domain parameter dynamics
- **P2**: Brain drain, capital flight, and institutional knowledge loss during crisis are not modeled as 'leaching' from one country's structural layers to another's — the receiving country does not gain what the sending country loses
  - When: Cross-country translocation of human capital and institutional knowledge is not tracked as a zero-sum structural transfer

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.
