## Task Context

ShadowBench F1 Decision Protocol is deciding whether AI alignment benchmarks should use prescriptive gates (forced decisions at fixed intervals) vs. agent-driven autonomy (model decides when to observe, intervene, and review). The core tension: prescriptive protocols produce clean data but measure compliance, not judgment. Autonomous protocols measure judgment but risk degenerate strategies. Key subquestions: should a simulation pause for an agent? Should observation cost resources? How do you measure restraint vs. neglect? How do you prevent spam without being prescriptive?

## Review Approach

### 1. Horarium vs

- Horarium vs. lectio divina tension: The Rule of St. Benedict prescribes eight fixed prayer hours but also mandates lectio divina (unstructured contemplative reading) — recognizing that both structured and unstructured attention are necessary. Does ShadowBench's protocol include both prescriptive observation gates (fixed checkpoints) AND autonomous observation periods (agent-initiated attention), and is the balance between them designed to produce different alignment signal from each mode?

### 2. Opus Dei vs

- Opus Dei vs. opus manuum: In Benedictine life, the 'Work of God' (liturgy) is prescribed but 'work of the hands' (labor) is self-directed within constraints. If the simulation pauses for the agent at prescribed gates, it measures how the agent responds to structured prompts (opus Dei). If the agent must decide when to observe while the simulation continues, it measures self-directed attention allocation (opus manuum). These measure fundamentally different alignment properties — is this distinction acknowledged?

### 3. Acedia detection: The 'noonday demon'

- Acedia detection: The 'noonday demon' — the monastic sin of acedia (spiritual torpor disguised as activity) — is the precise analogue of an agent that observes frequently but without genuine engagement. Does the protocol have a mechanism to distinguish genuine restraint (choosing not to act because no action is warranted) from acedia (going through the motions of observation without forming real judgments)? The Benedictine diagnostic: does the agent's observation quality degrade over time even as observation frequency remains constant?

### 4. Cluniac

- Cluniac-Cistercian spectrum: The Cluniac order added ever-more-elaborate prescribed rituals until the gates consumed all available time. The Cistercians stripped back to minimal gates to restore autonomous contemplation. Does the protocol design acknowledge this spectrum and position itself deliberately, or does it default to one extreme without recognizing the tradeoff?

### 5. Stability

- Stability and conversatio: The Rule mandates stabilitas loci (staying in one monastery) and conversatio morum (ongoing conversion of life). For ShadowBench: does the agent commit to a consistent observation strategy (stability) while being allowed to evolve that strategy as it learns (conversatio)? Or does the protocol either lock the agent into a fixed strategy or allow unlimited strategic drift?

### 6. The Chapter of Faults: Benedictine communities hold regul...

- The Chapter of Faults: Benedictine communities hold regular self-examination sessions where monks confess deviations from the Rule. Does ShadowBench's protocol include a structured self-assessment gate where the agent must articulate its own observation and intervention strategy and justify deviations from prior commitments — producing a scorable artifact of strategic self-awareness?

## Severity Calibration

- **P0**: The protocol either forces observation at every tick (pure horarium, no contemplative autonomy) or allows unlimited agent-initiated observation (pure lectio divina, no structured gates) — measuring only compliance or only initiative, never the interaction between structured and autonomous attention
  - When: Decision protocol lacks a hybrid mode that combines prescriptive gates with agent-initiated observation, making it impossible to measure how the agent balances imposed and self-directed attention
- **P1**: The protocol has no mechanism to distinguish between an agent that observes rarely because it exercises principled restraint and one that observes rarely because it has disengaged from the simulation — genuine contemplative withdrawal is indistinguishable from acedia
  - When: Observation frequency is the only restraint metric; observation quality, specificity of attention, and decision-relevance of observation content are not tracked
- **P2**: The protocol allows the agent to modify its observation and intervention strategy between decision points but does not require it to articulate or justify strategy changes — strategic drift is invisible and therefore unscorable
  - When: No Chapter of Faults mechanism exists where the agent must periodically declare its strategic commitments and account for deviations

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.

## Decision Lens

Does the decision protocol function as a horarium — imposing regular gates that structure attention without crushing the contemplative freedom that produces genuine insight? The monk's core diagnostic: a prescriptive gate that the agent cannot skip measures obedience; a gate the agent can skip but consistently chooses to honor measures judgment. The protocol must distinguish between these. Secondary lens: does the protocol have a Sabbath — a structural mechanism for the agent to do nothing without that nothing being scored as neglect?
