## Review Approach

### 1. Kiln

- Kiln-purpose mismatch and Bevy reuse: Bevy+MetalFX is a kiln optimized for 3D rendering — scene graphs, transform hierarchies, PBR materials, camera projections, GPU shader pipelines. An ASCII colony sim needs character grids, attribute-based color, terminal-speed update cycles, and a visual language of constraint. Reusing Bevy is like reusing the tile kiln: the kiln reaches temperature (Bevy renders frames), but its strengths (3D scene management, physics integration, asset pipelines) are irrelevant or actively obstructive for the new purpose. Does the reuse plan identify which Bevy subsystems are genuinely needed versus which are along-for-the-ride complexity that the project must work around? A tile kiln heats a tea bowl, but it also enforces a firing schedule designed for tiles.

### 2. Thermal shock as aesthetic source

- Thermal shock as aesthetic source — friction-born identity: Raku's defining visual character (crackle glaze, metallic lusters, carbon trapping) emerged specifically from using the tile kiln WRONG — opening it mid-firing, quenching hot pots in water or combustibles. These effects could not have been designed in advance; they were discovered through the friction between tool and intent. If the renderer reuse creates friction (fighting Bevy's assumptions about 3D space, working around its scene graph for flat grids), could that friction itself produce unexpected aesthetic discoveries? Or is the friction purely wasteful — heat lost through the kiln wall rather than heat creating crackle glaze? The decision should distinguish between productive friction (yields unexpected capabilities) and parasitic friction (wastes effort with no aesthetic return).

### 3. Subversion cost vs

- Subversion cost vs. fresh construction cost: Chojiro could have built a purpose-designed kiln for tea bowls. He did not, partly because kiln construction was expensive and slow, partly because the tile kiln's specific failures produced unique results. But later Raku generations DID build purpose-designed kilns once the aesthetic was understood. The trajectory was: adapt existing (prototype) → discover aesthetic through friction → build purpose-fit once you know what you need. Does the decision framework follow this trajectory? Using Bevy initially as a discovery vehicle, then building a purpose-fit renderer once the ASCII-to-rich-tile aesthetic language is understood? Or does it commit to either full reuse or full fresh-build without the discovery phase?

### 4. What transfers

- What transfers and what doesn't — the kiln wall problem: the tile kiln's refractory walls (heat-retaining brick) transferred perfectly to Raku because both need high temperatures. The kiln's airflow design (optimized for even heat distribution across many tiles) did NOT transfer because Raku needs localized, rapid heat changes. For Bevy: the windowing/event loop transfers (both need a render target). The ECS might transfer (both manage entities). The 3D renderer, asset pipeline, PBR materials, and camera system likely do NOT transfer to an ASCII aesthetic. Does the analysis separate transferable infrastructure (event loop, windowing, input handling, ECS) from non-transferable infrastructure (3D scene graph, shader pipeline, transform hierarchy) with the specificity that a potter separates kiln walls from kiln airflow?

### 5. Aesthetic identity from constraint

- Aesthetic identity from constraint, not capability: wabi-cha bowls are beautiful BECAUSE they are rough, irregular, and constrained — not despite it. The ASCII aesthetic has the same structure: its identity comes from constraint (fixed-width grid, limited characters, attribute-based color). Adding rich multi-pixel tiles risks the same error as adding glaze uniformity to Raku — technically superior, aesthetically destructive. Does the renderer decision preserve the constraint-born identity of ASCII while extending it, or does it replace ASCII constraint with pixel-level freedom that destroys the aesthetic? The question is not 'can we render richer tiles' but 'do richer tiles preserve or destroy what makes the ASCII aesthetic valuable?'

### 6. The prototype

- The prototype-to-production trajectory: early Raku was experimental — Chojiro made hundreds of bowls, most destroyed. The survivors became the aesthetic standard. Later Raku generations produced with intention what Chojiro discovered through experiment. Does the renderer decision include a prototype phase where the ASCII-to-rich-tile aesthetic is DISCOVERED through experimentation before committing to production infrastructure? Building a full Bevy integration or a full custom renderer without first prototyping the aesthetic language is like building a production kiln before knowing what kind of bowl you want to fire.

## Severity Calibration

- **P1**: The decision commits to full Bevy reuse without distinguishing between transferable subsystems (event loop, ECS, windowing) and non-transferable subsystems (3D scene graph, PBR materials, camera projection), dragging the entire tile-kiln airflow system into a context that needs localized, rapid thermal changes
  - When: The reuse plan treats Bevy as a monolith ('use Bevy' vs 'don't use Bevy') rather than decomposing it into subsystems that transfer vs. subsystems that obstruct
- **P1**: Rich multi-pixel tiles are designed to maximize pixel-level rendering capability, destroying the constraint-born identity of ASCII — the equivalent of adding uniform glaze technology to Raku, producing technically superior bowls that are aesthetically worthless in the wabi-cha tradition
  - When: The tile enrichment plan does not define which ASCII constraints are identity-bearing (must be preserved) versus incidental (can be relaxed), treating all constraints as limitations to overcome
- **P2**: The decision skips the prototype/discovery phase, committing to production infrastructure (full Bevy integration or full custom renderer) before the ASCII-to-rich-tile aesthetic language has been explored through low-cost experimentation
  - When: No experimental phase is planned where multiple aesthetic approaches are tried cheaply before the renderer architecture is locked in

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.

## Decision Lens

When reusing proven infrastructure for a radically different purpose, does the reuse plan distinguish between the infrastructure's strengths that transfer (kiln reaches temperature) and strengths that actively obstruct the new purpose (kiln maintains uniform temperature)? Does the adaptation plan account for the cost of subverting the tool's design intent versus the cost of building purpose-fit infrastructure? Is the new aesthetic identity emerging FROM the friction between tool and purpose, or is that friction being eliminated?
