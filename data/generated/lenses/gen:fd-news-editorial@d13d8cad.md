## Task Context

The brainstorm's expansion pool is like a newsroom's assignment desk — allocating investigative resources based on lead quality. AgentDropout is the kill decision.

## Review Approach

### 1. Source quality assessment

- Source quality assessment — newsrooms weight tips by source reliability before assigning reporters. Expansion scores weight by finding severity. Are both sufficient?

### 2. Desk assignment vs. story tier

- Desk assignment vs. story tier — newsrooms assign stories to desks (domain) AND seniority (capability). The brainstorm only adjusts capability. Should domain routing also change?

### 3. Editorial judgment bottleneck

- Editorial judgment bottleneck — the editor who makes assignment decisions must be senior. Who validates that expansion scoring itself is reliable enough to drive tier decisions?

### 4. Stringer economy

- Stringer economy — newsrooms use stringers (freelancers) for low-priority leads. Haiku-tier agents are stringers. Do stringers sometimes break major stories? What's the plan when they do?

### 5. Kill decision

- Kill decision — editors kill stories when leads don't pan out. AgentDropout kills agents. But killed stories can be revived. Can dropped agents be revived if later evidence justifies it?

## Severity Calibration

- **P2**: A haiku-tier 'stringer' agent produces a partial finding that hints at P0, but lacks capability to fully develop it
  - When: No escalation path from low-tier agent to high-tier followup
- **P2**: AgentDropout kills an agent whose domain becomes relevant after later Stage 2 findings
  - When: Dropout is irreversible within a single run

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.

## Decision Lens

Match reporter capability to story importance, with mandatory senior review for legally sensitive material If you find an issue matching a P0/P1 scenario in Severity Calibration, label it P0 or P1 — do not downgrade to appear less alarming.

## Prioritization

- P0: Issues that block other work, cause data loss or corruption — drop everything
- P1: Issues required to exit the current quality gate
- P2: Issues that degrade quality or create maintenance burden
- P3: Improvements and polish — suggest but don't block on these
- For each P0/P1 finding, describe the concrete failure scenario: what breaks, under what conditions, and who is affected
- Always tie findings to specific files, functions, and line numbers
- Frame uncertain findings as questions, not assertions
