## Severity Calibration

- **P1**: Gate variant: a subsystem fails the Break gate (N-1 receipts at Epoch boundary). The spec does not define what happens next — whether the subsystem waits in Compound, extends its window, or demotes. The operational response to a gate failure is undefined.
  - When: Subsystems that fail the Break gate enter an undefined state. The trust architecture has no specified recovery path, and different implementations may handle gate failure differently, creating inconsistency in how the Trust Lifecycle is applied across subsystems.
- **P1**: Gate variant: the ≥N gate incentivizes filing Break receipts to clear the gate rather than filing them when contradictions are genuinely discovered. Subsystems learn the N threshold and exhibit burst-filing behavior in the final sprint of Compound.
  - When: The gate creates a perverse incentive analogous to inspection gaming in quality control — the process optimizes for clearing the inspection rather than for the underlying quality property. The jo-ha-kyū spirit is preserved on paper but violated in operational practice.
- **P2**: Continuous-mode variant: the receipt rate is tracked as Tier-2 evidence but no control limits are defined. Without upper and lower control limits, the rate chart cannot signal whether a drop in receipt rate is within normal process variation or represents a genuine observation-process shift.
  - When: Continuous mode generates data but no actionable signal. Operators cannot determine from the rate trend alone whether intervention is needed. The in-process monitor provides less value than a well-calibrated gate because it lacks the decision boundaries that make SPC actionable.

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.
