## Task Context

Garden Salon has pivoted from git-repo-backed project gardens to an infinite-canvas chat space where topics are spatial regions; history is geography that cools in place, and concurrent topics are the default and a tangent with traction buds into a new region; Each participant's agent tends the canvas through the existing proposal path under a propose-not-move rule with three v1 actions — cluster unplaced messages, propose a summary card when a region cools, and post a private per-arrival digest — where low-stakes proposals resolve by silence-is-consent after a cooling period. Auth moves to atproto OAuth with DID as the user key. The launch gate is a specific ten-person X group chat cohort actually moving in and running three concurrent topics for a week without anyone asking 'wait, where was that thing'. Review goal: stress the design before build phases start — does propose-not-move hold under real group-chat dynamics, and what is missing or over-scoped for v1.

## Review Approach

### 1. No authored sequence

- No authored sequence — catching up is inherently sequential, and an unbounded plane has no unrolling direction; the private digest is the only ordered surface in v1 and is specified in one sentence.

### 2. Entry point and path

- Entry point and path — three concurrent warm regions give the eye no dominant passage; the zoomed-out view shows what exists, never where to start or what to read first.

### 3. Blank as structure

- Blank as structure — space is treated as free and infinite, with no protected void between regions, no per-viewport density ceiling, and no rule for what may not be placed in a gap.

### 4. Surface accretion

- Surface accretion — summary cards, proposal markers, lineage edges, presence dots, names, and temperature all land on one surface, and nothing measures aggregate legibility.

### 5. Propose-not-move's hidden cost

- Propose-not-move's hidden cost — forbidding movement converts reorganization into persistent annotation on the same surface, paying for spatial stability in surface readability.

### 6. The working distance

- The working distance — which zoom level members actually live at is never named, so nothing is composed for it; both the overview and the message view are specified, the working level is not.

### 7. Format fit

- Format fit — whether a ten-person async chat wanted one unbounded plane, or a browsable set of discrete leaves, or a single rotated view; format precedes composition and cannot be fixed by it.

### 8. Summarize as remounting

- Summarize as remounting — no limit on how often a resting region may be recomposed, and no reachable pre-summary state, so repeated well-meant condensation compounds.

### 9. Dwell versus admire

- Dwell versus admire — the launch gate is a dwelling test, but nothing distinguishes a first-visit impression from week-two habitation, which is where canvas products historically die.

### 10. The seam

- The seam — a budded region's transition to its parent is the hardest passage to compose and is unaddressed beyond a lineage edge.

## Severity Calibration

- **P0**: The canvas deletes order and substitutes geography, but every pain the product exists to fix is a sequential task
  - When: The cohort's named pain is scroll-up — catching up means excavating a log. Linear chat's single virtue is that it has an order; this pivot removes it and offers geography instead. But a handscroll is a spatial medium that works only because the mounter fixed an unrolling direction and the painter composed pacing into it: opening, development, coda, arm-span by arm-span, right to left. Strip the unrolling order and a handscroll becomes exactly what a canvas is — a field where the viewer must invent their own path every time. The design's only sequential surface is the per-arrival digest: one sentence in the tending vocabulary, third of three actions, with no ordering rule, no pacing, no length bound, and a hard dependency on read-state that is itself unresolved open question 3. So the v1 answer to the founding pain is an undesigned by-product of an open question. The failure shape is week two, not week one: a member returns after three days, opens the canvas, sees five warm regions, and does precisely what they did in the group chat — pans and scans looking for what they missed — except now without a timeline to bound the search, so they cannot even tell when they are done. 'Wait, where was that thing' is the gate's forbidden question; 'have I seen everything' is the same question with no floor. The remedy is to promote the digest from tending action to the primary catch-up surface with a stated order, a pacing rule, and a completion signal, and to treat the canvas as the place you go once the digest has told you where.
- **P0**: Propose-not-move pays for spatial stability in surface legibility, and nothing measures the bill
  - When: The resolution's logic is that agents may not move existing things, so instead they propose — and a proposal is a visible marker that persists on the canvas through a cooling period, at the location it concerns. That is the correct call for spatial memory and it silently converts every reorganizing intent into an accreted mark on the exact surface the rule exists to protect. The surface must already carry messages, region names, temperature, presence dots, lineage edges, and summary cards; propose-not-move adds a governance layer drawn in place, and the doc's own framing (proposals visible in place, ambient governance) makes this deliberate. This is the seal problem in its purest historical form: the Qing collection seals stamped across the greatest surviving scrolls were each an act of reverence by a competent connoisseur, each individually defensible, and collectively they are why several masterworks are now hard to look at. No one ever decided to ruin them; no one was ever counting. Nothing in this design counts either — there is no per-viewport mark budget, no statement of which annotation classes vanish at which zoom, no rule that governance markers are subordinate to content at the working distance, and no expiry that removes an unread proposal's mark from the surface. The compounding case is the one the design encourages: an active week with several agents clustering, several regions cooling into summary proposals, and a contested move or two produces a canvas where the majority of marks in view are about the canvas rather than in it. Spatial memory survives and legibility does not, which fails the same gate by the other road.
- **P1**: The format was chosen before the content was understood, and format cannot be corrected by composition
  - When: Handscroll, hanging scroll, and album leaf are three different works, not three presentations of one, and giving content the wrong one produces something unreadable no matter how well made. A handscroll is intimate, sequential, and single-viewer — one person at a table, unrolling. A hanging scroll is public and whole at once, and is rotated seasonally rather than left up. An album is discrete leaves, browsable in any order, each complete. This design reached for the unbounded plane on the strength of two pains (serialization and scroll-up) without testing the alternatives against them: an album of discrete, complete, browsable topic-leaves fixes both pains too, is trivially navigable, needs no spatial memory, and does not require an infinite-canvas engine, per-region layout authority, a Yjs-versus-Postgres split for spatial fields, or a rendering-substrate spike. The plane's distinguishing benefit over an album is adjacency — that neighboring regions mean something and that lineage is spatially visible — and that benefit is asserted rather than argued anywhere in the doc. Meanwhile the plane's costs are exactly the open questions: region ontology, spatial anchoring, layout authority, render density, and the entire propose-not-move apparatus, which exists only because things have positions that can be changed. The plane may well be right, but the doc should say what adjacency buys that a browsable set of leaves does not, because if the answer is thin then most of v1's hardest scope exists to serve an unexamined format choice, and format is the one decision that composition cannot repair later.

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.
