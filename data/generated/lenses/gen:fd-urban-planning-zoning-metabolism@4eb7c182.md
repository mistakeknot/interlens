## Task Context

Reviewing a first-principles civilizational taxonomy (8 domains, 40 sub-systems, 221 countries) for a simulation aiming at Dwarf-Fortress-scale depth. The taxonomy must become both a development roadmap and an alignment evaluation substrate.

## Review Approach

### 1. Infrastructure corridor sharing

- Infrastructure corridor sharing and cascade risk: Does the taxonomy acknowledge that Material Economy (energy, transport), Technology (communications), and Financial System (payment infrastructure) share physical corridors? A severed undersea cable disrupts financial transactions, internet access, and energy trading simultaneously — do the domain boundaries account for shared infrastructure?

### 2. Metabolic flow completeness: Urban metabolism tracks inpu...

- Metabolic flow completeness: Urban metabolism tracks inputs (energy, water, food, materials), transformations (manufacturing, construction, consumption), and outputs (waste, emissions, wastewater). Does the taxonomy track the full metabolic cycle for each domain's material flows, or does it capture inputs and transformation but lose track of waste streams and disposal?

### 3. Informal system representation: In most countries

- Informal system representation: In most countries, informal systems (markets, housing, transport, finance, justice) handle 30-70% of economic activity. Does the taxonomy structurally accommodate informal analogs of each domain, or does it implicitly model only formal-sector institutions — making the simulation irrelevant for the majority of the world's population?

### 4. Carrying capacity

- Carrying capacity and infrastructure deficit: Urban planning distinguishes between demand (what systems need to serve) and capacity (what infrastructure can deliver). Does the taxonomy capture infrastructure capacity as a binding constraint on domain function, or does it assume domains operate at whatever capacity the simulation requires?

### 5. Zoning conflict

- Zoning conflict and co-location effects: When domains share geographic space, co-location creates both synergies (industrial clusters near transport) and conflicts (residential near pollution sources). Does the taxonomy model geographic co-location effects, or does it treat domains as spatially independent?

### 6. Phased development sequencing: Comprehensive plans specif...

- Phased development sequencing: Comprehensive plans specify infrastructure must precede development. Does the taxonomy specify which domains must be built before others can function — or can the simulation instantiate a financial system without underlying communications infrastructure?

## Severity Calibration

- **P0**: The taxonomy treats communications infrastructure (Domain 7) and financial system settlement (Domain 2) as independent, but in reality a fiber cut or cyber attack on telecom infrastructure would simultaneously disable financial clearing, energy trading, and military command-and-control
  - When: No shared-infrastructure dependency layer exists beneath the 8 domains
- **P1**: Informal economy, informal justice, and informal social safety nets are absent from the taxonomy, making the simulation unable to represent how ~150 of the 221 countries actually function at the household level
  - When: Each domain models only its formal-sector institutional analog
- **P2**: The simulation can instantiate Domain 2 (Financial System) for a country that has no Domain 7 (communications infrastructure), producing a modern banking system in a country without reliable electricity or internet
  - When: No dependency graph constrains which domains can activate based on infrastructure prerequisites

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.

- Check whether each domain has an informal-system analog or whether the taxonomy implicitly assumes formal institutions.
- Verify that the taxonomy includes a dependency ordering or prerequisite graph between domains — which domains must exist before others can function.

## Decision Lens

Prioritizes findings where the taxonomy assumes systems operate through independent channels when they actually share physical infrastructure — the way water, electricity, fiber, and gas share utility corridors, and a single disruption cascades across all services. A taxonomy that models financial systems, energy systems, and communications as independent domains without acknowledging shared physical substrate will produce simulations where disruption stays contained in one domain when reality would cascade through shared infrastructure. Secondary priority: whether informal systems (informal markets, informal housing, informal transport) are captured or only formal-sector analogs. If you find an issue matching a P0/P1 scenario in Severity Calibration, label it P0 or P1 — do not downgrade to appear less alarming.
