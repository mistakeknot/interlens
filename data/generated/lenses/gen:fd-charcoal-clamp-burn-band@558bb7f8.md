## Severity Calibration

- **P0**: A dead burn and a good burn satisfy the same criterion
  - When: DONE WHEN requires '120+ simulated centuries (settle mode)' and a chronicle whose events cite real places. Both are satisfiable by a run whose civ dynamics seized at century 8 and then advanced the clock 112 times over a static world: the tick count is reached, some events exist, every one of them cites a real coordinate. Nothing in the condition asks for event density per century, for events in the last third of the run, or for any quantity that a stalled simulation cannot produce by waiting. Duration is being used as evidence of activity, and the entire depth bet — that simulated history is richer than authored history — rests on exactly the property no bullet measures. A collier who reports 'it burned five days' has reported nothing; the question is what came out of the mound.
- **P0**: The simulation regime is a name in a parenthesis with no sign attached
  - When: OUTCOME says the history is 'run for centuries in deferred-cascade mode' and DONE WHEN says '120+ simulated centuries (settle mode)'. Neither states what deferral or settling looks like from outside, so nothing distinguishes a correctly deferred cascade from one that silently degenerated into eager evaluation (right answers, ruinous cost) or into no evaluation at all (cheap, empty). This is the whole regime the affordability envelope is being measured against, and it is asserted rather than observed. The vents are what you actually control on a clamp; naming the burn does not regulate it, and a mode with no reading is a mode you are hoping for.
- **P1**: The cost curve reports size and duration, never yield
  - When: 'the probe reports its own cost curve (systems x civs x members x centuries) against the 2026-08-07 affordability envelope' names four input dimensions and no output term. There is no history-per-unit-cost figure, so the curve cannot tell the difference between an expensive run that generated a dense chronicle and an equally expensive one that generated almost nothing — and its stated purpose is to locate the next engine bottleneck. Worse, nothing counts the losing burns: a probe that has to be rerun three times before it yields a readable chronicle has a real cost the envelope never sees. Yield is charcoal over wood, always, and the wood you turned to ash still cost you a season of cutting.
- **P1**: The only in-process instrument is offered as an alternative to the cold reveal
  - When: 'watchable in the terminal (civ_scale --watch precedent) or browsable via the mk-67q chronicle path' presents these as interchangeable, but they are not the same kind of thing. The watch is smoke colour: it tells you mid-burn that something is wrong while you can still open a vent. The chronicle is what you find when you rake the mound out days later. Taking the or-branch and building only the chronicle path satisfies the bullet while removing the sole means of noticing that a 120-century run went wrong at century 30 — and the condition, by treating them as equivalent, invites exactly that choice on whichever path is cheaper on the day.
- **P1**: Slice one is asked to be black charcoal and white charcoal at once
  - When: OUTCOME simultaneously wants a disposable fast probe ('worlds disposable until played', 'rests on a running probe') and a durable substrate for a program ('interfaces are designed for later deepening', a cost curve that tells the next slice where the bottleneck is). These are different kilns with different firings, not one artifact with two uses: the charcoal that lights in minutes is soft and gone, the charcoal that burns all day takes an hour to catch and cannot be lit in a hurry. The condition never rules which one slice one is, so the implementer will silently choose, and every ambiguous tradeoff in the build — throwaway harness or real crate, quick chronicle dump or a queryable log — gets decided by that unstated choice rather than by mk's.
