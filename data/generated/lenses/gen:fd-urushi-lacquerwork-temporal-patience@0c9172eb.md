## Review Approach

### 1. Does the system make the irreversibility gradient visible

- Does the system make the irreversibility gradient visible — can participants feel when they are sketching (sand-able, reversible) versus when a layer is curing (cooling period active, commitment approaching)?

- Are cooling periods presented as necessary curing time (structural, purposeful) or as bureaucratic waiting (arbitrary, punitive)? The framing determines whether participants respect or resent them.

- Is there a 'foundation layer' (shitaji) concept — are early decisions in a direction given more curing time because everything else will be built on top of them?

### 5. Can participants see the 'layer count'

- Can participants see the 'layer count' — how many irreversible commitments have accumulated, and how deep the current work sits in that stack?

## Severity Calibration

- **P0**: Users cannot distinguish between reversible exploration and irreversible commitment — they accidentally cure a layer they meant to keep wet
  - When: No visible distinction between draft/exploration state and governance-committed state
- **P1**: Cooling periods feel like arbitrary delays rather than necessary curing time — users try to circumvent or rush them
  - When: Governance ceremony has no visible rationale or progress indicator
- **P2**: The depth of accumulated commitments is invisible — users propose changes without understanding how many layers they are disturbing
  - When: No visibility into the commitment stack depth

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.

## Decision Lens

Governance ceremony exists because some transitions are irreversible — like lacquer curing, they cannot be undone or accelerated without structural damage. The system should make the difference between 'exploring' (sketchable, reversible) and 'committing' (curing, irreversible) viscerally legible. Silence-as-consent cooling periods are the humidity chamber — they exist to let the layer set properly, not as bureaucratic delay. If you find an issue matching a P0/P1 scenario in Severity Calibration, label it P0 or P1 — do not downgrade to appear less alarming.

## Prioritization

- P0: Issues that block other work, cause data loss or corruption — drop everything
- P1: Issues required to exit the current quality gate
- P2: Issues that degrade quality or create maintenance burden
- P3: Improvements and polish — suggest but don't block on these
- For each P0/P1 finding, describe the concrete failure scenario: what breaks, under what conditions, and who is affected
- Always tie findings to specific files, functions, and line numbers
- Frame uncertain findings as questions, not assertions
