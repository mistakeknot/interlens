## Severity Calibration

- **P0**: The design defines exactly two upward messages from the roles that touch the material — the executor's verbatim verify output and the validator's PASS/FAIL — and both are conformance verdicts. Escalation fires only on failure. So an item that is applied correctly against a plan that is wrong in substance produces a PASS, lands, and teaches nobody anything; and every observation an executor or validator makes that is true, cheap, and outside its item is discarded by contract. The arrangement's rate of noticing drops to its rate of breaking, and the 30-day baseline it replaces had no such limit, because the mind doing the work was the mind that would notice.
  - When: P0 because it is the answer to the document's own Q-E, it is undetectable by every mechanism the document proposes (a gate on token share, a verify block, a frozen-criteria validator, and a strike counter all measure conformance), and unlike a wrong item it leaves no artifact — the finding is the absence of findings. Three pilots run this way will report success and cannot report what stopped arriving.
- **P1**: There is no stop command anywhere in the shape. Two-strikes counts failed attempts, so an executor whose verify block never converges — flaky test, slow suite, a check that hangs — accrues no strike, and the orchestrator has no defined signal to recall it, hold it, or declare the item not worth finishing. Nothing in the design bounds an item by duration or by usefulness, only by failure.
  - When: P1 rather than P0 because a stuck executor is eventually noticed by a human and costs time rather than correctness — but the gap is structural, cheap to close (a recall message and a per-item wall-clock bound in the executor contract), and it is exactly the kind of thing that only appears once several items run unattended, which is after the pilots.
- **P1**: The executor may not expand scope and the validator may not use its own taste, so the only role permitted to judge whether a plan is the right thing to do is the orchestrator, which never touches the repo. The bans are stated as prohibitions on acting; neither is paired with an obligation to report. An executor that can see the plan is applicable and unwise has no envelope for saying so, and a defect report is the wrong envelope because the plan is not defective.
  - When: P1 because the prohibitions are individually correct and the fix is small — separate 'may not act' from 'must say', and add one bounded line to the report contract for each of unrequested observation and plan-is-unwise — but left as written they convert authoring errors into completed errors with a PASS attached, and the design's own escalation path cannot fire on them.
- **P2**: The design moves the plan body out of the orchestrator's context into a file, then leaves every other class of carried orientation knowledge — rejected approaches, flaky tests, load-bearing conventions, what the last three goals learned — inside the 220-320K that gets re-sent every turn and lost at every session boundary, so each fresh spawn is ignorant of things that were expensive to learn and cheap to write down.
  - When: P2 because the shape works without it and the cost is diffuse rather than acute, but it is the same move the document already made once for plan bodies with no principle stated behind it, and knowledge settled into the repo is inherited by every fresh spawn at zero orchestrator cost — which is the only free lunch available to this design.

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.
