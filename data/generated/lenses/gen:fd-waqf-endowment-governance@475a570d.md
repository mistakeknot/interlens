## Task Context

Garden Salon has per-garden governance with append-only attribution, purpose statements, owner succession, and an evolutionary governance path. The review examines these governance primitives through the lens of waqf law — one of history's most sophisticated systems for governing perpetual assets.

## Review Approach

### 1. The waqf deed and the garden's purpose
- A waqf deed (waqfiyya) is irrevocable and specifies the purpose in perpetuity. The founder's intent cannot be altered by subsequent trustees. Garden Salon's "purpose" field is described as "stable, human-authored." But is it actually immutable? Can the Owner change it?
- In waqf, if the original purpose becomes impossible (a school in a deserted area), a judge can redirect the endowment to the nearest compatible purpose (cy-près). What happens when a garden's purpose becomes stale or irrelevant? Is there a cy-près mechanism?
- Waqf law distinguishes between the corpus (the endowed asset) and the usufruct (what it produces). In Garden Salon, is the garden content (CRDT state) the corpus, and the directions/artifacts the usufruct? This distinction matters for governance transitions.

### 2. The mutawalli and owner succession
- The mutawalli (trustee) manages the waqf but does not own it. The waqf owns itself — a legal fiction that prevents capture. In Garden Salon, the Owner has significant power. Is the Owner a trustee or a proprietor? The distinction matters for contributor trust.
- Waqf succession rules are specified in the deed: family line, then scholar, then judge. Garden Salon's succession (90 days inactivity → read-only → admin transfer) is simpler. Is it sufficient?
- A critical waqf principle: the mutawalli cannot benefit from the endowment more than other beneficiaries. Can the Owner of a cooperative garden unfairly benefit from attribution?

### 3. Irrevocability and contributor rights
- Waqf contributions are irrevocable — once you endow, you cannot reclaim. Attribution records are append-only. But the brainstorm says "gardens can configure what attribution means." Can a garden redefine attribution meaning after contributions are made? This would be like a waqf trustee redefining who the beneficiaries are.
- Contributors who contributed under one governance model "must have notice before their contributions are reinterpreted under another." In waqf law, beneficiary consent is required for changes, not merely notice. Is notice sufficient?

### 4. Perpetuity and the platform's role
- Waqf is perpetual — it outlives the founder, the trustees, even the civilization. "Attribution survives garden lifecycle events" — but does it survive the platform? If Garden Salon shuts down, who holds the attribution records?
- The platform is analogous to the qadi (judge) in waqf law — the external authority that enforces the deed. But Platform Invariants are defined by the platform itself. Who enforces the platform's adherence to its own invariants?

## Severity Calibration

- **P1:** Governance transitions require only notice, not consent, for attribution reinterpretation
- **P2:** Owner/trustee distinction is unresolved — affects cooperative governance legitimacy
- **P3:** Platform perpetuity assumptions are unstated
