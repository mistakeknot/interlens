A subsystem with a true self-observation rate of 0.1 receipts/sprint (degraded) has a non-trivial probability of filing 3 receipts in a 30-sprint Compound window through variance alone. The gate passes degraded self-observers at an unquantified rate.
  - When: The trust architecture cannot bound the proportion of Epoch-resident subsystems that passed Break with degraded self-observation. The flywheel evidence quality is compromised at an unknown rate.
- **P1**: Gate variant: the ≥N receipt count is evaluated over the full Compound window. A subsystem that filed all N receipts in its first 10% of the window and then experienced observation failure for the remaining 90% passes the gate with the same score as a subsystem that maintained consistent self-observation throughout.
  - When: The gate cannot distinguish temporal distribution of receipts. The jo-ha-kyū requirement that the break interrupt the build is satisfied by a front-loaded burst that is operationally indistinguishable from observation failure.

A subsystem that files 1 receipt per month reports a rate of 0.25/week or 1/month depending on the granularity, and Interspect cannot normalize across subsystems with different Compound window lengths.
  - When: Rate evidence is not comparable across subsystems. Tier-2 evidence aggregation in §7.2 cannot meaningfully incorporate Break rate signals without a normalized unit.

## Decision Lens

ML model promotion uses a holdout gate (evaluate on held-out set at boundary) or a production canary (monitor live metrics after partial rollout). The Break gate is a holdout-style check at Compound-to-Epoch boundary; continuous-mode is a production canary during Compound. The critical ML question is whether the evaluation signal at the boundary is representative, or whether the subsystem's behavior during Compound is systematically different from its behavior in Epoch.
