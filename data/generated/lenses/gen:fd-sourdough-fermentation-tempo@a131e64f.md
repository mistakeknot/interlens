## Task Context

Garden Salon's collaboration model is optimistic by default — agents land changes immediately, humans review post-hoc. Directions have lifecycle phases. The review examines the project's temporal dynamics through the lens of fermentation: a living process where timing is everything and neglect has consequences.

## Review Approach

### 1. Over-proofing — what happens when nobody reviews
- In bread making, over-proofed dough collapses — the yeast consumes all the sugar and the structure fails. In Garden Salon, optimistic landing means agent changes accumulate without review.
- What happens when Ryan is away for a day and agents have landed 50 changes? Is the state still reviewable, or has it "over-proofed"?
- The 30-day staleness auto-flag for directions — is this the right tempo? In fermentation, 30 days is either perfect (a long cold ferment) or disastrous (forgotten at room temperature). The tempo should vary by garden activity level.

### 2. The starter culture — garden health maintenance
- A sourdough starter requires regular feeding. Miss a feeding and the balance of organisms shifts — you get vinegar, not bread. What is the equivalent of "feeding" a garden? What happens to a garden that's neither active nor officially abandoned?
- The garden enters read-only after 90 days of owner inactivity. But a starter can go bad much faster at room temperature. Is 90 days the right threshold when agents are still running?

### 3. Bulk fermentation vs shaping — knowing when to transition
- Bread has phases: mixing, bulk fermentation (rise), shaping, proofing, baking. Each phase requires different conditions. The direction lifecycle (proposed → active → review_pending → merged) is analogous. But the baker KNOWS when to transition — they press the dough and read the spring-back.
- What are the "spring-back" signals in Garden Salon? How does a participant know a direction is ready for review? The progress signals (orienting | developing | converging | review_pending) are a start. But who sets them? If it's manual, it won't happen. If it's automatic, what's the heuristic?

### 4. Temperature control — the environment shapes the process
- Fermentation speed depends on temperature. A warm kitchen produces fast, sour bread. A cold retard produces slow, complex flavor. In Garden Salon, what controls the "temperature" — the pace of agent activity?
- Can an Owner slow things down when the garden needs consolidation? The brainstorm's "seasonal consolidation" open question is exactly this: a cold retard for the garden.

### 5. The baker's touch
- An experienced baker reads the dough through feel, smell, and visual cues. None of this is in the recipe. Ryan needs the equivalent of the baker's touch — intuitive signals about garden health that don't require reading attribution tables.
- Is the Meadowsyn ambient field (L1) the baker's touch? If so, is it calibrated to communicate tempo and health, not just activity?

## Severity Calibration

- **P0:** Optimistic landing with no review tempo creates an over-proofing failure mode
- **P1:** No mechanism for a garden owner to control the pace of agent activity
- **P2:** Direction lifecycle transitions depend on manual signals that won't happen
- **P3:** Staleness thresholds are one-size-fits-all instead of activity-proportional
