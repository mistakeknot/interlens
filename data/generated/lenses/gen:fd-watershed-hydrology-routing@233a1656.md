## Task Context

Reviewing a first-principles civilizational taxonomy (8 domains, 40 sub-systems, 221 countries) for a simulation. The taxonomy must route flows along natural paths rather than forcing them through artificial domain boundaries.

## Review Approach

### 1. Watershed boundaries vs

- Watershed boundaries vs. domain boundaries: Do the 8 domain boundaries follow natural flow lines (where material, information, and influence actually flow) or academic discipline lines (where scholars have drawn departmental fences)? Money flows from savers through banks through capital markets through governments back to savers — this flow crosses Financial System, Governance, and Material Economy. Is the flow routed naturally or forced through artificial domain handoffs?

### 2. Time

- Time-of-concentration modeling: In hydrology, a rainstorm upstream takes hours to days to produce flooding downstream — the delay depends on basin shape, slope, and roughness. Do the cross-domain feedback loops specify propagation delay, or do they assume instantaneous transmission? A financial crisis in one country takes months to produce food insecurity in another — does the cascade model capture this delay?

### 3. Subsurface aquifer connections: Surface watersheds that a...

- Subsurface aquifer connections: Surface watersheds that appear separate may be connected by underground aquifers. Does the taxonomy identify hidden connections between apparently independent domains — channels of influence that are invisible in the formal topology but critical for system behavior? (E.g., personal networks connecting governance and criminal networks, or cultural ties connecting diaspora communities across borders)

### 4. Flood vs

- Flood vs. drought asymmetry: Hydrological extremes are asymmetric — floods are acute, visible, and dramatic; droughts are chronic, invisible, and more destructive. Does the taxonomy handle domain crises asymmetrically — acute disruptions (financial crisis, war) vs. chronic depletions (institutional decay, environmental degradation, brain drain)?

### 5. Upstream

- Upstream-downstream power asymmetry: Upstream actors control downstream actors' water supply — this creates inherent power asymmetry. Does the taxonomy identify upstream-downstream power relationships between domains? If Technology (Domain 7) sits upstream of all other domains (by providing the tools they operate with), does the taxonomy model this structural power?

### 6. Return flow

- Return flow and consumptive use: Not all water diverted from a river is consumed — irrigation return flow reenters the system. Does the taxonomy distinguish between consumptive use (resources that leave the system) and return flow (resources that cycle back)? Economic activity consumes some resources and returns others — is this tracked?

## Severity Calibration

- **P0**: Domain boundaries force natural flows through artificial handoffs — money flowing from households (Material Economy) through banks (Financial System) through government budgets (Governance) back to households requires three domain boundary crossings, each introducing translation loss and delay that does not exist in reality
  - When: Domain boundaries follow academic discipline lines rather than natural flow paths
- **P1**: Cross-domain cascade propagation is modeled as instantaneous when real-world propagation has significant time-of-concentration — a commodity price shock takes 6-18 months to produce food insecurity, but the simulation propagates it in one tick
  - When: Feedback loops specify topology but not propagation delay
- **P2**: The taxonomy captures dramatic acute disruptions (floods) but not chronic slow-onset depletions (droughts) — institutional decay, environmental degradation, and demographic decline unfold over decades and are more destructive but less visible than crises
  - When: Pressure system designed for acute crisis events but not chronic slow-onset processes

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.
