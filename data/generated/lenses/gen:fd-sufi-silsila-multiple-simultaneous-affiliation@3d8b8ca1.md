### 2. The silsila hierarchy problem

- In multi-affiliation silsila traditions, the question of which sheykh takes precedence in a given situation is context-sensitive: in spiritual matters, one sheykh; in political matters, another; in domestic matters, a third. The practitioner has a *situational priority ordering* of his affiliations.
- Does the single-value politics-axis capture the phenomenon of the Unc whose politics is *domain-dependent* — the IBEW Unc who is `vocationally-absorbed` on labor issues, `traditionally-loyal` on regional cultural issues, and `single-issue-coded` on a personal-history issue? Each expression is authentic; none is the single value.

### 3. The incommensurability problem

- The Sufi tradition explicitly accommodated affiliations that were *theologically incommensurable* — orders with different positions on the status of the Prophet's barakah, for instance — without requiring the practitioner to resolve the incommensurability. The practitioner held both simultaneously.
- Apply this to the draft: can an Unc be simultaneously `disaffected` (lost his side) and `traditionally-loyal` (still votes the way the family votes)? These are not sequential — they can coexist. Is this adequately handled by the formative × current pair (formative: traditionally-loyal; current: disaffected) or does it require a simultaneous-affiliation structure?
- The `disaffected` + `traditionally-loyal` cross-pressure is one of the most common real-world political profiles (the county-flipped Democrat who still votes D because he can't make himself vote R). Is this compressible into a single trajectory value or does it require its own representation?
