## Task Context

Reviewing encryption and privacy architecture options for Garden Salon, a multiplayer human-agent collaboration workspace. The core design tension is how to encrypt collaborative content (CRDT state, git repos, governance records) while preserving server-side capabilities (AI proxy, governance operations, relay, search). Options range from encrypted-at-rest to full E2E with client-side AI.

## Review Approach

### 1. Whether the encryption layer granularity matches the cont...

- Whether the encryption layer granularity matches the content structure — in urushi, each coat must bond to the previous layer and the wood grain dictates technique even when invisible; encrypting at the wrong boundary (too coarse: entire garden; too fine: individual CRDT operations) creates either brittleness or performance death

### 2. Whether the system retains shape

- Whether the system retains shape-knowledge through opacity — a lacquer master knows the wood form beneath fifty coats through years of handling; when the server encrypts content it relays, does it retain enough metadata (structure, size, participant count) to operate effectively without seeing plaintext, or does it become blind to problems it should detect?

### 3. Whether key lifecycle has proper curing stages

- Whether key lifecycle has proper curing stages — urushi must cure in specific humidity for days between coats, and rushing produces failures that are invisible until the piece cracks years later; does the key management design have proper generation, distribution, rotation, and revocation stages, or does it skip stages that will cause delayed failures?

### 4. Whether protective layers can be selectively removed for ...

- Whether protective layers can be selectively removed for repair — damaged urushi can be carefully sanded back to the affected layer and rebuilt; can the encryption design allow re-keying a single layer (e.g., rotating a garden key) without destroying all content or requiring complete re-encryption?

### 5. Whether the toxicity of the protective material itself is...

- Whether the toxicity of the protective material itself is accounted for — raw urushi causes severe contact dermatitis, and the craftsman must protect themselves from their own protective material; does the encryption infrastructure itself introduce new attack surfaces (key storage, key exchange protocols, client-side crypto libraries) that are more dangerous than the plaintext exposure they prevent?

### 6. Whether the finish serves the object or the craftsman's v...

- Whether the finish serves the object or the craftsman's vanity — the finest urushi work is invisible, making the wood appear naturally lustrous; does the encryption architecture serve real user privacy needs or is it security theater that impresses auditors while leaving practical vulnerabilities unaddressed?

## Severity Calibration

- **P1**: Encryption key storage on the client introduces a more exploitable attack surface than server-side plaintext ever was
  - When: If browser-based key management (IndexedDB, localStorage) is less secure than the server-side access controls it replaces, the encryption layer is actively harmful — like sealing wood with toxic urushi that leaches into the food it was meant to protect
- **P2**: Encryption granularity forces all-or-nothing re-encryption when a single participant is removed from a garden
  - When: If the key architecture has no per-participant or per-session layering, revoking one person's access requires re-encrypting all content — a crack in the topmost layer that forces stripping back to bare wood
- **P3**: The server retains zero metadata about encrypted content, losing the ability to detect operational problems
  - When: If encryption makes the relay completely blind to content shape and flow patterns, the server cannot detect stuck sessions, abnormal growth, or synchronization failures — the lacquer master who cannot feel the form beneath the finish

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.
