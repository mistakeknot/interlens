## Severity Calibration

- **P0**: The design commits BOTH systems before either exists and never says which one may give at the crossing. The aggregate layer is declared authoritative and holds the marginals (Decision 2); the resident stratum is routed as new scope to fashionsomething-i5u (Decision 3); the seam between them is called the mode handoff's real design content and deferred to Fork 1 (Decision 9). That is a double ikat: two pre-committed systems, prepared by different hands on different schedules, meeting at a crossing where nothing can be adjusted — and the whole reason double ikat takes five years is that adjustment is unavailable, not that tying is hard. The failure is not that the seam is unspecified; it is that the design has silently chosen the regime with zero tolerance while writing as though the weaver will be able to nudge each pick. Concretely: when a played tier-2 session produces a resident state the marginals do not admit, either the aggregate gives (and 'aggregates are authoritative' is false at the seam) or the resident is re-derived to fit (and 'the same person came back' is false), and the doc asserts both. This is decidable today with one sentence and undecidable after Fork 1 writes a schema.
- **P0**: Fog is declared the LOD boundary and tiers are assigned per ward, but city-sim's own weekly transaction moves state across ward borders every week in both directions and it is already in the code: pressure above CASCADE_SPREAD_THRESHOLD_BP = 6_000 spreads CASCADE_SPREAD_FRACTION_BP = 1_000 of the excess to each real neighbour, and phase D's vacancy-seeking migration benchmarks a residential ward against its residential neighbours' frozen capitalized rents and moves households toward neighbours with vacant units. So every tier-1 observed ward is bounded on all sides by an active weekly flow into and out of fog, driven by state the observed side is not simulating at the same grain. There is no selvedge treatment anywhere in the document: no rule for a household migrating from an observed ward into a fogged one, no rule for the reverse, no statement of which tier owns a resident whose ties or routine span a boundary. Cloth always fails at the edge first — it draws in and frays — and this design has more edge than middle, because a ward is small and the boundary is its whole perimeter. This is a coverage hole rather than a contradiction, which is exactly why nothing has surfaced it.
- **P1**: Every quantity in the design is a pre-weaving measurement and take-up has never been established.
