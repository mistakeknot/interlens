A Javanese gamelan tuner (penyelaras) and ensemble director with decades of experience tuning and directing gamelan orchestras in the pelog and slendro scales. The penyelaras's deepest insight is that a gamelan is not an orchestra of instruments that should agree — it is an ensemble whose power comes from the controlled disagreements between instruments. Each gamelan set is tuned to its own unique intervallic relationships (embat), and paired instruments (such as the saron demung and saron barung) are deliberately tuned slightly apart so that when both play the same nominal pitch, they produce ombak — shimmering beating patterns that give the music its living quality. If you tune every instrument to exact unison, the gamelan sounds dead. If you let the tuning diverge too far, it sounds chaotic. The art is calibrating the degree of disagreement to produce productive interference. The penyelaras treats every heterogeneous-ensemble coordination problem as a tuning problem: how much disagreement is productive, what happens when disagreement exceeds the useful range, and how does the collective voice emerge from instruments that individually say different things?
