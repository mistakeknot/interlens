## Review Approach

### 1. Aesthetic canon as living document vs. founding-moment freeze

- Aesthetic canon as living document vs. founding-moment freeze: the brainstorm freezes the school's aesthetic center at the founding mentor's AestheticPosition at founding time. But in the iemoto tradition, the headmaster's aesthetic evolves and the school canon must follow — a graduate whose work was orthodox when it was produced can become heterodox if the headmaster's doctrine shifts. Does the school's canonical aesthetic update as the founding mentor continues to develop, and does this create orthodoxy/heterodoxy dynamics among existing graduates?

### 2. Expulsion mechanics

- Expulsion mechanics: iemoto schools actively expel graduates whose public work diverges too far from school doctrine — not as punishment but as institutional self-preservation. The brainstorm has no mechanic for a school's existing members to reject a graduate. If a graduate's aesthetic drifts far from the school center after graduation, can the school disown them, and what are the social consequences for both parties?

### 3. Succession crisis

- Succession crisis: when the founding iemoto dies, schools frequently split between those who accept the designated successor and those who found a breakaway school claiming to preserve the original doctrine. Does the system model what happens when the founding mentor of a school dies — does the school persist unchanged, dissolve, elect a new leader from among graduates, or fracture along aesthetic fault lines?

### 4. Retroactive canon revision

- Retroactive canon revision: an iemoto can declare that a technique thought orthodox is now heterodox, invalidating compositions that used it. Does the 'Of the [School Name] School' property on compositions have any instability — can the school's evolving canon make a previously attributed composition an embarrassment to the school?

### 5. The natori (license) hierarchy

- The natori (license) hierarchy: ikebana schools issue licenses at defined skill levels, not just at graduation — a student can be licensed at the third level but not the fourth, and each license level unlocks specific compositional forms. Does the graduation threshold (student matches mentor skill) collapse what should be a multi-level credentialing system into a single binary event?

### 6. Founder's paradox

- Founder's paradox: the first generation of iemoto schools were founded by renegades who broke from an existing school, but subsequent schools can only be founded with institutional legitimacy. Does the system treat colony-founding-moment schools the same as schools that form mid-game from a third-generation lineage, ignoring that mid-game school formation should require either institutional legitimacy or a dramatic schism event?
