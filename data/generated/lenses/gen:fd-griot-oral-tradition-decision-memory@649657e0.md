- Does the system distinguish between 'the record' (what happened) and 'the narrative' (why it matters now)? Both are needed — the record for accountability, the narrative for decision-making.

## Severity Calibration

- **P0**: Decision history is a flat chronological log with no genealogical structure — participants relitigate settled questions because they cannot find why something was decided
  - When: No structured decision genealogy, only chronological history
- **P1**: Human annotations decay into noise — early annotations become meaningless as context shifts, with no mechanism for recontextualization
  - When: Annotations are static and context-free
- **P2**: Agents propose changes without referencing decision history — they repeat suggestions that were already considered and rejected
  - When: Agent proposals are context-free, unaware of prior decisions

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.

## Decision Lens

The history of decisions is not a log to be searched but a living narrative to be consulted. Every proposal exists in a genealogy — it has ancestors (prior decisions that shaped it), siblings (alternatives that were considered), and descendants (work that depends on it). The system should surface this genealogy at the moment of decision, not require archaeology to reconstruct it. The griot does not recite the entire history — they tell the relevant part for the current situation. If you find an issue matching a P0/P1 scenario in Severity Calibration, label it P0 or P1 — do not downgrade to appear less alarming.

- P0: Issues that block other work, cause data loss or corruption — drop everything

- P2: Issues that degrade quality or create maintenance burden
- P3: Improvements and polish — suggest but don't block on these
- For each P0/P1 finding, describe the concrete failure scenario: what breaks, under what conditions, and who is affected
