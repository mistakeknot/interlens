## Severity Calibration

- **P0**: Comfort scale is hue-only with no CVD-safe redundant channel
  - When: A deuteranopic walker cannot reliably distinguish 'warm-amber' (moderate heat, proceed with awareness) from 'hot' (avoid, seek shade) by color alone in the exact scenario the app exists to serve (a hot summer walk) — this is a safety-relevant accessibility failure in the system's core signal, not a cosmetic one, and the canon does not flag it as a concern to resolve at the token layer.
- **P1**: Dark mode absent while the core metaphor (light/shade) is arguably most useful at the boundary hours
  - When: Evening/dusk walks are a primary comfort-routing use case (avoiding residual daytime heat, or routing for streetlight coverage after dark) and the canon's entire visual language is anchored to a light-mode 'paper in sunlight' metaphor with zero stated treatment for dark mode — implementers either ship no dark mode (accessibility/battery/preference gap) or invent one unguided by canon, defeating the 'every token cites a rule' discipline for roughly half the day.
- **P1**: Literal 'low contrast' cool-surface rule collides with WCAG AA text contrast
  - When: A component author renders map-skin labels or ambient chrome text at the described 'muted, low contrast' treatment and ships copy at 3:1 contrast against the paper substrate — this fails WCAG AA (4.5:1 for normal text) and is invisible in bright outdoor sun, the single lighting condition most relevant to a walking app, yet nothing in the canon requires a contrast floor carve-out for text specifically.
