## Severity Calibration

- **P0**: The inscription is cut into the mould before the profile is swept
  - When: DONE WHEN's first bullet requires a brainstorm to record mk's rulings on the GATE forks BEFORE implementation starts. Bullets two through six then specify, in detail, what the implementation must produce: a few hundred systems with spectral class, 120+ simulated centuries in settle mode, a chronicle whose events cite real places, one fallen civilization queryable in the present, a cost curve over systems x civs x members x centuries. But the GATE forks are precisely the rulings that decide what those artifacts ARE. If mk rules that celestial bodies are Souls with event-sourced genesis, 'a seeded galaxy scaffold generates deterministically' names a different object with a different cost curve and different axes. If mk rules the simulate/author line hybrid with grammar texture on top, 'every historical fact locatable at a real place on the map' means something weaker than it now reads. If mk rules Dixon evolution generated rather than stubbed, the substrate bullet is wrong. The condition has cast its inscription into the cope before anyone swept the profile, and the letters will be in the finished bell whatever note it turns out to have. Either the later bullets must be written as conditional on the ruling, or the ruling must be taken out of this goal and made its own — but they cannot both be fixed here.
- **P0**: The goal asks how thin, on the one dimension that only subtracts
  - When: GATE opens with 'how thin can the physical substrate be in slice one' and OUTCOME reassures that 'lower strata are scaffold-thin but their interfaces are designed for later deepening'. Metal comes off a bell on the lathe and never goes back on. A substrate that turns out too thin cannot be thickened in place: every stratum above it — species stubs, civ homeworlds and neighbourhoods, a simulated history whose events cite real coordinates — was generated FROM it and carries its shape, so deepening it means recasting everything above, which the condition itself half-admits by making the regeneration contract a GATE question. The whole strategy therefore assumes additive correction is available on exactly the axis where it is not, and the goal's opening question optimizes in the unrecoverable direction. Nothing in the condition states a margin: no over-provisioned field the substrate carries but slice one ignores, no rule that when in doubt the scaffold carries more rather than less. The characteristic bell-founder's error is a bell cast light because light was cheaper, and it is the one error the lathe cannot fix.
- **P1**: The deliverable is the shape of the void, and no criterion inspects it
  - When: A false bell is built with great care and then destroyed; what you sell is the space it left. OUTCOME says worlds are disposable until played and lower strata are scaffold-thin, and it rests the entire later program on their INTERFACES being designed for deepening — that is, the real deliverable of slice one is not the scaffold but the shape it leaves for what comes after. Yet every DONE WHEN bullet inspects the metal: a probe that runs, a chronicle that reads, a cost figure, a green test suite, beads filed. Not one inspects an interface. Nothing states what the species-stub interface must expose, nothing requires a second consumer or a stub-swap to prove the boundary is real rather than incidental to the one implementation behind it, and nothing distinguishes an interface designed for deepening from a function signature that happens to exist. The condition will therefore be satisfied by a slice one whose strata are welded together, and the deepening claim will not be discovered to be false until the stratum it was made about is actually deepened.
- **P1**: Five partials, no nominal
  - When: A bell sounds several notes at once and the founder tunes them against one another; you cannot chase them all, so one is the nominal and the rest are brought to it. This condition strikes at least five: deterministic generation from a seed, DF/Qud-grade historical depth, a chronicle a human can read, every fact locatable at a real place, and an affordability envelope. These pull against each other in known directions — depth against the envelope, grammar texture against place-fidelity, readability against event density — and the condition names all five as requirements at the same rank. It never says which one it is willing to be judged on, so when they conflict at hour six of implementation the sacrifice is made silently and invisibly, and the goal will be reported complete against whichever four survived. A goal with five equal notes has not been tuned; it has been cast and left.
- **P1**: No small bell cast first
  - When: Founders cast a small bell or pour a test bar to prove the metal and the method before committing the big mould, because the first full-scale casting is the most expensive place to learn that the method is wrong. This condition requires one end-to-end run at full scale — a few hundred systems, civs of member Souls, 120+ centuries — as the first evidence that four strata compose at all, and puts it inside a 20-turn budget that also has to absorb a human brainstorm, a workspace test pass, commits, a FINDINGS write-back and a beads filing. There is no required prior green run at a scale chosen to be cheap (a dozen systems, three civs, ten centuries) whose only job is to prove the pipeline joins up. Adding one costs almost nothing and converts the expensive run from an experiment into a confirmation; leaving it out means every composition bug is discovered at the highest cost point and latest turn.
- **P1**: The condition does not say which castings are bells and which are scrap
  - When: A cracked bell returns its metal to the furnace and none of the labour, and a founder knows before pouring what a failure will leave them holding. Here the final DONE WHEN bullet — 'Stop after 20 turns and report what was generated, measured, and still assumed' — sits at the same rank as the bullets requiring a working pipeline, a readable chronicle and a measured cost curve. Read literally it is an alternative satisfaction: a run that stops at turn 20 with a report has met a DONE WHEN, and nothing in the structure says that report is a scrap notice rather than a completion. That may be the intent, but as written the condition cannot distinguish the bell from the remelt, and a goal that cannot say what failure looks like will not be recorded as having failed. State plainly which subset must hold for the goal to close, and what of a stopped run is kept — the brainstorm rulings, the scaffold, the cost figures — versus what goes back in the furnace.
- **P2**: Size and note specified independently
  - When: Proportion sets a bell's pitch: you choose the diameter or you choose the note, and the other follows. This condition fixes magnitudes on four axes (a few hundred systems, civs of member Souls, 120+ centuries, and the members within them) and separately fixes an affordability envelope dated 2026-08-07, and treats both as requirements. If the envelope is real, at least one magnitude is not free; if the magnitudes are real, the envelope is a prediction rather than a constraint. The condition never says which way round it is, so the implementer discovers the conflict and resolves it alone — most likely by quietly shrinking whichever axis is easiest to shrink, which is exactly the axis the depth bet is about.
- **P2**: The bell needs the pit, and the pit is out of scope
  - When: A great Korean bell is hung over a resonance chamber cut into the ground; part of what makes the sound is not the bell. This slice runs 'on the measured palettice engine' while OUT forbids building the FINDINGS #2 snapshot loads, instructing instead to 'measure where they become blocking; do not build them'. If they become blocking mid-run, the condition gives no instruction — not a stop rule, not a permission, not a documented fallback — inside a hard 20-turn budget. The exclusion is probably right, but an OUT that names a known load-bearing absence must also say what the run does when it hits it, or the exclusion becomes a trap the run walks into at turn fifteen.

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.
