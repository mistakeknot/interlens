### 1. Noise

- Noise-to-glyph mapping coherence: mapping Perlin/value noise to a glyph palette requires a perceptually ordered glyph sequence — using '.' ',' ';' ':' '!' '#' in luminance order is conventional, but the correct order depends on the font's ink coverage, not Unicode codepoints

### 2. Biome character vocabulary: forest biome might use '♠' '♣...

- Biome character vocabulary: forest biome might use '♠' '♣' '|' for tree density; meadow uses '.' ',' '`'; rocky uses ':' '.' ',' — are these vocabularies designed with internal consistency, or is each biome independently assigned without cross-biome coherence?

### 3. Elven colony typographic identity: some roguelikes use a ...

- Elven colony typographic identity: some roguelikes use a consistent 'house style' glyph set (CDDA uses ASCII-only deliberately; Qud uses Unicode selectively) — what is Elf Revel's typographic identity? Are elven structures rendered differently from natural terrain?

### 4. Spatial coherence of procedural texture: pure per

- Spatial coherence of procedural texture: pure per-cell noise produces salt-and-pepper texture; artistic ASCII terrain requires spatial correlation (nearby cells should be related) — does the generation use spatially correlated noise or independent per-cell sampling?

### 5. Animation via ASCII texture cycling: slow glyph cycling o...

- Animation via ASCII texture cycling: slow glyph cycling on water tiles ('~' '≈' '~') or wind-blown foliage ('♠' '♣' '♠') creates motion without pixel graphics — does the animation system support per-cell glyph cycling with configurable period and phase offset?

### 6. Procgen vs

- Procgen vs. hand-authored tension: fully procedural terrain texture loses the 'authored feel' of classic roguelike maps; hand-authored glyph palettes per biome with procedural selection within palettes may be the right balance

## Severity Calibration

- **P1**: Forest and meadow biomes use overlapping glyph vocabularies — at a glance, a forest tile is indistinguishable from a dense meadow tile, breaking spatial orientation
  - When: No reserved glyph namespace per biome type; glyphs drawn from a shared pool
- **P2**: Per-cell independent noise sampling produces visually uncorrelated texture — tiles look like static, not terrain, because there is no spatial coherence radius in the sampling function
  - When: Noise sampled at cell integer coordinates without fractional offset or spatial smoothing
- **P3**: ASCII texture glyph cycling for animated water runs at the same frequency as the game tick, causing water to appear to flicker at 60fps rather than lazily undulate at 2-3Hz
  - When: Animation period hardcoded to tick count rather than configured in seconds with tick-rate normalization

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.
