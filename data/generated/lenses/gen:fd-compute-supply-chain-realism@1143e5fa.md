## Review Approach

### 1. Fab capacity concentration
- Audit the fab capacity concentration model: TSMC produces ~92% of sub-7nm nodes — verify the baseline data encodes this as a single-country production constraint (TWN), not spread across the East Asia region, and that no other country starts with >5% advanced-node share without a design justification

### 2. Equipment supply chain layering
- Check the equipment supply chain layering: ASML extreme-UV lithography machines are Dutch export-controlled and manufactured in the Netherlands alone — verify NLD appears as a second-order chokepoint distinct from TWN fab capacity, with its own shocked_ratio or denied_ratio field

### 3. Export control implementation
- Assess export control implementation: CHIPS Act entity list logic should block specific country pairs (USA→CHN for advanced nodes) not apply a global tariff multiplier — verify the control is modeled as a bilateral flow gate rather than a price modifier

### 4. Fab construction lead time
- Evaluate the fab construction lead time: semiconductor fabs require 4-6 years and $20B+ capital — verify the capacity scaling mechanic enforces a minimum lead time (e.g., 48-72 sim-months) before new fab capacity comes online, preventing states from bypassing the bottleneck too quickly

### 5. Second-order dependency chains
- Check the rare earth / packaging dependency chain: advanced semiconductors require neon (UKR), palladium (RUS), and OSAT packaging (MYS, PHL) — verify at least one of these second-order dependencies is modeled so that non-fab countries can still be strategic chokepoints

### 6. Data center resource constraints
- Assess data center energy and water requirements as local resource constraints: a 1GW data center requires regional grid capacity and water cooling — verify these are tied to per-country energy_grid_gw and water_stress fields rather than being abstract cost multipliers
