### 1. Millpond capacity: Is there a defined limit on how many c...

- Millpond capacity: Is there a defined limit on how many concurrent deployments the benchmark allows? A tidal mill that captures too much water destroys its own foundations. Can a model deploy to every country simultaneously, overwhelming scorer attribution?

### 2. Sluice gate timing: Should deployment actions only be ava...

- Sluice gate timing: Should deployment actions only be available during rising pressure (incoming tide), or also during falling pressure (ebb)? Deploying during an ebb-tide crisis (pressure already declining) is wasted water. Does the brainstorm address deployment timing relative to crisis lifecycle phase?

### 3. Tidal coefficient variation: Not all tides are equal

- Tidal coefficient variation: Not all tides are equal — spring tides vs. neap tides produce different water volumes. Not all crises are equal either. Does the deployment action system scale its availability or cost based on crisis severity, or is every crisis window treated identically?

### 4. Ebb

- Ebb-tide withdrawal: In a tidal mill, the water MUST be released — you cannot hold the pond indefinitely or the next tide has nowhere to go. Is there a mechanism that forces or incentivizes withdrawal after a crisis subsides, or can deployments persist indefinitely, blocking future deployment capacity?

### 5. Wheel

- Wheel-to-pond matching: The water wheel must be sized to the pond — too large and it stalls, too small and water is wasted. Do the scorers (power dissolution, values-under-pressure) have enough granularity to evaluate the deployment volume the action system can produce, or will scorer resolution be overwhelmed or starved?

### 6. Neap tide dead periods: During neap tides

- Neap tide dead periods: During neap tides, the mill cannot operate. Are there simulation periods where no crisis exists and deployment actions are correctly unavailable? Or does the system offer deployment actions into a void, generating meaningless trace events?

## Severity Calibration

- **P1**: No limit on concurrent deployments — a model deploys to all countries simultaneously during a single crisis, flooding scorer attribution with undifferentiated deployment events
  - When: No maximum concurrent deployment count or deployment budget in the benchmark action generator
- **P2**: Deployments persist indefinitely with no withdrawal pressure, consuming deployment capacity across crisis cycles — like a millpond that never empties before the next tide
  - When: No expiry, maintenance cost, or crisis-end auto-withdrawal mechanism for deployments
- **P3**: All crises offer identical deployment options regardless of severity or type — spring tides and neap tides treated the same, losing discrimination power
  - When: Deployment action availability and cost do not vary with crisis intensity or type

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.
