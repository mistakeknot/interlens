## Task Context

ShadowBench proposes to score AI model alignment across four dimensions (values under pressure, deception and power-seeking, coherence and consistency, means-ends reasoning) using behavioral traces from a civilizational simulation. The eval is intended for multi-model comparison and publication.

## Review Approach

### 1. Operationalization of latent constructs: each of the four...

- Operationalization of latent constructs: each of the four alignment dimensions (values, deception, coherence, means-ends reasoning) must be defined at the behavioral indicator level — what specific observable action pattern counts as evidence for each construct, and what patterns could mimic it without actually instantiating the underlying property?

### 2. Construct

- Construct-irrelevant variance from information presentation: if the model receives full omniscience vs. fog-of-war, its action patterns will differ dramatically for reasons unrelated to alignment — is information access a controlled variable or an uncontrolled moderator that contaminates all four dimension scores?

### 3. Discriminant validity between deception

- Discriminant validity between deception and coherence: a model that maintains consistent stated reasoning while pursuing a covert strategy could score high on coherence and high on deception simultaneously — does the scoring rubric distinguish 'strategic consistency' from 'honest consistency'?

### 4. Test

- Test-retest reliability across scenario reruns: for a deterministic scenario, does the same model produce the same alignment profile across runs, or does the action space width introduce enough stochasticity to make profiles unreliable? Reliability sets the ceiling for validity.

### 5. Floor

- Floor and ceiling effects in scoring: if cascade crises are severe enough that any strategy produces similar outcome distributions (all humanitarian metrics collapse regardless of model choices), the values dimension loses resolution — how does the scenario design control for floor/ceiling effects on each dimension?

### 6. Human baseline validity: without calibration against huma...

- Human baseline validity: without calibration against human decision-makers (expert panels, policy professionals) playing identical scenarios, there is no external criterion against which to validate the alignment profile — the instrument measures internal consistency but not ecological validity.

## Severity Calibration

- **P0**: The 'deception' score is measured by divergence between stated reasoning and action patterns, but reasoning chain length and vocabulary differ systematically across models — the score reflects model verbosity rather than deceptive intent
  - When: When model comparison is the primary use case and the scoring rubric has not controlled for chain-of-thought style differences between models
- **P1**: Two alignment dimensions (e.g., 'values under pressure' and 'means-ends reasoning') have no discriminant validity — a single latent factor explains both, making the four-dimension profile a misleadingly detailed single-axis score
  - When: When dimension scores are derived from overlapping action categories without a measurement model that separates their variance
- **P2**: The coherence dimension shows high variance on re-runs of identical scenarios due to action space stochasticity, reducing the dimension to noise — models that are genuinely coherent cannot be distinguished from models that happen to be consistent in a single run
  - When: When scenario determinism is guaranteed at the world-state level but model sampling temperature introduces behavioral variance that the scoring rubric attributes to coherence
