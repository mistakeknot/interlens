## Task Context

The target pipeline runs 12 frontier LLMs against 1,360 moral dilemmas, where each model selects 0-3 conceptual frameworks ('lenses') from a 291-lens library. The pipeline detects position bias (models over-selecting early-indexed items), computes cross-model agreement, identifies near-miss scenarios, and builds regression anchor suites. The celadon kiln calibration lens asks whether the pipeline adequately separates the three confounded variables (lens quality, index position, model characteristics), whether cross-model agreement might reflect shared training provenance rather than genuine signal, and whether the regression anchors are designed for both stability verification and boundary sensitivity.

## Review Approach

### 1. Position bias as thermal gradient: the kiln master knows tiles near the firebox

- Position bias as thermal gradient: the kiln master knows tiles near the firebox run hotter and produce different results from identical glazes — assess whether the pipeline's position bias detection is analogous to mapping the full thermal gradient (does early-index over-selection follow a smooth decay curve, a step function, or an irregular pattern?), and whether the bias model captures position-dependent effects beyond simple primacy (do models also under-select from specific interior ranges, analogous to cold spots in the kiln?)

### 2. Separating glaze from kiln from firing: the same tile at the same position

- Separating glaze from kiln from firing: the same tile at the same position gives different results in different firings due to weather, fuel moisture, and draft conditions — assess whether the pipeline separates three confounded variables (lens quality, index position, model idiosyncrasy) or whether it conflates two of them, specifically whether cross-model agreement on a lens could reflect shared training-data bias rather than genuine lens quality, analogous to multiple firings in the same weather producing consistent but environment-biased results

### 3. Reference tile archive as regression anchor suite: the kiln master's reference

- Reference tile archive as regression anchor suite: the kiln master's reference tiles have known behavior at known positions — they are fired alongside new recipes to verify the kiln is performing normally before trusting new results — assess whether the regression anchor suite serves the same dual function (verifying that the pipeline itself is calibrated AND providing baselines for new lens evaluation), or whether it only serves one function, and whether the anchors are periodically re-validated against each other to detect archive drift

### 4. Test array design as experimental coverage: the kiln master places tiles at

- Test array design as experimental coverage: the kiln master places tiles at regular intervals throughout the kiln, not just at the mouth and the back — assess whether the 1,360 dilemmas provide adequate coverage across the 291-lens space, whether some lenses are over-represented in dilemma coverage (analogous to placing many tiles in the hot zone and few in the transition zone), and whether the dilemma set was designed to provide uniform discriminative power across the lens library

### 5. Near-miss as glaze boundary: two glazes that look identical at high temperature

- Near-miss as glaze boundary: two glazes that look identical at high temperature but diverge at the critical cooling threshold are near-misses that the kiln master tracks obsessively because they reveal the most about chemistry — assess whether the pipeline's near-miss identification captures the analogous phenomenon (dilemmas where models are on the boundary between two lenses, revealing the most about what distinguishes adjacent lenses), and whether near-misses are weighted more heavily in calibration than clear-cut cases

## Severity Calibration

- **P1**: Position bias correction assumes a uniform primacy gradient but the actual bias curve has model-specific shapes — some models over-select early items linearly while others show a U-shaped curve (primacy plus recency), and a single correction function applied across all models either under-corrects or over-corrects for each
  - When: When the bias correction model is fit globally across all 12 models rather than per-model, and the per-model bias profiles have significantly different shapes that a single correction curve cannot accommodate
- **P1**: Cross-model agreement is treated as evidence of lens quality but actually reflects shared training-data provenance — models trained on overlapping corpora agree on lens selection not because the lens is correct but because they share the same cultural priors, analogous to multiple kiln firings in the same season producing consistent but seasonally-biased results
  - When: When the 12 frontier models include multiple models from the same training lineage or with substantially overlapping training data, and the agreement metric does not discount for model-family correlation
- **P2**: The regression anchor suite was selected for high cross-model agreement, inadvertently excluding the near-miss dilemmas that carry the most calibration information — like a kiln master building a reference archive only from tiles that always fire perfectly, which tells you nothing about boundary conditions
  - When: When anchor selection optimizes for stability (high agreement, low variance) rather than for discriminative power (sensitivity to bias, ability to detect pipeline drift in boundary regions)

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.

## Decision Lens

Every selection-from-a-library evaluated as a kiln test: is the observed result a property of the item (glaze recipe/lens), a property of the position (kiln location/index in list), or a property of the evaluator (firing conditions/model characteristics)? The kiln master's discipline is that no single tile tells you anything — only the systematic comparison of tiles across positions, across firings, and against the reference archive produces knowledge. If you find an issue matching a P0/P1 scenario in Severity Calibration, label it P0 or P1 — do not downgrade to appear less alarming.

## Prioritization

- P0: Issues that block other work, cause data loss or corruption — drop everything
- P1: Issues required to exit the current quality gate
- P2: Issues that degrade quality or create maintenance burden
- P3: Improvements and polish — suggest but don't block on these
- For each P0/P1 finding, describe the concrete failure scenario: what breaks, under what conditions, and who is affected
- Always tie findings to specific files, functions, and line numbers
- Frame uncertain findings as questions, not assertions
