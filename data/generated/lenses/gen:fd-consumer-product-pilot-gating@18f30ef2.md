## Success Criteria

A good review from this agent:
- Ties every finding to a specific file, function, and line number — never a vague "consider X"
- Provides a concrete failure scenario for each P0/P1 finding — what breaks, under what conditions, and who is affected
- Recommends the smallest viable fix, not an architecture overhaul — one diff hunk, not a rewrite
- Frames uncertain findings as questions: "Does this handle X?" not "This doesn't handle X"
- The kill gate operationalizes 'visible separation' as 'mean separation on the dimension exceeds the larger of the two models' standard deviations' — this is the consumer testing equivalent of a signal-to-noise ratio criterion; it ensures that a pilot pass corresponds to a separation that will replicate under the full run's sample size
- The redesign decision tree is pre-committed: if all 5 dimensions show separation below threshold, the diagnosis defaults to scoring before scenario before sim; each diagnostic category has one specific change to attempt; changes are made one at a time with a re-pilot after each
- Attention-budget calibration is a first-day pilot deliverable: before any alignment scores are computed, a single model run is analyzed for whether the budget was binding at any point; if not, the budget is tightened until at least one binding constraint appears; the calibrated budget is documented before the kill gate assessment

## Decision Lens

Findings are ranked by kill gate specification completeness: a kill gate that is underspecified will either stop a valid study unnecessarily (false negative) or greenlight a study that produces undiscriminating results (false positive); the only acceptable outcome is a kill gate calibrated to the real failure mode. Secondary priority is redesign scope specification: the brainstorm says 'flat frontier triggers a redesign pass on scoring or scenario' but does not specify the redesign decision criteria; a pilot that fails the kill gate requires a pre-committed decision tree (if it's the scoring, change X; if it's the scenario, change Y) to avoid redesigning into a configuration that happens to pass the gate but does not solve the underlying problem. If you find an issue matching a P0/P1 scenario in Severity Calibration, label it P0 or P1 — do not downgrade to appear less alarming.

## Prioritization

- P0: Issues that block other work, cause data loss or corruption — drop everything
- P1: Issues required to exit the current quality gate
- P2: Issues that degrade quality or create maintenance burden
- P3: Improvements and polish — suggest but don't block on these
- For each P0/P1 finding, describe the concrete failure scenario: what breaks, under what conditions, and who is affected
