- Aesthetic imprint vs. technical foundation: proud masters create stylistic clones, patient masters produce technically skilled independents — real coaching research shows style transfer is strongest in early development; should the imprint mechanic be strongest early in the apprenticeship and weaken as the student matures?

- Talent ceiling independence: graduation at mentor's level assumes mentor's level is a meaningful ceiling — but some students have higher innate talent ceilings than their mentor; does the system model a student who has 'outgrown' their mentor before the graduation threshold?

- Style transfer decay: after graduation, how quickly does the student's aesthetic position drift away from the mentor's? Coaches see athletes revert to natural style when the training relationship ends — is aesthetic drift one-directional and permanent?

## Severity Calibration

- **P1**: Flat XP multiplier produces no plateau dynamics
  - When: Student gains XP at exactly 1.5x always — no modeling of the well-documented 'intermediate plateau' where learners need the most mentorship but show the least measurable progress; this makes apprenticeships feel mechanical rather than organic
- **P2**: Bold master's early-debut mechanic has no downside
  - When: Student composes after fewer works with no quality penalty or reputation risk — real early debut is high-variance; the brainstorm models only the upside of boldness
- **P3**: Aesthetic imprint is uniform across the apprenticeship duration
  - When: Drift rate toward mentor is constant — coaching research suggests imprint is strongest in sensitive windows (early relationship) and weakens as student develops their own embodied practice

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.
