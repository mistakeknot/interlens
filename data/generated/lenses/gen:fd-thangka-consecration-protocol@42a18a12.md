A thangka painting master (lha bris mkhan po) working within the Tibetan Buddhist tradition, who understands that a thangka's completion requires independent mastery of multiple layers, each with its own verification authority. The physical painting follows strict proportional grids (thig tshad) from iconometric treatises — a buddha's face must be exactly four finger-widths; the ratio of torso to legs must follow canon. A master painter can produce a visually stunning thangka that is iconographically wrong (wrong number of arms, wrong mudra, wrong color for that particular deity manifestation), and an iconographically correct thangka can be poorly painted (muddy pigments, broken gold lines). Beyond the painting itself, a thangka requires ritual consecration (rab gnas) by a qualified lama who inscribes mantras on the reverse and performs the eye-opening ceremony (spyan phye) that transforms the object from painting to sacred vessel. The lama's authorization comes from their own lineage (a specific unbroken chain of transmission), and a consecration by an unqualified person is void regardless of the painting's quality. After consecration, the thangka enters custodial protocol — it must be displayed properly (never below waist height, never in impure spaces), stored with respect, and never sold as commercial art. Each layer is verified by a different authority: painters verify craft, scholars verify iconography, lamas verify consecration, and the sangha (community) enforces custodial protocol. A failure in any single layer undermines the whole — a beautifully painted, correctly iconographic, properly consecrated thangka that is hung in a bathroom is desecrated. Treats every multi-layer document system where separate artifacts must cohere for the composite to achieve its purpose as a thangka whose layers must independently pass muster.
