## Task Context

Garden Salon governance brainstorm where attribution (token count) is the universal primitive from which governance weight, influence, and eventual economics may be derived. Governance models include solo, cooperative, and patronage variants, targeting both technical and non-technical collaborators.

## Review Approach

### 1. Contribution legibility for non-developers

- Contribution legibility for non-developers — the design targets users like Ryan (non-developer). Token counts are a developer metric. Does the attribution display translate compute contribution into something a non-technical participant understands and trusts?

### 2. Compute vs intellectual contribution asymmetry

- Compute vs intellectual contribution asymmetry — token count measures compute spent, not the quality or influence of a contribution. A participant who proposes a single high-impact direction and attaches minimal compute may be systematically undervalued versus one who runs verbose low-quality generations.

### 3. Direction sprawl as a contribution gaming vector

- Direction sprawl as a contribution gaming vector — the design asks how to prevent direction sprawl. In open-source, this is well-studied: low-cost contribution mechanisms (opening issues, proposing directions) are systematically over-used because the proposer externalizes review costs. The design needs a friction mechanism.

### 4. Patronage model dynamics

- Patronage model dynamics — the sponsored/patronage model allows supporters to contribute compute to directions they believe in. This replicates DAO staking dynamics, which have well-documented failure modes: wealthy patrons can dominate direction selection, creating plutocratic outcomes the cooperative framing masks.

### 5. Attribution display timing

- Attribution display timing and finality — when attribution is shown in real-time during an active direction, participants may optimize their behavior for the displayed metric (run more completions to increase their count). Post-hoc attribution display would avoid this Goodhart's Law failure.

### 6. Recognition beyond attribution

- Recognition beyond attribution — in successful open-source communities, non-compute contributions (design review, documentation, synthesis) are often the highest-value work but go unrecognized in systems that only measure code commits or, in this case, tokens. The design has no pathway for recognizing non-compute contributions.

## Severity Calibration

- **P1**: Compute contribution conflated with quality contribution
  - When: If attribution (token count) is used as a proxy for governance weight in a cooperative model, participants are incentivized to maximize token spend rather than contribution quality. This is a direct analog to the GitHub commit-count gaming problem and will produce the same outcomes.

- **P2**: Patronage model without plutocracy guard
  - When: A well-resourced patron funds 80% of all active directions in a garden by committing large compute budgets. Even if voting is nominally equal, direction selection is effectively controlled by whoever can commit the most compute. The design names patronage as a valid model without acknowledging this dynamic.

- **P3**: Non-compute contributions invisible in attribution
  - When: A participant who writes the direction description, synthesizes results, and communicates with stakeholders contributes meaningfully but spends no compute. They appear as zero-contribution in the attribution system. Over time, this drives away exactly the participants most valuable in collaborative creative work.

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.
