## Severity Calibration

- **P1**: All agent actions produce the same visual-weight notification regardless of whether they need human decision or are informational
  - When: If proposal-generated, agent-status, governance-event, and error notifications all arrive with identical visual treatment, the escalation model collapses — Ryan calibrates to ignore everything at the cost of missing the genuine interventions, analogous to ICU alarm fatigue where life-critical alarms are silenced because non-critical alarms fire constantly
- **P1**: Ceremony-required governance actions block the active editing surface
  - When: If a governance transition (ownership transfer, cooling period start) fires a modal or interrupts the collaborative session rather than appearing as a parallel ambient track, participants experience governance as punitive friction rather than legible structure — the clinical equivalent of stopping an ICU procedure to complete paperwork that could wait until the procedure is done
- **P2**: The entry threshold delivers raw state rather than a structured handoff briefing
  - When: Showing '14 proposals, 3 active directions, 2 agents' as a count list fails the handoff test — a new participant cannot triage from counts alone. The briefing needs to distinguish what is actionable from what is background, in priority order, with enough context to act

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.

## Decision Lens

Every notification decision is evaluated against two costs: the cost of the alarm that doesn't fire when it should (missed deterioration), and the cost of the alarm that fires when it shouldn't (fatigue that causes the next real alarm to be ignored). The right design minimizes both simultaneously by anchoring alarm thresholds to clinical significance, not technical events. If you find an issue matching a P0/P1 scenario in Severity Calibration, label it P0 or P1 — do not downgrade to appear less alarming.

## Prioritization

- P0: Issues that block other work, cause data loss or corruption — drop everything
- P1: Issues required to exit the current quality gate
- P2: Issues that degrade quality or create maintenance burden
- P3: Improvements and polish — suggest but don't block on these
- For each P0/P1 finding, describe the concrete failure scenario: what breaks, under what conditions, and who is affected
- Always tie findings to specific files, functions, and line numbers
- Frame uncertain findings as questions, not assertions
