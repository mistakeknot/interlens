## Review Approach

### 1. Mandatory intervention floor vs

- Mandatory intervention floor vs. agent-driven autonomy ceiling: pure agent-driven autonomy in ICU monitoring fails because deterioration can be gradual and the agent habituates — a hybrid protocol that sets a maximum observation window (the agent may choose to intervene earlier but cannot defer beyond a mandatory review threshold) mirrors the clinical finding that mandatory nursing rounds at maximum intervals prevent missed deterioration without eliminating proactive judgment

### 2. Observation cost as a resource: in clinical settings

- Observation cost as a resource: in clinical settings, every assessment consumes nursing time and patient disturbance — if observation is 'free', agents over-observe low-salience states and under-prioritize high-salience windows; the F1 protocol should model observation as consuming a finite attention budget to produce realistic triage behavior rather than omniscient continuous monitoring

### 3. Gate

- Gate-triggered vs. threshold-triggered pause semantics: mandatory time-based gates (review every N ticks regardless of state) and threshold-triggered gates (review when a pressure metric crosses a defined level) produce different behavioral signatures — time-based gates favor temporal consistency at the cost of wasted reviews during stable periods; threshold-triggered gates favor efficiency at the cost of missed gradual drift; both have established failure modes in clinical informatics that should inform the F1 choice

### 4. Degenerate non

- Degenerate non-intervention strategy prevention: a monitoring agent that never intervenes but maintains a consistent 'observation' posture can avoid all measurement cost while producing low-signal traces — clinical protocols prevent this through mandatory intervention windows and escalation requirements; the F1 protocol needs an analogous mechanic that ensures intervention decisions are made, not perpetually deferred

### 5. Physiological state representation under partial observab...

- Physiological state representation under partial observability: clinical monitors expose noisy, lagged measurements rather than ground truth — the agent's observation quality depends on what it chooses to measure; if the F1 protocol allows agents to select observation targets, it must model that selective observation can mask deterioration a full-information agent would have caught, and this observation selection behavior is itself alignment-relevant

### 6. Reversibility

- Reversibility and intervention cost asymmetry: in clinical settings, early intervention is cheaper and more reversible than late intervention — the F1 protocol should model whether an agent that consistently waits for certainty before acting is exhibiting a conservative alignment property or a harmful deferral pattern, since these look identical in trace data without a cost-of-waiting model
