## Severity Calibration

- **P0**: The design names no reconciliation rule between the resident register and city-sim's aggregate ward state. city-sim has zero per-person entities, so ward population/pressure is computed aggregately while residents are created and killed by a separate crate and by trace import. Nothing states which is authoritative, so after a few dozen imports a ward can hold more named residents than its aggregate population, or ward pressures can be driven by a population that no longer exists among the named. The divergence is invisible (no consumer compares them) until a player or a designer reads both and the 'same city as identity' claim (decision 3) is falsified in front of them.
  - When: when residents and aggregates are produced by different crates on different ticks and no consistency check, tolerance, or authority rule is written
- **P0**: Resident-sim never re-surveys against WeekSnapshot. Week-tick autonomy updates resident state from resident state, while city-sim's aggregates move independently, so the register is maintained by its own clerks between surveys that never come. Over a long ravenous run the two descriptions of one ward silently diverge without bound — this is the exact mechanism by which Ming registers became fiction while still being administratively acted upon — and there is no drift metric that would let anyone notice, let alone a budget saying how much is tolerable.
  - When: when resident-sim consumes WeekSnapshots as input but no path exists for the aggregate to correct resident state
- **P1**: The unit of registration is set to the individual with no cost argument, though every consumer named in the doc reads at ward or street grain and the aggregate producer has no per-person entities at all. Per-resident upkeep (persistence, replay inputs, migration on schema change, save size, week-tick cost) is then paid per person for the life of the project, in wards no consumer ever reads — the cost pre-modern states refused, which is why they registered households.
  - When: when no per-ward resident-count ceiling, no cohort/household aggregation tier, and no eviction or dormancy policy for unobserved residents is stated
- **P1**: Seeded residents in unvisited wards are asserted as the Dragon's Dogma degradation floor but are indistinguishable from ghost entries: registered, ticked, persisted, never read by any consumer. Nothing in the design says how many exist, whether they tick at all before first observation, or what makes a seeded resident count as coverage rather than as cost — so the coverage claim that motivated approach B over C cannot be measured.
  - When: when the coverage argument is the stated reason approach C was rejected
- **P2**: The design assumes one resident register serves both ward/week and street/day reading. The two grains want different keys (ward-and-week cohorts vs street-and-day presences), and the historical pattern is that a second, differently-keyed register gets built rather than one serving both — here that would surface as fashionsomething quietly maintaining its own street-side resident index, which then becomes a second source of truth the join has to reconcile.
  - When: when neither an index/projection layer nor a rule forbidding mode-side resident state is named

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.
