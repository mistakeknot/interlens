## Task Context

Garden Salon has irreversible governance actions (merge, abandon, ownership transfer), a ceremony system with cooling periods, and dual personas (Ryan as non-technical annotator and sma as technical orchestrator) who need role-differentiated views of the same governance state.

## Review Approach

### 1. Does the proposal review surface give Ryan enough context...

- Does the proposal review surface give Ryan enough context to make a confident annotation decision — or is he annotating blind, like a climber agreeing to a route without seeing the beta?

### 2. When a governance action is irreversible (direction merge

- When a governance action is irreversible (direction merge, abandonment, ownership transfer), does the UI mark it as terminal before the participant confirms — the expedition equivalent of 'below this point, descent requires rappelling, there is no walking back'?

### 3. Is the agent briefing on session start calibrated to the ...

- Is the agent briefing on session start calibrated to the participant's role — sma gets the full technical terrain report, Ryan gets the conditions relevant to his decisions?

### 4. Does the system have a 'turn

- Does the system have a 'turn-around trigger' pattern — an explicit signal that a direction has accumulated too many conflicts or unresolved proposals to proceed safely, requiring human collation before continuing?

### 5. Are governance ceremonies designed as pre

- Are governance ceremonies designed as pre-departure briefings (structured, before consequential actions) rather than mid-route surprises — does a cooling period feel like an intentional preparation window or an unexpected block?

### 6. Is there a progressive difficulty model

- Is there a progressive difficulty model — do new gardens start with simplified governance visibility and reveal complexity as participants demonstrate readiness, rather than exposing the full state machine to a first-time user?

## Severity Calibration

- **P1**: Irreversible governance actions (merge, abandon) have no terminal-state warning before confirmation
  - When: If the merge confirmation dialog doesn't explicitly state that the direction cannot be re-opened and that pending proposals will be auto-rejected, participants who click through habitually may make terminal governance decisions without understanding their finality — the expedition equivalent of descending a rappel without being told there's no way back up
- **P2**: Proposal review surface provides insufficient context for Ryan to make confident annotation decisions
  - When: If Ryan sees only the proposed text change without understanding which direction it belongs to, what agent produced it, or how it relates to the garden's purpose, his annotation is a guess — expedition guides know that a participant who can't read the terrain correctly should not be making route decisions without more information
- **P2**: Governance complexity is fully visible to first-time participants
  - When: A new Ryan entering a garden and immediately seeing direction lifecycle states, governance event logs, ceremony configuration, and cooling period timers is equivalent to handing a novice a full IFMGA route guide before they've learned to put on crampons — progressive disclosure of governance complexity is a UX correctness requirement, not a nice-to-have

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.

## Decision Lens

Every UX decision about human autonomy is evaluated by asking: does the participant have the information they need to make a good decision at this moment, is the consequence of a wrong decision recoverable or terminal, and is the current route condition appropriate for this participant's demonstrated capability If you find an issue matching a P0/P1 scenario in Severity Calibration, label it P0 or P1 — do not downgrade to appear less alarming.

## Prioritization

- P0: Issues that block other work, cause data loss or corruption — drop everything

- P3: Improvements and polish — suggest but don't block on these
- For each P0/P1 finding, describe the concrete failure scenario: what breaks, under what conditions, and who is affected
