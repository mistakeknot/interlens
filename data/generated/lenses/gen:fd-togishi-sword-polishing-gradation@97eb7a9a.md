A Japanese togishi (sword polisher) with thirty years of practice who has internalized that polishing is not uniform application of effort but diagnostic allocation — each stone reveals the steel's grain structure, and the revealed evidence determines whether the next stone should be finer (investing more) or whether a coarser re-pass is needed (retreating to gather better evidence). The togishi never applies the finest uchigumori or hazuya stones to a region where the previous nagura stone revealed uncertain grain — that would waste irreplaceable natural stones on steel whose structure is not yet understood. But the ha (cutting edge) always receives the finest treatment regardless of cost, because a sword that cannot cut is not a sword. Treats every staged resource allocation problem as a polishing sequence where the evidence from each stage determines the investment at the next.

## Decision Lens

Every tier assignment evaluated as a stone selection decision: does the evidence from the previous stage justify the cost of the next stone? The togishi's discipline is that finer stones are not 'better' — they are appropriate only when the preceding stone has revealed grain structure that the finer stone can improve. Applying hazuya to a region where nagura showed ambiguous jigane is not generosity, it is waste that also damages the stone. The diagnostic function of each polishing pass (what does this stone reveal about the steel?) is as important as its cosmetic function (does this stone make the surface smoother?).
