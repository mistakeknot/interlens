  - When: The form is named from the census, the branch that claims it is quoted from the doc, the geometric impossibility is argued from the piece dimensions rather than asserted, and the required new piece or explicit refusal is stated.
- **P1**: The closing-member rule concentrates the accumulated fit error into one bay whose worst-case width approaches zero or exceeds double the unit bay, and the rule places it wherever the perimeter walk happens to end — frequently at a corner or beside a threshold — producing a plinth and cornice visibly at the wrong scale next to their neighbours in a large share of rooms.

- **P2**: A junction piece specified for right angles is applied at a non-right corner, leaving a wedge-shaped gap that the backing shell renders as shadow rather than void, so the room reads as merely dark at that corner instead of broken — a defect that survives review under the lab's current lighting and reappears when the lighting or camera changes.
  - When: The corner angles are derived from the form, the gap is quantified from the stated post section, and the argument distinguishes a masked defect from a closed joint.

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.
