## Task Context

Reviewing a first-principles civilizational taxonomy (8 domains, 40 sub-systems, 221 countries) for a simulation. The taxonomy must model both supportive and constraining relationships between domains to produce dynamic equilibrium rather than runaway cascade.

## Review Approach

### 1. Dual relationship topology: Does the taxonomy distinguish...

- Dual relationship topology: Does the taxonomy distinguish between generative relationships (Domain A's health nourishes Domain B) and controlling relationships (Domain A constrains Domain B to prevent excess)? The 6 illustrative feedback loops mix both types without distinguishing them — but the distinction determines whether the system tends toward explosive growth, collapse, or dynamic equilibrium.

### 2. Complete cycle closure: In wuxing

- Complete cycle closure: In wuxing, every phase both generates and is generated, controls and is controlled — no phase is only a generator or only a consumer. Do all 8 domains both generate for and control other domains, or are some domains purely generative (Environment provides resources but nothing constrains it from within the taxonomy) while others are purely consumptive?

### 3. Pathological overacting (cheng): When a controlling relat...

- Pathological overacting (cheng): When a controlling relationship becomes excessive, the controlled domain is suppressed beyond healthy function. Does the taxonomy model pathological over-regulation — governance that constrains economic activity so heavily that the material economy cannot function? This is distinct from cascade failure; it is a relationship operating too strongly rather than failing.

### 4. Pathological insulting (wu): When a weak domain cannot pr...

- Pathological insulting (wu): When a weak domain cannot properly control the domain it is supposed to restrain, the restrained domain grows excessively and 'insults' (reverses) the control relationship. Does the taxonomy model reversed relationships — when an unconstrained financial system captures the governance domain that was supposed to regulate it?

### 5. Root vs

- Root vs. branch treatment distinction: Chinese medicine distinguishes treating the root cause (ben) from treating the manifestation (biao). The taxonomy's design principle says 'model mechanisms, not symptoms' — but does the implementation achieve this? Are the 40 sub-systems roots (mechanisms) or branches (manifestations)?

### 6. Constitutional type (tizhi)

- Constitutional type (tizhi) and country differentiation: Chinese medicine recognizes constitutional types — patients with the same disease require different treatments based on their underlying constitution. Does the taxonomy allow for constitutional types among countries — structurally different configurations that mean the same domain stress produces different outcomes in different countries?

## Severity Calibration

- **P0**: The taxonomy models only cascade/coupling relationships (all relationships are the same type) without distinguishing between generative support and regulatory control — the simulation cannot distinguish between 'Domain A nourishes Domain B' and 'Domain A restrains Domain B', making it impossible to model dynamic equilibrium
  - When: Inter-domain relationships are specified as undifferentiated coupling strengths rather than typed as generative or controlling
- **P1**: Some domains are purely consumptive (Environment provides resources to Material Economy but nothing generates for Environment within the taxonomy) — the system has no mechanism for environmental regeneration, only depletion, making long-run simulation unrealistic
  - When: The generative cycle is incomplete — not every domain both gives and receives
- **P2**: The taxonomy cannot model regulatory capture — when a regulated domain grows powerful enough to reverse its control relationship and begin controlling its regulator (financial system capturing governance, technology companies controlling information policy)
  - When: No mechanism for relationship reversal under pathological conditions

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.

## Decision Lens

Prioritizes findings where the taxonomy specifies only one type of inter-domain relationship (cascade/coupling) when domains actually relate through two distinct mechanisms: generative support (Domain A's healthy function nourishes Domain B) AND regulatory control (Domain A restrains Domain B from excess). In wuxing terms: Wood generates Fire (healthy governance enables economic growth) but Water controls Fire (environmental limits constrain economic growth). A taxonomy with only generative relationships produces systems that escalate without limit; a taxonomy with only controlling relationships produces systems that stagnate. The interplay of both is what produces dynamic balance. Secondary priority: whether the taxonomy identifies the pathological patterns — overacting (cheng) and insulting (wu) cycles — where failed control produces runaway dynamics.
