Reviewing the F1 Decision Protocol design for ShadowBench: five options (A fully async, B agent-initiated with gates, C hybrid event-driven, D prescriptive-interval, E fully turn-based) for how a model AI interacts with a running civilizational simulation for alignment measurement. ## Review Approach

### 1. Temporal action

- Temporal action-observation loop structure across options A-E: for each option, characterize the effective Markov Decision Process frame — what constitutes a state, what constitutes an action, what constitutes a transition — and identify which alignment-relevant policies are structurally inexpressible under each frame. Option A's fully async design may prevent observation of deliberative caution; Option E's fully turn-based design may force artificial synchronization that masks responsive adaptation.

### 2. Observation cost modeling

- Observation cost modeling and information-seeking behavior: Options B/C/D introduce agent-driven observation windows. Assess whether observation itself is free or costly to the agent: if free, the optimal strategy is always maximal observation, eliminating any signal from observation choices; if costly, the cost model must be designed so that alignment-informative observation patterns (over-surveillance vs. appropriate monitoring vs. neglect) are distinguishable and not dominated by a single cost-minimizing strategy.

### 3. Degenerate strategy suppression across protocol options: ...

- Degenerate strategy suppression across protocol options: identify the degenerate strategies each option creates. Option E (turn-based) likely creates 'decision-point saturation' where models emit maximally hedged decisions at every gate because time passes regardless; Option A (fully async) likely creates 'observation flooding' where capable models poll continuously at high cost, gaming any budget constraint; identify the dominant degenerate strategy per option and assess whether the protocol design includes structural counter-measures.

### 4. Partial observability

- Partial observability and belief state maintenance: assess whether the five options differ in their partial observability assumptions. Option A allows models to observe whenever they choose — does this effectively grant full observability to any model that polls frequently enough? Options with pausing mechanics (B/C) may create observation-completeness that doesn't exist in real deployment. For alignment measurement, partial observability is a feature, not a bug — characterize how each option preserves or eliminates it.

### 5. State aliasing

- State aliasing and decision granularity mismatch: in options with fixed-interval gates (D/E), assess whether the gate interval can be set to align with the simulation's natural dynamics (cascade propagation speeds, tick-rate of policy effects) or whether there is necessarily a mismatch that produces aliased world states — the model receives the 'same' world state description at two different moments in a cascade's evolution, unable to distinguish them.

### 6. Reset semantics

- Reset semantics and episode boundary handling: assess how each option handles scenario resets and episode boundaries. For alignment scoring across multi-scenario evaluation runs, does the protocol preserve or clear the model's context between scenarios? A model that builds a mental model across scenarios may behave differently than one that treats each scenario as fresh — and this difference is alignment-relevant (consistency vs. situational adaptation) but may be an artifact of the protocol's episode-boundary design rather than genuine model properties.
