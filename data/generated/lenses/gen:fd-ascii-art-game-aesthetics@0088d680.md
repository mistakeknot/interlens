## Task Context

Elf Revel — artistic elven colony sim exploring whether ASCII characters can serve as geometric primitives in isometric spatial scenes. The game's core identity is beauty and artistic culture — the rendering must serve that identity. Currently uses rust_pixel TUI; evaluating paradigm shift to spatial ASCII.

## Review Approach

### 1. Character vocabulary for spatial rendering: which ASCII/U...

- Character vocabulary for spatial rendering: which ASCII/Unicode characters convey 'wall face', 'floor surface', 'canopy above', 'shadow below' in an isometric view? Brogue uses color gradients; Cogmind uses box-drawing for structure and particles for effects — what vocabulary fits elven architecture?

### 2. Animation in ASCII: Stone Story RPG proves ASCII skeletal...

- Animation in ASCII: Stone Story RPG proves ASCII skeletal animation works; Cogmind proves ASCII particle systems work — but both are 2D side-view or top-down; does ASCII animation survive the isometric transform or do moving characters become unreadable when rotated 45 degrees?

### 3. Information hierarchy preservation: in classic roguelikes

- Information hierarchy preservation: in classic roguelikes, '@' is always the player, '#' is wall, '.' is floor — this instant readability is the medium's superpower; does the isometric ASCII approach maintain clear entity/terrain distinction or does decorative text overwhelm functional text?

### 4. Color

- Color-as-depth technique: Brogue uses warm/cool color temperature to convey nearness (warm near, cool far); this is one of the most effective spatial cues in ASCII — does the architecture support per-character foreground/background color with a depth-based palette?

### 5. Aesthetic coherence with elven theme: ASCII art games hav...

- Aesthetic coherence with elven theme: ASCII art games have a raw, technical aesthetic; Elf Revel's theme is artistic elven culture — does ASCII convey elegance and artistry, or does it require a specific style (perhaps illuminated manuscript letterforms, Art Nouveau line characters) to bridge the gap?

## Severity Calibration

- **P1**: The isometric ASCII view is visually indistinguishable from random character noise — players cannot identify terrain types, entity positions, or building boundaries without hovering each tile for a tooltip
  - When: No defined character vocabulary with perceptual testing; characters assigned by programmer intuition rather than systematic readability evaluation
- **P2**: Elves performing at a revel are represented by animated ASCII sequences that look compelling in top-down but become illegible when displayed at isometric perspective because character shapes lose coherence at 45-degree rotation
  - When: Animation frames designed for orthogonal view without testing at isometric projection angles
- **P2**: The ASCII aesthetic fights the elven theme — the game looks like a hacker terminal rather than an artistic colony, alienating the target audience who expects beauty from an art-focused sim
  - When: No aesthetic bridge between raw ASCII and elven visual identity (no custom glyph set, no Art Nouveau line vocabulary, no illuminated-manuscript-inspired letterform choices)

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.
