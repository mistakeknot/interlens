The landing page is a choice architecture problem — visitors need to choose where to go, and the design should make the right choice obvious

### 1. Visual anchor points

- Visual anchor points — where does the eye land first on the landing page?

- Choice overload — does the portal offer too many options or the right number?

### 3. Decoy effect

- Decoy effect — is the wiki link positioned as the obvious best choice?

### 4. Section naming

- Section naming — do labels describe benefits or just categories?

### 5. Scan patterns

- Scan patterns — does the layout work for both F-pattern scanners and deep readers?

### 6. Price of attention

- Price of attention — what's the cognitive cost to reach the wiki from the landing page?

## Severity Calibration

- **P1**: Landing page has 8+ equal-weight links with no visual hierarchy
  - When: No primary CTA differentiation
- **P2**: Section labels are generic (Features, Links) instead of benefit-oriented
  - When: Labels don't communicate value proposition

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.

## Decision Lens

Choice architecture — does the layout guide visitors toward the highest-value action? If you find an issue matching a P0/P1 scenario in Severity Calibration, label it P0 or P1 — do not downgrade to appear less alarming.

## Prioritization

- P0: Issues that block other work, cause data loss or corruption — drop everything
- P1: Issues required to exit the current quality gate
- P2: Issues that degrade quality or create maintenance burden
- P3: Improvements and polish — suggest but don't block on these
- For each P0/P1 finding, describe the concrete failure scenario: what breaks, under what conditions, and who is affected
- Always tie findings to specific files, functions, and line numbers
- Frame uncertain findings as questions, not assertions
