## Task Context

Garden Salon's Meadowsyn visualization has three layers: L1 (WebGL ambient field), L2 (FIDS information ribbon), L3 (interactive detail). The review examines the visualization strategy through the lens of classical Chinese landscape composition.

## Review Approach

### 1. The three distances and the three layers
- In shanshui, gao-yuan (looking up at mountains) creates awe. Shen-yuan (looking deep into valleys) creates mystery. Ping-yuan (looking across water) creates tranquility. Does each Meadowsyn layer have a distinct emotional register, or are they just zoom levels?
- The three distances work because they offer different perspectives on the SAME landscape. Do L1/L2/L3 offer different perspectives on the same garden state, or different subsets of information?
- Classical composition places the viewer's entry point at the bottom. Where does the user enter the Meadowsyn view? Is there a deliberate eye-path?

### 2. Liu bai — emptiness as information
- In shanshui, what is NOT painted carries as much meaning as what is. Mist between mountains creates depth; empty water creates calm.
- Does Meadowsyn use negative space deliberately? Or does it fill every pixel with information?
- The attribution display rule (aggregate shown only after terminal state, not during active work) creates a deliberate absence. What fills that absence? Is the absence itself informative?
- When no agents are active, when no directions are proposed — what does an empty garden look like? Is the emptiness inviting or alarming?

### 3. The wandering path
- Shanshui paintings are designed to be "entered" — the eye follows paths through the composition, discovering details along the way. The viewer's journey IS the meaning.
- Ryan's journey through the salon view: does the visualization invite exploration, or present everything at once?
- Agents propose, humans annotate. Is this temporal flow visible as a path through the visualization — a narrative that the viewer can follow?

### 4. Inscription and colophon
- Shanshui paintings carry inscriptions (poems, thoughts) and colophons (later annotations by owners and viewers). These are part of the painting, not metadata.
- In Garden Salon, annotations (human feedback on agent work) are the colophon. Are they visually integrated into the work, or segregated as metadata?
- The garden's "purpose" statement is the inscription. Is it as prominent as the poem on a shanshui scroll?

## Severity Calibration

- **P0:** The visualization has no deliberate compositional strategy (just a dashboard)
- **P1:** No coherent eye-path for the non-developer persona through the information layers
- **P2:** Negative space and emptiness are not treated as design elements
- **P3:** Annotations are segregated from the work instead of integrated
