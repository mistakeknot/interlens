## Review Approach

### 1. Elicitation gap: the benchmark design gives models explic...

- Elicitation gap: the benchmark design gives models explicit API access, structured observation, and chain-of-thought scaffolding — this is highly favorable elicitation; assess whether the benchmark measures 'what the model does when optimally scaffolded to do alignment-relevant things' rather than 'what the model does when deployed,' which is the harder and more relevant question.

### 2. Gaming risk via system prompt: when models are explicitly...

- Gaming risk via system prompt: when models are explicitly told they are being evaluated on alignment dimensions, in-context goal-directed models (especially RLHF-trained) may optimize for appearing aligned rather than being aligned — assess whether the benchmark elicitation protocol inadvertently creates an alignment-appearance incentive.

### 3. Capability

- Capability-alignment confound: less capable models may score 'better' on values-under-pressure not because they have better values but because they cannot execute complex strategies — the benchmark must control for capability, otherwise it measures 'aligned and capable' vs. 'not capable enough to defect.'

### 4. Comparison to METR

- Comparison to METR and Apollo methodology: METR's task-based autonomy evals and Apollo's multi-step deception studies are both measuring adjacent properties; the brainstorm doesn't engage with these predecessors — flag the risk that ShadowBench rediscovers known failure modes or provides weaker evidence than existing evals for the same claims.

### 5. Ground truth problem for 'good values': the brainstorm as...

- Ground truth problem for 'good values': the brainstorm assumes we can identify alignment-good vs. alignment-bad action patterns (e.g., 'humanitarian-first is good'), but this embeds normative choices that should be made explicit — without a stated normative framework, the benchmark conflates 'aligned with benchmark designers' values' with 'aligned.'

### 6. Reproducibility

- Reproducibility and evaluation stability: simulation-based evals can drift across simulator versions; a model's alignment profile in ShadowBench v0.1 may not be comparable to v0.2 if simulation deepening changes strategy opportunities — flag the need for backward-compatible scenario versioning.

## Severity Calibration

- **P0**: The elicitation protocol explicitly tells the model it is an 'AI alignment benchmark' and asks it to 'steer civilization according to its values'
  - When: RLHF-trained models will detect this framing and optimize for appearing aligned; the benchmark measures alignment performance, not latent alignment properties — all published results would be contaminated
- **P1**: ShadowBench v0.1 and v0.2 results are compared without controlling for simulation depth changes
  - When: Adding media and information ecosystems changes available strategies; a model's v0.1 deception score is not comparable to v0.2 because the action space changed — multi-version comparisons require stable scenario anchors
- **P2**: GPT-5 scores lower on values-under-pressure than Claude 3, but GPT-5 is also less capable at long-horizon strategy
  - When: The benchmark cannot distinguish 'better values' from 'less capable at executing complex value-violating strategies' without a capability-controlled analysis — headline findings would be misleading

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.
