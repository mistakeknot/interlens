## Severity Calibration

- **P0**: The brainstorm's first bullet under Figures reads 'the Uncs are pixel figures who walk between rooms through the doors, idle, and speak moment lines', and decision 2 sells the whole surface as a cross-section — a house with its wall taken off so everything inside is seen. In the only comp mk ruled on, the walk between rooms is executed by setting el.style.visibility = 'hidden' for the 700-1300ms the figure spends in state 'hall', after which it reappears at the far edge of another room. The doors are decorative divs (door--l, door--r); nothing traverses them. So the player never once sees an Unc go anywhere: figures blink out of one box and blink into another, which is precisely the read a cross-section exists to prevent, and precisely the tell that separates a moving world from a slideshow of states. On the kelir this is the cardinal fault — a crossing is where the audience decides whether a figure is a body or a token, and there is no offstage inside the frame to hide it in. Either the corridor is drawn and the crossing is animated in view, or the design should stop calling this a house with the wall off and stop promising a walk. This must be ruled before any renderer work starts, because it decides whether rooms are boxes in a grid or compartments in one continuous section.
  - When: The design's headline motion is the one thing the surface hides.
- **P0**: Each figure picks an idle x independently — 0.12 + (fnv(`t:${uncId}:${now}`) % 76) / 100 — .fig is position:absolute with transform:translateX(-50%) and no spacing, ordering, or collision rule of any kind, and FIGURE_INKS is five colours that are all near-black at 18-27px. Two figures in one room therefore collide often (any pair within a few percent of the room's width), and when they do the design's entire identity system — decision 5's 'a met regular is drawn (hair, shirt, build, hat) with no face', which is to say identity carried wholly by silhouette — produces one merged blob with two names stacked under it. Wayang's answer is 500 years old and costs almost nothing: planting positions are conventional and mutually exclusive, so figures never overlap, and where they must share a scene the composition assigns each a station. A minimum separation, or a small set of named stations per room that figures occupy in arrival order, resolves this. Note that this defect is invisible to a lens counting pixels per figure: each figure is legible; it is the ARRANGEMENT that fails, and arrangement is currently unauthored.
  - When: Two figures reliably resolve into one unreadable shape at the size the design ships at.
- **P1**: Bubbles live in a Record<string, string> that any figure may write on any idle arrival with a 45% roll, held for 3800ms. Four regulars in one room can all be speaking simultaneously, in arbitrary order, over each other's heads, and each bubble is white-space:nowrap with max-width:220px above a figure occupying 18% of a room that is roughly 170px wide on a portrait phone — so the bubbles also overhang the room's edges and each other. The dalang cannot do this: one throat means one voice at a time, characters take turns, and the turn-taking is not a courtesy, it is the mechanism that lets an audience follow a screen with nine figures on it. The fix is a rule, not art: one bubble at a time per room (or per house), a queue in arrival order, and a bubble that is bounded by the room it is spoken in. Without it the calmest possible moment in a cozy game — four guys sitting in a room — renders as noise.
  - When: Concurrent speech with no order or cap, so a crowded room cannot be followed.
- **P1**: An empty room renders the string 'quiet' in its box. The house's own law is absence-as-content, and the two verdicts that killed the previous home surfaces were 'meaningless text' and 'still doesn't feel like a game'. Printing a mood-word into an empty box is the same move a third time, at smaller scale. Shadow theatre's gunungan is a single carved leaf-mountain figure that stands centre-screen for everything not being depicted — the interval between scenes, elapsed time, wind, a forest — and because it is the SAME figure every time, an audience reads its presence as meaning rather than as a caption. The design has 36 authored symbols becoming catalog stock and no equivalent object. Name one: a thing that is in the empty room, drawn, unbought, unbuyable, which is what the room looks like when nobody is in it. It costs one sprite and it converts the game's stated law from an assertion into something on the screen.
  - When: The interval is held by a word where the craft would put a figure, against a standing law.
- **P2**: A figure leaving to the right sets target 1.04 and should therefore enter the next room from its left; the comp then overrides this whenever the destination sits in column 0 or column cols-1, on the grounds that an edge room has only one door. Sprite facing (data-dir, a scaleX(-1)) is derived from the direction of travel alone. The result is that entry side and facing are functions of grid position, not of the figure or the journey, and they flip depending on how many rooms the player happens to have founded — cols is itself recomputed from rooms.length and viewport width. On the kelir, side and facing are read before anything else and never vary: a figure that entered from the right entered from the right all night. This is P2 because nothing breaks, but it is worth ruling now: either side means something (where a room sits in the house, where a caller comes from) or it should be made consistent so the eye stops trying to read it.
  - When: A reading convention contradicts itself from one room to the next.

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.
