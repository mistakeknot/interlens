## Task Context

Reviewing the encryption design brainstorm for Garden Salon. The current architecture uses a WebSocket relay for Yjs CRDT binary updates with no server-side persistence. Under E2E encryption (Options 3-5), the relay would forward encrypted binary blobs it cannot read. The design questions center on what operations remain possible when the relay is blind to content, and what information leaks through metadata, traffic patterns, and network topology.

## Review Approach

### 1. Gradient

- Gradient-based routing without content inspection: mycorrhizal networks route nutrients via concentration gradients at the root-hyphal interface — high concentration flows toward low concentration without the fungus parsing the nutrient's biological role. The WebSocket relay currently forwards Yjs binary updates without persistence. Can it also PRIORITIZE, LOAD-BALANCE, and RATE-LIMIT encrypted traffic using gradient signals (message frequency, byte volume, session participant count) without decrypting? What routing intelligence is possible when the relay is blind to content?

### 2. The mycorrhizal tax

- The mycorrhizal tax — the fungus takes a cut: mycorrhizal fungi retain 10-30% of transferred carbon as metabolic payment for transport services. This is not theft — it is the economic basis of the symbiosis. In the encryption design, the platform must extract SOME value from facilitating collaboration (for sustainability) without reading content. What is the 'mycorrhizal tax' — what metadata, usage patterns, or aggregate signals can the platform legitimately collect from encrypted traffic to sustain itself?

### 3. Network topology

- Network topology and single points of failure: mycorrhizal networks are meshes with hub trees ('mother trees') connected to many partners through dense hyphal networks. If a hub tree dies, the network reroutes through alternative paths — but there is transient disruption. In the E2E encryption design, what is the network topology? Is the relay server a single hub (single point of failure) or can encrypted gardens operate peer-to-peer when the server is unavailable? Does the key management topology match the content routing topology?

### 4. Species

- Species-specific transport and multi-tenant isolation: different tree species form mycorrhizal associations with different fungal partners — a pine's ectomycorrhizal network does not connect to an oak's arbuscular mycorrhizal network. The per-garden encryption (Options 3-5) creates analogous species-specific networks. But mycorrhizal networks occasionally bridge species through generalist fungi. What is the encrypted equivalent of a generalist bridge — a participant who belongs to multiple encrypted gardens and could, through their client, become a cross-garden information conduit?

### 5. The signaling paradox

- The signaling paradox — communicating need without revealing content: trees under herbivore attack release chemical signals through mycorrhizal networks that cause neighboring trees to preemptively upregulate defensive compounds. The attacked tree communicates URGENCY and TYPE OF THREAT without sending the actual herbivore. In the encryption design, can an encrypted garden communicate operational state (needs more compute, governance ceremony pending, participant needed) to the server or to other participants without revealing what the garden contains?

### 6. Symbiosis vs

- Symbiosis vs. parasitism — when the relay extracts too much: some fungi shift from mutualistic mycorrhiza to parasitic under stress — taking nutrients without providing transport benefit. The platform operator could similarly shift from providing relay services to extracting excessive metadata, traffic analysis, or behavioral data from encrypted traffic. What architectural safeguards prevent the relay from becoming parasitic — extracting more signal from encrypted traffic than the participants consented to?

## Severity Calibration

- **P0**: The relay server is a single point of failure with no peer-to-peer fallback — if the relay goes down, all real-time collaboration stops even though the content is client-side encrypted. The network topology creates a centralized dependency that undermines the decentralized trust model of E2E encryption.
  - When: E2E encryption creates a trust model that implies decentralization, but the relay architecture is fully centralized — the cryptographic guarantee and the operational architecture are mismatched
- **P1**: Traffic analysis on encrypted relay data reveals content characteristics — a burst of small rapid messages indicates real-time editing, long pauses indicate review, large payloads indicate file uploads. The relay cannot read content but can infer activity patterns that constitute a metadata side-channel.
  - When: The encryption design focuses on content encryption without addressing traffic-analysis resistance, leaving a gradient-signal-based side channel
- **P2**: A participant belonging to multiple encrypted gardens becomes an uncontrolled cross-network bridge — their client holds keys to multiple gardens and could (through malware, coercion, or intentional action) transfer content between gardens that the encryption was designed to isolate
  - When: Per-garden encryption creates isolated networks but multi-garden participants bridge them at the client layer, and no architectural mechanism addresses this cross-contamination vector

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.

## Decision Lens

Every routing and relay decision is evaluated by the mycorrhizal principle that the transport network operates on GRADIENT SIGNALS (source/sink, concentration differential, demand pressure) rather than on content inspection. The fungus routes phosphorus from surplus trees to deficit trees without 'understanding' phosphorus the way a tree does — it responds to concentration gradients at hyphal interfaces. The fundamental question: can the encrypted relay route, prioritize, and load-balance content using gradient-like signals (message size, frequency, session activity, participant count) without ever inspecting content? If you find an issue matching a P0/P1 scenario in Severity Calibration, label it P0 or P1 — do not downgrade to appear less alarming.
