## Review Approach

### 1. Model warm-up and order effects: sensory panels randomize sample presentation

- Model warm-up and order effects: sensory panels randomize sample presentation order per session — the pipeline must verify that dilemma presentation order is randomized per model-run to prevent carry-over from morally priming or fatiguing the model's lens selection toward salient options presented earlier in the batch

### 2. Reference standard anchoring: trained panels use reference standards to anchor

- Reference standard anchoring: trained panels use reference standards to anchor attribute scales — the pipeline's position-bias detection must check whether the first lens in the presented list acts as an implicit anchor, inflating its selection probability independent of dilemma content

### 3. Panelist drift detection: panelists drift over long sessions; check whether

- Panelist drift detection: panelists drift over long sessions; check whether models show systematic lens-selection drift across the ordinal position of dilemmas within a batch (dilemma 1000–1360 vs 1–360), which would indicate context-window saturation or instruction-following degradation

### 4. Sample insertion: sensory protocols insert blind duplicates to measure

- Check sample insertion: sensory protocols insert blind duplicates to measure within-panelist reproducibility — the pipeline should insert a random 2–3% duplicate dilemma set (same dilemma, different position in the batch) to compute within-model reproducibility before cross-model agreement is meaningful

### 5. Attribute reduction vs lens reduction: panels often discover that trained

- Attribute reduction vs lens reduction: panels often discover that trained attributes collapse into fewer perceptual dimensions — the pipeline should apply a parallel dimensionality check (PCA or hierarchical clustering) on the 291-lens space to detect which lenses are perceptually redundant from the models' collective perspective

### 6. Fatigue and carry-over in async batching: when batches resume after interruption

- Fatigue and carry-over in async batching: when batches resume after interruption, the context window resets — this is the sensory equivalent of a panelist returning from a break, which typically reduces carry-over but may introduce inconsistency at batch boundaries; verify resume logic doesn't re-expose the model to a summary of prior selections that would re-anchor it

## Severity Calibration

- **critical**: No within-model reproducibility check before computing cross-model agreement
  - When: If a model selects Lens A for Dilemma 47 when it appears at position 12 in a batch, but selects Lens B when the same dilemma appears at position 1,200, the pipeline has no way to detect this — cross-model agreement is computed on position-confounded data, making the agreement metric meaningless as a measure of genuine model alignment
- **high**: Lens list presented in fixed alphabetical or frequency order across all dilemmas
  - When: Alphabetical presentation consistently places 'Autonomy' before 'Beneficence' — models with recency-blind attention learn to anchor on early options, creating a systematic alphabetical bias in selection frequency that inflates certain lenses' apparent salience and corrupts the anchor suite
- **medium**: Resume logic re-exposes model to a summary of previous batch completions
  - When: If the resume mechanism injects a 'you previously analyzed 800 dilemmas and frequently selected utilitarian lenses' prompt to orient the model, subsequent lens selections are anchored to that summary — a carry-over artifact that makes later-batch selections artificially consistent with earlier ones, suppressing genuine late-batch drift detection

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.

## Decision Lens

A calibrated panel requires three preconditions before its output is actionable: panelist-by-attribute interaction must not be significant in the ANOVA (panelists agree on direction even if scale differs), the panel's r-index for discrimination must exceed 0.75 on check samples, and no individual panelist's mean deviates from the panel mean by more than 1.5 standard deviations on any trained attribute. If any precondition fails, the panel is in retraining, not production. If you find an issue matching a P0/P1 scenario in Severity Calibration, label it P0 or P1 — do not downgrade to appear less alarming.
