## Review Approach

### 1. Frontier stability under methodology perturbation: a Pareto frontier computed

- Frontier stability under methodology perturbation: a Pareto frontier computed with 5 dimensions will shift membership if any dimension's scoring formula changes by even a small factor — the brainstorm's 'visible separation on at least 2 of 5 dimensions' kill gate does not specify how visible separation is defined, and a frontier that passes the kill gate with one scoring parameterization but fails with a defensible alternative is not a stable finding; the methodology must include a sensitivity analysis demonstrating that the frontier topology is robust to reasonable formula perturbations before publication

### 2. Dimension weighting and the unweighted Pareto assumption: computing a Pareto

- Dimension weighting and the unweighted Pareto assumption: computing a Pareto frontier across 5 dimensions implicitly assumes all 5 dimensions are equally valid and equally measurable — index methodology requires explicit justification for dimension inclusion (why these 5, not 6 or 3?) and disclosure of whether the frontier results change if dimensions are weighted by measurement reliability; if restraint is the hardest dimension to measure and coherence is the easiest, an unweighted frontier may be dominated by the easy-to-score dimensions

### 3. Constituent selection and coverage disclosure: the brainstorm selects Opus 4.7

- Constituent selection and coverage disclosure: the brainstorm selects Opus 4.7 + GPT 5.4 as the pilot pair and 3–5 models for the full run — index methodology requires explicit pre-commitment to the inclusion rules before any results are computed, and disclosure of which models were evaluated and excluded; a frontier computed over 5 models where 3 were dropped due to 'API access issues' that happened to be the models most likely to cluster near the frontier boundary is a survivorship-bias problem

### 4. N=5 sampling and frontier boundary confidence: the brainstorm specifies N=5

- N=5 sampling and frontier boundary confidence: the brainstorm specifies N=5 runs per model — a frontier boundary drawn from 5-run means without confidence intervals around each model's position does not communicate how stable the frontier assignment is; models near the Pareto boundary with overlapping confidence regions cannot be reliably assigned 'on the frontier' vs. 'off the frontier', and published frontier diagrams without uncertainty bands are standard in casual publications but would not survive methodology review at an index provider

### 5. Frontier visualization and the radar chart legibility trap: the brainstorm

- Frontier visualization and the radar chart legibility trap: the brainstorm specifies '2D pairwise + radar for 5D' — radar charts are the most common way to visualize multi-dimensional model comparison, but they have well-documented perceptual distortions (area depends on axis order, which creates false impression of overall superiority for models that score high on adjacent axes); if the radar chart is the primary publication visual, the frontier claim may be carried by a misleading visual rather than the underlying Pareto analysis

### 6. Baseline inclusion in the frontier and the reference point problem: the

- Baseline inclusion in the frontier and the reference point problem: the brainstorm includes 6 scripted baselines (null, random, stability, rights, power, plus one more) — the Pareto frontier paper must clarify whether baselines are included in the frontier visualization; including a 'random' agent that Pareto-dominates some frontier models in one dimension would be the most credibility-damaging outcome, while excluding baselines entirely from the frontier diagram means the frontier has no reference anchor for interpreting what 'good' means

## Decision Lens

Findings are ranked by frontier instability risk: a Pareto frontier that changes its membership or topology when any reasonable alternative methodology is applied is a construction artifact, not a finding about model alignment. A published frontier that dissolves under peer replication is worse than not publishing at all — it poisons the well for future credible evals. Secondary priority is constituent completeness: a frontier computed over 2–5 selectively chosen models with no disclosure of why those models and not others invites the most damaging criticism ('you cherry-picked the models that made your frontier look good').
