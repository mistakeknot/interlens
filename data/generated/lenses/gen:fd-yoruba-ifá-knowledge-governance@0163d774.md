## Task Context

Garden Salon's governance is evolutionary (emergent → templates → constitutions) with append-only records, per-garden interpretation, and no platform-wide governance model. The review examines this through Ifá's knowledge governance: how an oral tradition maintains coherence across thousands of practitioners without centralized authority.

## Review Approach

### 1. The odù and the ledger
- In Ifá, the 256 odù are the corpus. Each odù accumulates ese (verse commentaries) across centuries. The corpus is append-only — no verse is removed, but new interpretations layer on. The babalawo's skill is navigating this accumulation.
- Garden Salon's attribution records are append-only with governance_snapshot_ref. But unlike the odù, there is no mechanism for navigating the accumulation. How does a participant in year 3 of a garden understand attribution records from year 1? Is there an equivalent of the babalawo's training that teaches interpretation?

### 2. Individual reading, collective deliberation
- When a babalawo consults Ifá, they first read the odù privately (individual interpretation), then present to the council for deliberation (collective validation). The reading is individual; the validation is collective.
- In Garden Salon, agents propose (individual reading) and humans annotate (collective deliberation). But the brainstorm doesn't describe collective deliberation among humans. Multiple humans can annotate, but is there a structured protocol for resolving disagreements BETWEEN humans about agent work?
- The Ifá council uses seniority and reputation to weight voices. Garden Salon uses attribution for influence (in cooperative gardens). But attribution measures quantity of compute, not quality of judgment. The babalawo who has the most verses memorized is not necessarily the wisest reader.

### 3. Transmission and the evolutionary path
- Ifá knowledge governance evolved over centuries: oral transmission → structured training → regional variations → cross-regional standardization. This is exactly Garden Salon's evolutionary path (emergent → templates → constitutions).
- A critical Ifá insight: regional variations are not bugs — they are the system's resilience mechanism. Different babalawo lineages interpret the same odù differently, and this diversity prevents brittle consensus.
- Does Garden Salon's per-garden governance serve the same function? Is divergence between gardens a feature (resilience) or a liability (fragmentation)?

### 4. The role of the awo (mystery)
- Ifá has a concept of awo — knowledge that is intentionally not public. Not secret out of malice, but reserved because premature access is harmful. A babalawo learns certain verses only after years of preparation.
- In Garden Salon, everything is transparent by default. Is there room for awo — knowledge that is deliberately held back until the garden is ready? Agent reasoning, for example — should Ryan see ALL of it, or only what he's prepared to interpret?

## Severity Calibration

- **P0:** No mechanism for navigating accumulated attribution over time (the ledger becomes unreadable)
- **P1:** No structured protocol for collective human deliberation, only individual annotation
- **P2:** Attribution measures compute quantity, not judgment quality — wrong signal for governance weight
- **P3:** Full transparency may be harmful when participants lack interpretive training
