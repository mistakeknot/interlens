## Severity Calibration

- **P0**: Regions have no identifier independent of name, position, and contents
  - When: Read-state marks, digests, lineage edges, governance proposals, summary cards, and links all reference regions. If a region is keyed by its name, its bounding box, or a hash of its message set, then a rename, a merge, a split, or a summarize invalidates every reference at once and the governance ledger cannot describe what it approved. This choice lands simultaneously in the Yjs document shape, the Postgres schema, and the agent proposal format, so it is made — deliberately or accidentally — in the first build phase and is expensive in all three places afterward.
- **P1**: Silence-is-consent has no notice rule and no cooling period matched to real absence
  - When: Acquiescence doctrines are enforceable only because a party is presumed to have had the opportunity to object. The cohort is explicitly async-heavy — members are away for days. A cooling period shorter than realistic absence converts absence into approval, and the design gives agents the power to propose merges, splits, and summaries of existing regions with low-stakes ones resolving by silence, while defining neither 'low-stakes', nor how a member is notified, nor whether a returning member can contest a resolved proposal. The first time someone returns to find their topic merged into another, the spatial-memory contract is broken by the governance mechanism meant to protect it.
- **P1**: Region growth relocates places without any approved move
  - When: Regions accrete messages and are described as having a center of mass and a temperature. If that center is computed rather than monumented, every new message in a busy region shifts its anchor; new regions budding off push neighbors; the summary card is inserted at the center as content arrives. A member returning after a week finds the geography rearranged without a single governed move having occurred. Propose-not-move governs discrete moves and leaves continuous drift entirely ungoverned, which is where the whole spatial-memory argument actually lives.

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.
