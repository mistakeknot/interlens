## Task Context

Reviewing the UX philosophy of Garden Salon. Governance lives in a Postgres layer with ceremony tables, quorum requirements, and succession mechanisms. The two-phase merge bridges CRDT collaborative editing with governance authorization. The core design tension is making governance visible enough to be trustworthy without making it oppressive enough to feel bureaucratic.

## Review Approach

### 1. Surface preparation vs

- Surface preparation vs. mark-making: does the governance UI feel like setting conditions (preparing the canvas, choosing the flame distance) or like drawing lines (explicit rule-display, permission matrices)? The fumage artist never draws — they create conditions for emergence. Does the garden owner 'draw' governance rules or 'prepare surfaces' for governance to emerge?

### 2. Smoke

- Smoke-trace legibility: in fumage, the traces are beautiful but ambiguous — they invite interpretation without demanding it. Does governance state appear as a natural residue of process (activity traces, ceremony outcomes, attribution patterns) or as explicit bureaucratic annotation (status badges, permission labels, approval stamps)?

### 3. Flame distance as control granularity: in fumage

- Flame distance as control granularity: in fumage, moving the candle closer produces dark, dense, controllable marks; further away produces ethereal, unpredictable wisps. Does the governance system offer this granularity — tight control for high-stakes decisions, loose ambient guidance for routine work?

### 4. The stopping problem: the hardest decision in fumage is w...

- The stopping problem: the hardest decision in fumage is when to stop — one more pass of the flame could enhance or destroy. Does the ceremony system help participants sense when governance intervention is sufficient, or does it encourage over-governance (too many ceremonies, too many votes)?

### 5. Emergent pattern recognition: fumage images are read afte...

- Emergent pattern recognition: fumage images are read after creation — the artist discovers what the smoke 'drew.' Does the governance layer support retrospective pattern recognition — seeing governance patterns that emerged without being designed?

### 6. The conservation paradox: fumage works are fragile becaus...

- The conservation paradox: fumage works are fragile because soot is loosely bonded. The conservator must decide what to fix (stabilize the soot) without destroying the quality that makes it fumage (the ephemeral, smoke-like appearance). When governance traces are persisted in the Postgres layer, does fixing them in place destroy their ambient quality?

## Severity Calibration

- **P1**: Governance is presented as an explicit overlay — permission badges, approval stamps, status indicators painted on top of work — rather than emerging as natural traces of collaborative process
  - When: Governance visibility is implemented as bureaucratic annotation rather than process residue
- **P2**: The ceremony system applies the same governance intensity to all decisions — no flame-distance control between high-stakes structural changes and routine editorial tweaks
  - When: No granularity in governance weight — all changes go through the same ceremony regardless of stakes
- **P2**: Governance traces are so thoroughly persisted and displayed that they become the dominant visual element, overwhelming the actual work — the soot is fixed so heavily it stops looking like smoke
  - When: The Postgres governance layer surfaces historical governance data so prominently that it creates bureaucratic weight

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.

## Decision Lens

Every governance-visibility decision is evaluated by the fumage principle: the artist's role is to prepare the surface, position the flame, and choose when to stop — NOT to draw the image. The image emerges from the interaction of prepared conditions and physical processes. Governance should be visible the way smoke traces are visible — as the natural residue of process, not as an imposed overlay. If you can see the governance, it should be because it left traces through its operation, not because someone painted a diagram of it on top. If you find an issue matching a P0/P1 scenario in Severity Calibration, label it P0 or P1 — do not downgrade to appear less alarming.

## Prioritization

- P0: Issues that block other work, cause data loss or corruption — drop everything
- P1: Issues required to exit the current quality gate
- P2: Issues that degrade quality or create maintenance burden
- P3: Improvements and polish — suggest but don't block on these
- For each P0/P1 finding, describe the concrete failure scenario: what breaks, under what conditions, and who is affected
- Always tie findings to specific files, functions, and line numbers
