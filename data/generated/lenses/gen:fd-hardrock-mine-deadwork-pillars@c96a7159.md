## Severity Calibration

- **P0**: The cost curve measures the stope and not the shaft
  - When: The condition requires the probe to report its own cost curve over systems x civs x members x centuries against an affordability envelope, so the next engine bottleneck is measured rather than guessed. Every axis in that curve is a production axis — it prices what the run raises. None of it prices the dead work: the galaxy scaffold that yields no history, the species stubs that exist only so civ genesis has something to consume, the regeneration machinery implied by the GATE's own contract question, the crosscutting through barren substrate needed to give Reynolds ruins coordinates. A mine captain reading a report of shillings per ton with no fathoms of development in it knows exactly what he is looking at: a number that will look excellent right up until the prepared ground runs out. The measured bottleneck this goal ships will therefore be a bottleneck in stoping, and the real constraint on the program — what it costs to drive a new level down to a lower stratum once history has been generated above it — will be exactly the quantity the goal was designed to measure and did not.
- **P0**: Robbing the pillars is scheduled as the next slice
  - When: OUTCOME states that lower strata are scaffold-thin but their interfaces are designed for later deepening, and the GATE asks how thin the physical substrate can be. Together these make the interfaces load-bearing: they are the structure holding up everything the program intends to build afterwards, and the goal's entire depth-bet rests on their being right. But not one DONE WHEN bullet examines an interface. Every bullet inspects something raised — a scaffold that generates, a probe that runs, a chronicle that reads, a cost curve that reports. The pillars are the one part of the deposit that must not be extracted and the one part nobody inspects, and the condition proposes to spend all its depth budget in the stopes beside them. When a later slice deepens the Dixon stratum and finds the interface admits the wrong parameters, the correct move will be the cheapest one available and also the last one: cut into the pillar and regenerate everything above it.
- **P1**: Below adit level with no pump, by explicit exclusion
  - When: Above the adit, water leaves a mine by itself; below it, the mine pumps for as long as it exists, and the pump is not a cost of going deeper but a cost of having gone deeper. This condition proposes to run 120-plus simulated centuries over hundreds of systems in deferred-cascade settle mode, and simultaneously puts FINDINGS #2 snapshot loads OUT with the instruction to measure where they become blocking and not build them. If snapshots turn out to be the drainage — the thing that lets a long run be resumed, inspected, or re-entered without regenerating from the seed — then the goal has committed to depth and forbidden the only mechanism that makes depth survivable, and 'measure where they become blocking' is a plan to discover this at the moment the workings fill. The condition needs to say what the probe does when it hits that water: abandon the run and report, restart shallower, or spend turns building the pump it was told not to build.
- **P1**: One exposed face reported as a body of ore
  - When: A generator is a body; a run is a face. The acceptance criteria are written for a run: a seeded galaxy scaffold generating deterministically from the world seed, generated history including at least one fallen civilization whose ruins are queryable at a real location. All of this can be true of one seed and false of the next, and the condition contains nothing that distinguishes the two — no second seed, no statement of what fraction of worlds must satisfy the 'at least' list, no account of the barren draw. Worse, the seed is chosen by whoever runs the probe, which is how prospects get salted without anyone intending fraud: you run until you get a world with a good schism in it and you demonstrate that one. The goal will then ratify a depth bet on ore in sight from a single exposure, and the first thing the next slice discovers is the grade of the body rather than the grade of the sample.
- **P1**: All bargains set on tribute, none on tutwork
  - When: A Cornish mine paid two ways because output-priced work and enabling work respond to money differently: tribute in shillings in the pound rewards the man who raises ore, and tutwork by the fathom pays for driving that raises none. Set every bargain on tribute and the levels go undriven while the tributers pick over the accessible ground. This condition's DONE WHEN list is almost entirely tribute: a scaffold that generates, a probe that runs end to end, a chronicle whose events cite real places, a fallen civilisation queryable in the present, a cost curve. The enabling items — the brainstorm that rules the GATE forks, the interfaces, the beads for stubbed strata, the FINDINGS write-back — appear as bullets of equal weight with no acknowledgement that they yield nothing visible and will therefore be the first things compressed when twenty turns start to bite. Nothing in the condition says which bullets may be short and which may not, so the compression will happen silently and in the direction it always does.
- **P1**: No development standing ahead of the stopes
  - When: The captain's real number is not this month's tonnage but how much prepared ground stands ahead of production — a mine raising well with nothing developed ahead of it is already dying. This goal is slice one of a declared program, and its DONE WHEN list describes a full-scale run at the end of twenty turns with no statement of what is left prepared for slice two. The chronicle is raised, the cost curve is reported, the beads are filed — and then what stands ready? The condition asks for a measured bottleneck, which is a statement about where the next drive should go, but it never asks the run to leave that drive begun, or even to say what shape it takes. Stopping at maximum production with nothing developed ahead is exactly the position from which programs get abandoned: the next slice must start with barren work, which is the work nobody wants to fund.
- **P2**: Disposability priced as free rather than as a cost of return
  - When: The GATE asks whether worlds are disposable until played, and treats the answer as a property of the world. It is really a property of the return trip. A mine you leave standing is cheap to leave only if reopening costs about what walking away saved; the Cornish rule is that it does not — a knacked mine takes the water in weeks and reopening costs more than sinking did. The condition never states what regenerating a world costs relative to generating it, and the whole disposability argument rests on an unstated assumption that the two are the same. If regeneration is dearer — because a played world has accumulated state, or because deepening a lower stratum invalidates strata above it in a widening cone — then 'disposable until played' is not a simplification but a deferral, and the bill arrives at exactly the moment the program starts caring about worlds.
- **P2**: Crosscutting through country rock, billed to neither account
  - When: Some substrate work exists only to reach the stratum above it: planetary parameters that no one wants for their own sake but Dixon evolution consumes, coordinates that matter only because Reynolds ruins must sit somewhere real. That is crosscutting — barren driving toward ore. The condition sorts work by stratum and budgets depth by stratum, which gives this work nowhere to sit. Counted as substrate depth, it violates 'lower strata are scaffold-thin' and the GATE's minimising question about how thin the substrate can be. Counted as civ-stratum work, it silently consumes the one budget the goal said it was protecting. The likely outcome is that it is not counted at all, done informally under whichever bullet is open at the time, and reported as neither — and the next slice inherits a substrate whose actual thickness nobody recorded.
- **P2**: Nowhere to stow the deads
  - When: Hoisting waste is expensive, so worked-out stopes are packed with the rock that came out of them. A run over hundreds of systems and 120-plus centuries will produce a great deal that is neither chronicle nor evidence: systems with nothing on them, civilisations that never rose, long stretches of century with no era-scale event in them, event-log entries generated by the deferred cascade to reach the ones that matter. The condition speaks only of what it raises — a readable chronicle, queryable ruins, a cost curve — and says nothing about the proportion of the run that is barren, where it goes, or whether it is kept. This matters twice: the waste fraction is a real term in the cost curve the goal claims to measure, and 'a chronicle a human can read' is a claim about the ore-to-waste ratio of the output that no acceptance criterion states.

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.
