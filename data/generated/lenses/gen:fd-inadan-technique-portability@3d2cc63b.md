## Review Approach

### 1. Technique triage discipline

- Technique triage discipline — the four categories: the Inadan smith facing a new commission sorts their workshop into four piles: (1) tools that transfer directly (the forge heats any metal), (2) tools that transfer with adaptation (chasing punches work on silver but need different angles than on iron), (3) tools that are irrelevant (sword-tempering quench tanks for jewelry), (4) gaps requiring new acquisition (filigree technique for a commission the smith has never done). Does the renderer decision perform this four-category triage on the Bevy+MetalFX stack? Category 1: what transfers directly (windowing, event loop, input handling). Category 2: what transfers with adaptation (ECS for entity management, but adapted for 2D). Category 3: what is irrelevant (3D scene graph, PBR materials, MetalFX upscaling). Category 4: what's missing and must be built new (ASCII grid renderer, attribute-based color system, tile-based procedural art pipeline). A decision that only considers 'reuse' vs 'build fresh' is a smith who can only see 'use my workshop' vs 'build a new workshop' — missing the triage granularity.

### 2. Prestige bias

- Prestige bias and the sunk-cost of inherited technique: an Inadan smith faces social pressure to demonstrate mastery of inherited techniques — using a grandfather's tools and methods carries prestige. But forcing inherited technique onto an unsuitable commission produces bad work. The analogous pressure with Bevy: there is institutional investment (code already written, knowledge already acquired, integration already proven) that creates bias toward reuse regardless of fit. Does the decision framework acknowledge this bias explicitly? The sibling project's Bevy expertise is an inherited technique — valuable, real, but not automatically applicable. The decision should evaluate fit independently of investment.

### 3. The adapter commission

- The adapter commission — when the client needs both old and new: some Inadan commissions explicitly request that old techniques be visible in a new context — a modern pendant that references ancestral sword-hilt motifs. This is the renderer equivalent of 'preserve ASCII aesthetic while gaining rich tiles.' The smith's approach: execute the new form (pendant) with new techniques (fine silver filigree) while embedding quotations of the old technique (chased iron motifs) as deliberate aesthetic references rather than structural load-bearing elements. Does the renderer plan treat ASCII motifs as deliberate aesthetic quotations within a new rendering substrate, or does it try to make the new renderer structurally dependent on ASCII conventions that belong to the old substrate?

### 4. Workshop portability

- Workshop portability and WASM as a new market: Tuareg Inadan are historically mobile — they carry their workshops across the Sahara, setting up in different encampments and markets. The workshop must be portable: tools that are too heavy or too specialized for one context get left behind. WASM deployment is analogous — the renderer must be portable to a constrained environment (browser sandbox, no native GPU access without WebGPU). Does the technique triage account for portability constraints? A Bevy+MetalFX stack that works on native macOS may be too heavy for WASM, like a forge bellows designed for a permanent workshop that can't be carried on a camel. The lightweight-tools option maps to the traveling smith's kit: minimal, portable, sufficient.

### 5. Teneghelt (personal style)

- Teneghelt (personal style) and project identity: the Inadan's teneghelt — distinctive personal style — emerges from their specific combination of inherited and invented techniques. It is not threatened by learning filigree but IS threatened by forcing chasing technique where filigree is needed, because forced technique produces generic-bad work rather than distinctive-good work. The project's identity (ASCII-aesthetic colony sim) emerges from its specific combination of inherited code (agent-fortress patterns) and novel intent (art/social simulation). Does the renderer decision preserve this identity by honestly matching technique to purpose, or does it threaten identity by forcing inherited rendering technique where purpose demands something different?

## Severity Calibration

- **P1**: The renderer decision is framed as binary (reuse Bevy vs. build from scratch) without performing the four-category technique triage, missing the option of selective reuse where transferable subsystems are kept and non-transferable ones are replaced — like a smith who can only 'use the whole inherited workshop' or 'build a new workshop from nothing'
  - When: The decision framework presents 2-3 monolithic options without decomposing the existing stack into independently evaluable subsystems
- **P1**: WASM portability constraints are evaluated after the renderer architecture is chosen rather than as a primary triage filter, like a traveling smith who selects workshop tools for capability and only discovers their weight when packing the camel
  - When: Bevy+MetalFX reuse is evaluated for rendering capability without early validation that the chosen approach compiles to WASM with acceptable binary size, startup time, and runtime performance in a browser sandbox
- **P2**: Sunk-cost bias toward the sibling project's Bevy investment drives the decision without honest acknowledgment that expertise in 3D rendering does not automatically transfer to 2D ASCII-aesthetic rendering — the prestige of inherited technique overriding commission-fit analysis
  - When: The decision rationale cites existing Bevy code, knowledge, and integration work as primary advantages without independently evaluating whether those specific capabilities serve the new project's actual rendering needs

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.
