- **P0**: The only bystander-facing signal design describes is the operator's persistent REC banner on the phone's own screen — for a chest-mounted device the screen necessarily faces away from bystanders (toward the wearer or outward-but-unreadable), meaning there is effectively zero bystander notice, which is both an ethical gap in a project whose own CLAUDE.md treats bystander privacy as non-negotiable, and a materially higher legal/App-Store-review risk than the design's framing acknowledges.
  - When: no external/bystander-facing indicator (e.g., visible external light, worn badge, signage) is discussed as at least an open question, and the design treats the on-phone indicator as sufficient 'recording indicator' without qualification
- **P1**: Two-party/all-party consent jurisdictions (several US states, many countries) require notice or consent from all recorded parties for audio recording specifically (not video) in public or semi-public spaces; the design's consent section addresses only the wearer's own consent flow and doesn't flag continuous ambient audio recording of bystanders as a distinct legal-risk surface requiring its own risk acknowledgment or jurisdiction-scoping decision.
  - When: the brainstorm's consent/privacy section doesn't separately address bystander audio recording legality as an open question or documented accepted risk
- **P1**: Info.plist purpose strings and App Store review posture for a camera+mic+location+motion app whose actual behavior is continuous ambient recording of third parties in public are not mentioned anywhere in the plan; if usage-description strings are written generically ('used to record your session') without addressing what a reviewer will see on install/first-launch, the app risks App Store rejection or removal after the fact, which is a ship-blocking risk for a device-side MVP whose acceptance test assumes the app can simply be installed and run.
  - When: no mention of Info.plist purpose-string content or anticipated App Store review characterization anywhere in the design or open questions
- **P2**: The one-tap pause control's reachability during actual chest-mounted wear (phone screen against the wearer's body, not glanceable mid-run) is not addressed — if pause requires unlocking and precisely tapping a small on-screen control while running, the practical ability to stop recording on bystander request (a real scenario for a device recording strangers) is much worse than the UX design implies from a hand-held testing context.
  - When: pause/stop UX is described only in screen-layout terms without addressing physical reachability during the actual chest-mount use case
