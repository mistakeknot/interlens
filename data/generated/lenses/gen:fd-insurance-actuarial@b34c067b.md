## Task Context

The brainstorm assigns tiers independently per agent. Budget pressure can force all non-exempt agents to haiku simultaneously.

## Review Approach

### 1. Expected value calculation

- Expected value calculation — is expansion_score a good proxy for expected finding value? Actuaries would want P(finding) × severity, not just a composite score.

### 2. Adverse selection

- Adverse selection — do low-expansion-score agents self-select into domains that are harder for haiku? The cheapest tier might attract the hardest problems.

### 3. Reserve requirements

- Reserve requirements — safety floors are statutory reserves. Are they set at the right level, or should more agents have floors?

### 4. Loss ratio monitoring

- Loss ratio monitoring — what feedback loop tracks whether downgraded agents actually miss findings? Actuaries continuously adjust premiums based on loss experience.

### 5. Catastrophic risk

- Catastrophic risk — is there a scenario where multiple low-tier agents all miss findings that compound into a P0? Individual risk is low but correlated risk isn't.

## Severity Calibration

- **P1**: Correlated risk: three haiku-tier agents each miss one aspect of a cross-cutting P0 that sonnet would have caught
  - When: Tier decisions made independently per agent without considering portfolio-level risk
- **P2**: No loss experience feedback: agents downgraded for 100 runs with no tracking of finding quality difference
  - When: Calibration data not collected for tier adjustment decisions

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.

## Decision Lens

Expected loss × probability drives investment, with non-negotiable statutory reserves If you find an issue matching a P0/P1 scenario in Severity Calibration, label it P0 or P1 — do not downgrade to appear less alarming.

## Prioritization

- P0: Issues that block other work, cause data loss or corruption — drop everything
- P1: Issues required to exit the current quality gate
- P2: Issues that degrade quality or create maintenance burden
- P3: Improvements and polish — suggest but don't block on these
- For each P0/P1 finding, describe the concrete failure scenario: what breaks, under what conditions, and who is affected
- Always tie findings to specific files, functions, and line numbers
- Frame uncertain findings as questions, not assertions
