## Task Context

Reviewing a first-principles civilizational taxonomy (8 domains, 40 sub-systems, 221 countries) for a simulation. The taxonomy must capture the temporal grammar of how civilizational systems unfold and the structural identity that makes each system recognizably itself.

## Review Approach

### 1. Temporal phase grammar: Raga performance unfolds through ...

- Temporal phase grammar: Raga performance unfolds through alap (slow, unmetered melodic exposition), jor (addition of pulse), jhala (intensification), and gat (composed section with full rhythmic cycle). Does the taxonomy specify a temporal unfolding grammar for how domains come into existence and complexity — which domains must be established first (alap), which add rhythmic structure (jor), which intensify existing dynamics (jhala), and which require the full system to function (gat)?

### 2. Mandatory exposition before elaboration: In dhrupad

- Mandatory exposition before elaboration: In dhrupad, the alap must establish the raga's sa-re (tonic-second) relationship before introducing any other notes — skip this and the raga's identity is never established. Does the taxonomy specify which sub-systems must be established before others can meaningfully exist? State capacity (Domain 3) and physical infrastructure (Domain 1) may need 'alap exposition' before financial markets (Domain 2) or information ecosystems (Domain 4) can function.

### 3. Note

- Note-ordering constraints (aroh/avaroh): Each raga specifies permitted ascending (aroh) and descending (avaroh) note sequences — not just which notes are allowed, but which sequences. Does the taxonomy specify permitted development sequences — which sub-system expansions can follow which others? Can a country develop a derivatives market (ascending complexity) without first establishing property rights (prerequisite note)?

### 4. Pakad (characteristic phrase) as system identity: A raga'...

- Pakad (characteristic phrase) as system identity: A raga's pakad is the irreducible melodic phrase that identifies it — two ragas using identical notes but different pakads are different ragas. Does the taxonomy capture the pakad of each country or country-type — the characteristic pattern of domain interaction that makes this system recognizably itself? Or does it reduce all countries to parameter sets on a common template?

### 5. Vadi/samvadi (dominant/co

- Vadi/samvadi (dominant/co-dominant): Each raga has a vadi (most important note) and samvadi (second most important, typically a fifth apart). Does the taxonomy identify the vadi and samvadi of each country's system — the dominant and co-dominant domains that define the country's character? A petrostate's vadi is Material Economy (energy extraction); a knowledge economy's vadi is Technology & Innovation. Is this asymmetry captured?

### 6. Prahar (time association): Ragas are associated with spec...

- Prahar (time association): Ragas are associated with specific times of day (prahar) — performing them at the wrong time violates the grammar. Does the taxonomy capture temporal associations — which domain dynamics are appropriate at which development phase of a country? Imposing full financial liberalization (evening raga) on a country in early institutional development (morning raga) violates the temporal grammar.

## Severity Calibration

- **P0**: The taxonomy treats all 8 domains as simultaneously activatable for any country, with no temporal unfolding grammar — a simulation that instantiates derivatives markets, free press, and independent judiciary for a country that lacks basic state capacity is performing the gat without the alap. The domains exist but produce meaningless dynamics.
  - When: No temporal phase grammar constrains which domains can be active based on development prerequisites
- **P1**: All 221 countries are modeled as parameter variations on a single domain template — there is no pakad (characteristic identity phrase) that makes Nigeria's system recognizably different from Denmark's in structural kind, not just parameter degree
  - When: Country differentiation is purely parametric (same structure, different numbers) rather than grammatical (different structural rules)
- **P2**: The taxonomy does not identify dominant and co-dominant domains (vadi/samvadi) for different country types — a petrostate where Material Economy dominates all other domains behaves identically in the simulation to a knowledge economy where Technology dominates, because the domain interaction grammar does not encode which domain is dominant
  - When: No mechanism for one domain to be structurally dominant in shaping a country's overall system behavior
