## Severity Calibration

- **P0**: Gate variant: N is chosen without a sample-size calculation. A subsystem with a genuine blind spot in self-observation has a 30% base rate of surfacing contradictions per sprint. At N=3, the gate has insufficient power to distinguish a healthy self-observer (30% rate, occasionally unlucky) from a degraded one (5% rate, occasionally lucky).
  - When: The gate promotes subsystems with degraded self-observation at a non-trivial rate; the false-promotion rate is unquantified and the trust architecture cannot bound how often a blind subsystem enters Epoch.
- **P1**: Continuous-mode variant: the spec defines a receipt rate threshold but not a decision rule — the rate is measured and reported as Tier-2 evidence, but Epoch eligibility is never formally tied to it. A subsystem with a rate below threshold is neither blocked from Epoch nor flagged for remediation.
  - When: Continuous mode becomes an observation-only requirement with no enforcement mechanism; it degrades to a logging exercise and the jo-ha-kyū break requirement has no operational teeth.
- **P2**: Gate variant: Break receipts accumulated before an Epoch reset are not explicitly zeroed. A subsystem that entered and exited Epoch carries over prior Break receipts toward its new Compound window's ≥N threshold.
  - When: The gate is satisfiable by historical receipts from a prior operational context, violating the Epoch partial-reset intent of §7.1; the new-conditions re-demonstration requirement is bypassed for the Break phase specifically.

## Decision Lens

In progressive delivery, a canary gate (pass/fail at boundary) is fast and binary but misses gradual drift; shadow evaluation (continuous metric stream) catches drift early but requires a decision rule to act on it. The Break phase gate is structurally a canary gate; the continuous-mode variant is structurally shadow evaluation. Which failure mode — a subsystem that drifts blind slowly versus one that passes a burst gate with noise — is more dangerous for Sylveste's trust architecture?
