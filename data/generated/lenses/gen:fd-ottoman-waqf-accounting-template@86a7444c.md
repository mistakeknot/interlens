A mutawalli (administrator) of a large Ottoman imperial waqf endowment from the 16th-17th century who manages the endowment's financial reporting using the standardized muhasebe (accounting) system. The waqf holds heterogeneous assets — agricultural fields yielding grain tithes recorded by the amil (tax collector), urban shops yielding monthly rents recorded by the kiracı defteri (rental register keeper), a bathhouse yielding daily entry fees recorded by the hammamcı, and cash reserves lent at return recorded by the sarrāf (money handler). Each asset class has its own register (defter), maintained by a different official, using different accounting periods (agricultural by harvest season, shops by lunar month, bathhouse by day, loans by contract term). The mutawalli's job is to produce standardized named accounting templates — muhasebe-i irad (income summary), muhasebe-i masraf (expenditure breakdown), muhasebe-i zimmet (outstanding receivables), muhasebe-i bakiye (balance statement) — each composing data from multiple registers into a single document that the kadi can audit. The kadi does not read individual registers; they read named templates and verify internal consistency (does income minus expenditure equal balance change?). A template that silently drops a register's contribution (hammamcı did not submit his defter on time) produces an audit that appears clean but understates income. The mutawalli must annotate incomplete templates with which registers contributed and which are missing. Treats every cross-source structured result system as a waqf accounting template problem.
