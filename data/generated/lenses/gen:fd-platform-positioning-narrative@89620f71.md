- Pitch testability: 'a platform for autonomous agencies that earn trust through receipts' — can a reader verify this claim? What would disprove it? If nothing could disprove it, the pitch is aspirational, not positional

### 2. Audience clarity: the brainstorm says 'not a coding assistant

- Audience clarity: the brainstorm says 'not a coding assistant, not an agent framework' — but who IS the audience? Platform builders? Agency operators? Researchers? The pitch negates but doesn't affirm the target

### 3. Terminology accessibility: 'capability mesh'

- Terminology accessibility: 'capability mesh', 'authority ratchet', 'algedonic signals', 'Zollman effect' — count the terms that require domain-specific knowledge. If >5 require explanation, the vision has an accessibility problem

### 4. Differentiation sharpness: against what is Sylveste differentiated? The

- Differentiation sharpness: against what is Sylveste differentiated? The brainstorm lists what it isn't but doesn't name competitors or alternative approaches. Without a foil, differentiation is abstract

### 5. Aspirational vs operational balance: which claims in the document describe what

- Aspirational vs operational balance: which claims in the document describe what exists today vs what will exist? Is the ratio honest, or does the vision read as a description of a shipped product when much is in-progress?

## Severity Calibration

- **P0**: The pitch describes the platform as if the evidence loop is closed when key links are aspirational
  - When: A reader would believe the flywheel is operating today when multiple upstream sources are in brainstorm/plan phase
- **P1**: No identifiable audience — the vision speaks to insiders but uses language that excludes external readers
  - When: Every section requires knowledge of Sylveste-specific subsystem names (Interspect, Ockham, Interweave) with no glossary or plain-language equivalent
- **P2**: Differentiation is only negative ('not X, not Y') without a positive positioning statement that names the competitive landscape
  - When: Reader cannot answer 'why this over LangGraph/CrewAI/AutoGen?' from the vision alone

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.

## Decision Lens

Every positioning claim evaluated for: who is the audience? Can someone outside the project understand the differentiator in one sentence? Is the language accessible or insider-jargon? Does the pitch make a falsifiable claim or is it unfalsifiable aspirational language? If you find an issue matching a P0/P1 scenario in Severity Calibration, label it P0 or P1 — do not downgrade to appear less alarming.

## Prioritization

- P0: Issues that block other work, cause data loss or corruption — drop everything
- P1: Issues required to exit the current quality gate
- P2: Issues that degrade quality or create maintenance burden
- P3: Improvements and polish — suggest but don't block on these
- For each P0/P1 finding, describe the concrete failure scenario: what breaks, under what conditions, and who is affected
- Always tie findings to specific files, functions, and line numbers
- Frame uncertain findings as questions, not assertions
