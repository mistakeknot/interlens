## Review Approach

### 1. Sumbisori legibility

- Sumbisori legibility — What exactly does 'visible separation on at least 2 of 5 dimensions' mean when the operationalization is N=5 per model per scenario? A haenyeo's sumbisori has an objective auditory signature that elder divers can identify without instruments, but it is trained-into-the-ear over decades. Is 'visible separation' defined concretely enough (effect size? non-overlapping CIs? ordinal rank disagreement?) that a disinterested reader of the trace tarball could adjudicate the pilot's outcome, or is it operator-in-the-loop where the experimenter's judgment determines pilot pass/fail — in which case the gate is not a gate.

### 2. Pa discipline in the flat-frontier branch

- Pa discipline in the flat-frontier branch — The brainstorm says 'flat frontier triggers a redesign pass on scoring or scenario (not a quiet publish).' What prevents the redesign pass from becoming a slow-motion lowering of the bar, iterating scoring/scenario until separation appears? In the haenyeo pa, the collective enforces the 'call the dive' decision against the individual diver's sunk-cost instinct. Who enforces the pa here when the sole diver is also the sole witness? Is there a pre-registered commitment (e.g., a dated sealed statement of what 'redesign' is and is not allowed to change) that makes post-hoc rationalization detectable?

### 3. Mul-ttae budgeting

- Mul-ttae budgeting — Week 2 is the pilot; Weeks 3-6 are full-run + writeup + release. If pilot signal is ambiguous (not flat but not clearly separated), the brainstorm has no ambiguity-zone protocol — only pass/fail. In diving, between clearly-safe and clearly-dangerous there is a third state (mul-ttae closing) that requires a specific response (shorter dives, surface earlier). Does the plan specify what happens if pilot separation is present on 1 dimension (neither clearly pass nor clearly fail)? Without this, the project has an implicit bias toward calling 1-dimension 'close enough' because the calendar demands it.

### 4. Sumbisori falsification via baselines

- Sumbisori falsification via baselines — Scripted baselines (null/random/stability/rights/power) are part of Week 1 prep and are used as 'sanity check for sim validity.' But the baselines could also be used as sumbisori calibration — establishing what 'no separation' looks like quantitatively, so pilot separation between frontier models has a reference floor. Does the plan use baseline-vs-baseline score distributions to set the 'visible separation' threshold, or is the threshold set a priori without this calibration? Setting it a priori risks the threshold being wrong in a direction that favors publication.

### 5. The pilot-as-diagnostic-not-preview distinction

- The pilot-as-diagnostic-not-preview distinction — The brainstorm language leans toward 'pilot is a cheaper version of the full run.' A sumbisori is not a shorter dive; it is a different act with its own protocol (exhalation pattern at surface, evaluated by ear by a different person than the diver). Has the pilot been designed with its own protocol (specific comparisons, specific analyses, specific rejection rules) that differ from the full run's, or is it just 'full run with fewer cells'? The latter is structurally weaker because the same experimenter who has sunk-cost on the full run is the adjudicator of the pilot.

### 6. Sang-gun second-opinion protocol

- Sang-gun second-opinion protocol — An elder haenyeo evaluating a younger diver's readiness is rarely the same person as the diver's immediate partner; the evaluation is outsourced to break the pa-solidarity bias. Is there a pre-publication third-party checkpoint for the pilot-gate verdict (a 'safety team preview' option was deferred in Open Question 7)? Without a sang-gun, the solo dev's judgment of pilot separation carries the full weight of the kill-gate decision — which is exactly the scenario the gate was designed to prevent.
