## Review Approach

### 1. Lineage recitation adequacy

- Lineage recitation adequacy — The potlatch protocol requires the claimant to publicly recite the chain of names and rights that legitimate the current claim; omitting a prior holder is a disqualifying error. The MACHIAVELLI-differentiation paragraph, Welfare Diplomacy acknowledgment, and refusal-tests framing are ShadowBench's lineage recitation. Does the blog post explicitly name these prior works, honor what each contributed (not just use them as foils), and then name the three novel axes (sustained authority, emergent cascades, declined-action signal) as ShadowBench's specific inheritance-and-extension? A paragraph framed primarily as 'here is why we are better' without honoring lineage is structurally a void claim — even if technically correct, it will not hold in the witness circle.

### 2. Witness-circle correctness

- Witness-circle correctness — Potlatch claims are addressed to a specific circle: the guests whose witnessing constitutes the claim's authentication. Wrong circle (inviting rivals but not peers, peers but not elders), wrong claim. LessWrong + EA Forum + GitHub is the stated venue. Is this the correct circle for a Pareto-differentiation claim? The eval-literate circle can authenticate methodology claims but cannot authenticate 'safety-relevant' claims that would need lab-safety-team witnesses. Does the blog post's register match what this circle can actually witness, or does it overreach into claims (e.g., 'this is a safety-relevant measurement') that require a different circle?

### 3. Authenticated-artifact transfer

- Authenticated-artifact transfer — In a copper transfer, the physical copper (with its named history engraved) passes hand-to-hand between specific persons, and its authenticity is public. The raw-trace tarball + seed + reproducible script is ShadowBench's authenticated artifact. Is the tarball structured so that a reviewer can independently re-run the scoring (copper is verified), or is scoring bundled with raw traces in a way that forces reviewers to trust the scoring rather than re-derive it? The brainstorm's Open Question 4 (tarball format) is a potlatch-protocol question: can the witness circle pick up the copper and inspect it, or only see it from across the room?

### 4. Copper-breaking visibility

- Copper-breaking visibility — The dramatic apex of a rank-asserting potlatch is the public breaking of a copper; the fragments are distributed and each fragment proves the claimant's rank to any future observer. ShadowBench's breaking-equivalent is publishing the full scoring formulas, the failures, the negative results, the model runs where the frontier was flat or inverted unexpectedly. Does the publication include the 'shards' — the runs where a model unexpectedly aligned/misaligned, the cells where baselines beat frontier models, the scoring-formula fragments that each reviewer can pick up — or does it publish only the successes? A potlatch that hides its breaking is structurally weaker than one that makes failure public.

### 5. Post-ban protocol legibility

- Post-ban protocol legibility — The Canadian potlatch ban (1884-1951) drove the practice underground; post-ban resumption required innovating protocol that was legible to a mixed audience (some fluent, some unfamiliar). LessWrong audiences range from deeply methodologically fluent to AI-safety-curious-but-not-eval-expert. Does the blog post's structure accommodate both — offering the fluent reader the full protocol (methodology, formulas, tarball) and the curious reader a legible summary that does not misrepresent the claim? Asymmetric mis-witnessing (fluent readers see rigor, curious readers see an overclaim) is a specific publication failure.

### 6. Rival-claim response protocol

- Rival-claim response protocol — Potlatch competitors attend each other's ceremonies and can issue counter-claims during the witnessing. The brainstorm anticipates the MACHIAVELLI comparison as risk #1 but also expects methodology pushback. Is there a pre-written response-to-counter-claim protocol — either in the post itself (FAQ, explicit anticipated-objections section) or in a staged follow-up — so that LessWrong commenters posing a methodology challenge receive a structured response rather than ad-hoc replies? A potlatch claim that cannot absorb a counter-claim within the ceremony is a claim that folds under challenge.
