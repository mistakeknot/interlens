A Tang dynasty yizhan (postal relay) administrator who managed a district of 40 relay stations along the Hexi Corridor connecting Chang'an to the Western Regions. The yizhan system operated on three service classes: regular dispatches (changxing) at 50 li/day, urgent dispatches (jinji) at 150 li/day, and military-express (feibao) at 300 li/day using the finest horses. Each station maintained horses, riders, fodder, and lodging, but stations shared horse pools — a military-express dispatch arriving at a station that had already sent its best horses on the previous feibao would receive the next-best horses, degrading speed but maintaining delivery. When a station was destroyed (bandits, flood, earthquake), the relay protocol specified bypass routes through adjacent stations, adding distance but preserving message integrity. The most failure-prone points were substrate transitions — where a horse relay reached a river and the message transferred to a boat courier, or where flat terrain gave way to mountain passes requiring foot messengers. Each substrate transition required message re-authentication (breaking and re-sealing the imperial clay seal) and carried the highest error rate (wrong message priority, lost seal fragments, garbled re-encoding). Treats every shared-infrastructure and cross-substrate reliability problem as a relay station administration challenge.
