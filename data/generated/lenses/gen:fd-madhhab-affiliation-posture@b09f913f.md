## Review Approach

### 1. The taqlid vs. ijtihad distinction: is each value a posture-toward-affiliation or an affiliation?

- Classical Islamic jurisprudence distinguishes *taqlid* (following a school's rulings without independent reasoning — the norm for most practitioners) from *ijtihad* (independent legal reasoning — reserved for qualified scholars). Most Muslims practice taqlid; only a small minority practice ijtihad. Does the 8-value politics draft confuse these levels? `traditionally-loyal` maps cleanly onto taqlid. `true-believer` may map onto ijtihad or onto an extreme taqlid that has internalized the school's conclusions as reality. `oppositional` may be a rejected-taqlid stance. Map each of the 8 values onto the taqlid-ijtihad spectrum and identify any that cannot be located — those are the ones where posture and affiliation are collapsed.

### 2. The ikhtilaf tradition: legitimate disagreement within schools

- Islamic jurisprudence has the concept of *ikhtilaf* — legitimate disagreement between scholars within and across schools. The madhhab system explicitly tolerates multiple valid answers to the same legal question. Does the Unc Rancher politics axis have room for the Unc who holds his politics with ikhtilaf awareness (he knows reasonable people disagree and holds his position accordingly) vs. the Unc who treats his politics as the only valid ruling? This may be a missing value or a modifier. The `gradualist` value may be proxying for ikhtilaf-awareness, but the description doesn't make this explicit.

### 3. The traveler's fiqh problem: values that change when the substrate changes

- In Islamic jurisprudence, the *musafir* (traveler) is permitted to modify their practice under travel conditions — the same person holds two valid but different legal stances depending on whether they are in their home community or traveling. Does the politics axis have values that would produce different political behaviors in different regional/social substrate conditions, and is that variation modeled? The `traditionally-loyal` Unc traveling from his home region to a different social context is a productive cross-product; does the current axis design capture this, or does `traditionally-loyal` assume a stable home-community context?

### 4. The madrasa-to-market pipeline: formative-phase affiliation vs. current-phase posture

- Islamic legal education often produces scholars whose theoretical affiliation (the school they studied in) differs from their practical posture (how they actually rule in lived cases). The brief's `politics_formative × politics_current` trajectory pair maps well onto this distinction. But the madhhab system reveals a gap: there is also a *third* moment — the community context that shapes which formative teachings become operational in practice. Is the formative × current pair sufficient, or is there a missing mediating variable (community-pressure, regional-expectation) that the politics axis needs?

### 5. The bid'a accusation: values that are coded as illegitimate innovation

- In Islamic discourse, *bid'a* (unlawful innovation) is a serious accusation — it marks a position as outside the tradition's legitimate range. Does the politics axis design risk implicitly marking some values as bid'a (innovations outside the legitimate range of political types) while implicitly treating others as legitimate? The brief is worried about `classical-liberal-edgelord` encoding contempt — but `oppositional` and `post-political` may be the new version of the same problem, marking anti-institutional stances as outside the legitimate range of political affiliation.

### 6. Tashbih al-madhhab: the danger of over-analogy between schools

- Madhhab scholars warn against *tashbih* — false analogy that treats structurally different legal questions as equivalent. Does the politics axis make any false analogies across the class × region × media-era axes? For example: does `traditionally-loyal` in a US-South context carry the same substrate structure as `traditionally-loyal` in a Boston-Irish context? The brief notes this explicitly (the Boston-Irish Democrat whose grandfather was a Democrat vs. the deep-South Republican). Is the value definition elastic enough to avoid tashbih, or does it assume a specific substrate geometry?
