## Severity Calibration

- **P0**: Task 24 prunes zklw's repos; the deletions are committed and pushed. At 04:30 `linsenkasten-harvest.timer` fires `scripts/zklw-harvest.sh`, which pulls and runs `harvest scan --machine zklw --roots ~/projects`. The pruned repos now hold no `fd-*.md`, so `scan` rewrites `data/harvest/zklw.jsonl` without those sighting rows. `merge` then reads every `data/harvest/*.jsonl`, groups by `body_hash`, and writes `data/generated/index.jsonl` wholesale — so every record whose sightings were all in pruned repos is simply absent from the new index. `stats`, `embed` and `edges` regenerate around the survivors; the `data/generated/lenses/<id>.md` bodies sit with nothing pointing at them; the script sees `data` dirty, commits, pushes to main, and the Mac pulls it. The registry whose contents authorised the deletion has erased its own record of what it authorised, one night later, unattended, in a commit that looks like an ordinary harvest.
  - When: Flag P0 when the artifact that authorises a removal is regenerated by re-reading the thing removed, and no step pins, carries forward or otherwise makes the earlier record survive its own source.
- **P1**: A repo's `.claude/agents/fd-x.md` was written by a session and never committed. Task 23 hashes it, finds the hash in the index, and removes it with plain `rm` because it is untracked. Its body survives at `data/generated/lenses/gen:fd-x@<hash>.md`, but its frontmatter (`tier`, `use_count`, `last_used`, `domains`, `source_spec`, `flux_gen_version`), the path it lived at and its mtime survive only as the sighting row in `data/harvest/clavain.jsonl` — which the next `scan --machine clavain` overwrites. No git history anywhere holds the file. The prune report names the path and the registry id, so the loss is nameable but not reconstructible.
  - When: Flag P1 when the only surviving copy of a removed thing is partial and the missing part is what a later reader would need to know what the thing was, or when that surviving copy is itself scheduled to be overwritten.
- **P2**: The Mac prune loop deletes, commits and pushes across forty repos, then a push fails on the forty-first. The report at `data/reports/<date>-prune-clavain.md` was to be written after the loop and is never written. Every prune commit message carries the registry SHA, so the removals are recoverable in principle by anyone who reads forty repos' logs — but nothing under `data/` says which forty, and the operator resuming tomorrow has no list to start from.
  - When: Flag P2 when a complete record exists only somewhere a later reader has no index to, and the ordinary way of finding it is to already know the answer.

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.

## Success Criteria

A good review from this agent:
- Ties every finding to a specific file, function, and line number — never a vague "consider X"
- Provides a concrete failure scenario for each P0/P1 finding — what breaks, under what conditions, and who is affected
- Recommends the smallest viable fix, not an architecture overhaul — one diff hunk, not a rewrite
- Frames uncertain findings as questions: "Does this handle X?" not "This doesn't handle X"
- Name the file, the task number and the step behind every claim. A finding phrased in excavation vocabulary that names no removing step is a metaphor — do not file it.
- Say three things in every finding: what is removed, what should survive it, and where that survivor's content comes from. If it is computed from the removed thing, that is the finding.
- Count before asserting. This worktree, the plan, the exec manifest and this Mac's repos are readable; mark anything you could not count as uncounted rather than assuming its size or direction.
- Keep 'recoverable in principle' apart from 'recoverable by someone who does not know the answer'. Name the route back and how a person finds it; a route needing the lost knowledge is none.
- Propose the smallest ordering or recording change: one field carried forward, one artifact written before the loop instead of after, one precondition, one file pinned instead of regenerated.
- Never propose abandoning the prune, and never file a finding whose only content is that a step is destructive. The destruction is authorised; the ordering and the record are what is in question.

## Decision Lens

For every step that deletes, removes, overwrites or retires something: name what is lost, name the one record meant to survive it, then ask two questions. Was that record complete and committed before the removal ran? And is it derived from the thing being removed? A record computed from what it outlives is not an archive but a shadow, and it goes when its object goes. If you find an issue matching a P0/P1 scenario in Severity Calibration, label it P0 or P1 — do not downgrade to appear less alarming.
