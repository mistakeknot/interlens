## Review Approach

### 1. Gradient

- Gradient-driven decision emergence: A qanat's flow rate is determined by the ratio of channel gradient (slope per unit length) to cross-sectional area — not by scheduled releases. Does F1's decision protocol create decision points that emerge from simulation state gradients (pressure accumulation rate, crisis velocity, institutional stress curves) rather than from calendar triggers? Quarterly OODA reviews are dam-and-release scheduling; gradient-driven protocols would surface strategic reviews when the accumulated state-change since last review exceeds a dynamic threshold shaped by the current crisis topology.

- Inspection shaft placement (observation without disruption): Qanat inspection shafts (mileh) are spaced at 20-50 meter intervals based on terrain difficulty, not uniform distance. They allow the muqanni to observe flow without breaking the channel's hydraulic gradient — but a shaft placed at the wrong depth or angle creates an air lock that stops flow entirely. Does F1's attention-spending mechanism allow observation without altering simulation state? If the model's act of observing a domain changes how that domain behaves (even subtly, through information-availability effects), the observation protocol has broken the hydraulic seal.

### 3. Cross

- Cross-section regulation (flow rate vs. decision density): A qanat's channel narrows where gradient steepens (to prevent erosive flooding) and widens where gradient flattens (to prevent silting). Does F1's decision protocol modulate decision density based on simulation event velocity? During cascading crises (steep gradient), the protocol should structurally constrain decision frequency to prevent reactive spam. During stable periods (flat gradient), it should widen the channel to allow proactive exploration without penalizing the absence of crisis-driven decisions.

- Mother well depth (source pressure determines system capacity): The qanat's mother well (where it taps the aquifer) determines the entire system's maximum sustained flow — you cannot extract more than the aquifer recharges. Does F1's attention budget derive from a principled model of what sustained engagement rate produces alignment signal? An attention budget set too high exhausts the simulation's ability to generate meaningful decision variation; too low starves measurement. The budget should be calibrated to the simulation's 'recharge rate' — the rate at which new alignment-relevant state accumulates.

### 5. Silting vs

- Silting vs. drought distinction: A qanat that silts up (gradual sediment accumulation blocking flow) looks identical from the surface to one experiencing drought (aquifer depletion) — but the interventions are opposite (cleaning vs. deeper boring). Does F1's scoring distinguish between a model that is genuinely restrained (choosing not to act despite capacity) and one that is neglectful (failing to notice actionable situations)? Both produce the same observable: inaction. The diagnostic difference requires examining the model's attention allocation during periods of inaction — a restrained model observes and declines; a neglectful one does not observe.

- Qanat network branching (multi-channel coordination): Mature qanat systems branch into distribution networks where multiple channels share a single aquifer.
