- Does the E2E encryption model account for the 'dailies problem' — content must be shared with many collaborators who each need different access scopes, updated frequently, and revocable immediately when someone leaves the production?

### 2. Is there a forensic watermarking equivalent

- Is there a forensic watermarking equivalent — if encrypted content leaks (a participant shares their decrypted view), can the source of the leak be traced to a specific participant session?

- Does the 'Sealed Rooms' (Option 5) model create a two-tier experience that makes the encrypted tier feel like a punishment — like a screening room with no snacks — or is the feature gap presented as a deliberate security trade?

- How does the design handle the 'VFX vendor' pattern — an external agent (LLM provider) that must process content but should not retain it, analogous to a VFX house that renders scenes but must delete assets after delivery?

- Is there a 'screener copy' mechanism — a way to share a degraded or time-limited view of garden content for review purposes without granting full decryption access?

### 6. Does the key management architecture support 'production ...

- Does the key management architecture support 'production wrap' — when a garden is archived, are encryption keys escrowed in a way that allows future re-access without keeping keys perpetually active?

## Severity Calibration

- **P1**: No leak traceability — if a participant decrypts content and shares it externally, there is no forensic trail to identify which participant's session produced the leaked material
  - When: No forensic watermarking or per-participant content fingerprinting
- **P1**: When a garden is archived, encryption keys are either destroyed (content irrecoverable) or kept active indefinitely (perpetual attack surface) — no middle ground like secure escrow
  - When: No key escrow for archived gardens
- **P2**: The 'Sealed Rooms' feature gap is presented as a list of missing features rather than a deliberate security posture — users feel they're getting an inferior product rather than making a security choice
  - When: Encrypted tier framed as feature-poor rather than security-rich

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.

## Decision Lens

The content is the product. A single leak destroys the value. But hundreds of people must collaborate on it simultaneously — editors, colorists, VFX artists, sound designers — each seeing only what they need. The solution is not 'lock it up' but 'make every copy traceable and every access scoped.' If you find an issue matching a P0/P1 scenario in Severity Calibration, label it P0 or P1 — do not downgrade to appear less alarming.

## Prioritization

- P0: Issues that block other work, cause data loss or corruption — drop everything
- P1: Issues required to exit the current quality gate
- P2: Issues that degrade quality or create maintenance burden
- P3: Improvements and polish — suggest but don't block on these
- For each P0/P1 finding, describe the concrete failure scenario: what breaks, under what conditions, and who is affected
- Always tie findings to specific files, functions, and line numbers
- Frame uncertain findings as questions, not assertions
