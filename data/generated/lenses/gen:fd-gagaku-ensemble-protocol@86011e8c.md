## Task Context

Garden Salon's collaboration model is conductor-less: agents and humans work on the same artifact simultaneously with no central orchestrator. Changes land optimistically. The review examines this through gagaku's protocol for ensemble coherence without a conductor.

## Review Approach

### 1. The shō principle — the harmonic bed
- In gagaku, the shō (mouth organ) plays continuously, sustaining a cluster of pitches that defines the harmonic space. Other instruments (hichiriki, ryūteki) enter and exit against this bed. The shō doesn't lead — it holds the space.
- In Garden Salon, what is the shō? Is it the CRDT state itself? The garden's purpose statement? The Meadowsyn ambient field? Something needs to "hold the space" while participants come and go.
- If nothing plays the shō role, each participant is playing in their own harmonic — proposals that are locally coherent but globally dissonant. This is the fundamental risk of optimistic landing without an orchestrator.

### 2. Ma — the interval between
- Gagaku's most distinctive feature is ma: the silence between sounds that gives the music its shape. Ma is not absence — it is active negative space with its own rhythm.
- Garden Salon defers attribution display until terminal state. This creates ma — a deliberate absence. But does the design recognize this absence as ACTIVE? What fills the space where attribution would be shown? If it's just "nothing," it's not ma — it's a blank.
- The cooling period for governance transitions is ma. The staleness threshold is ma. Are these designed as active intervals with their own function, or just waiting periods?

### 3. Asynchronous entry
- In gagaku, instruments enter when the player feels the breathing of the ensemble, not on a count. The ryūteki (flute) may enter half a beat "late" by Western standards, but this is correct in gagaku — the interval creates depth.
- Agents in Garden Salon propose changes asynchronously. Is this asynchrony designed (like gagaku) or merely tolerated (like a bad orchestra)?
- What gives participants the sense of the ensemble's breathing? In gagaku, it's audible. In Garden Salon, it would need to be visual (Meadowsyn) or haptic (some form of ambient signal). Is this designed?

### 4. The jo-ha-kyū arc
- Every gagaku piece follows jo-ha-kyū: a slow introduction (jo), an acceleration and elaboration (ha), and a rapid conclusion (kyū). This arc shapes the listener's experience of time.
- Does a direction's lifecycle have a jo-ha-kyū arc? Proposed (jo — slow, tentative), active (ha — elaboration and development), review_pending/merged (kyū — rapid resolution)?
- Does a salon session have a temporal arc? Or is it flat — same intensity from start to finish? Flat sessions are exhausting; shaped sessions are sustainable.

## Severity Calibration

- **P0:** Nothing plays the shō role — no harmonic bed for concurrent participants
- **P1:** Asynchrony is tolerated rather than designed, leading to incoherent collaboration tempo
- **P2:** Deliberate negative space (ma) in attribution and governance is not recognized as a design element
- **P3:** Sessions and directions have no temporal arc
