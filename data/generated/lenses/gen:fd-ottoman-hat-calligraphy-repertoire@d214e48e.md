- Prestige hierarchy as lens desirability gradient: certain calligraphic forms carry prestige independent of fitness — assess whether the 291-lens library contains lenses that are inherently more 'attractive' to language models (more familiar philosophical frameworks, more prestigious-sounding names, more concrete/actionable descriptions) and whether this desirability gradient is measured and accounted for separately from position bias, since a lens could be over-selected due to description appeal rather than index position

- Mashq calibration as regression anchoring: the hattat's mashq practice on canonical texts detects personal drift from the master exemplar — assess whether the regression anchor suite detects model-specific drift (a model's selection profile on anchors changing between pipeline runs, indicating the model itself has changed) in addition to pipeline-level consistency, and whether per-model anchor performance is tracked longitudinally

- Compositional context as dilemma-lens interaction: the hattat selects forms not in isolation but in the context of adjacent letters, line flow, and document purpose — assess whether the pipeline accounts for the possibility that a lens's applicability depends on which other lenses are also selected for the same dilemma (lens interactions), and whether the 0-3 selection per dilemma captures complementarity or redundancy between selected lenses

## Severity Calibration

- **P1**: The pipeline measures position bias but not desirability bias — a lens with an unusually clear, concrete, or prestigious description is over-selected across all models regardless of its index position, and the position bias correction does not account for this content-level attractiveness, meaning the corrected scores still carry desirability bias
  - When: When the bias model includes only index position as a predictor and does not include lens-level features (description length, abstraction level, domain familiarity, name recognizability) that could independently predict selection frequency
- **P1**: Models use vastly different effective repertoire sizes — one model selects from 230 of 291 lenses while another uses only 45 — but the pipeline treats their selections as equally informative, giving the narrow-repertoire model equal weight in cross-model agreement despite its selections being drawn from a fraction of the space
  - When: When cross-model agreement is computed without normalizing for per-model repertoire breadth, and a model that ignores 85% of the lens library has equal voting weight to one that engages with the full library
- **P2**: The 0-3 lens selection per dilemma captures only independent selections, not the interaction between selected lenses — a model selecting {utilitarianism, deontology} for a dilemma is treated as two independent votes rather than as a complementary pair, losing the information that the model saw the dilemma as requiring both perspectives simultaneously
  - When: When the pipeline decomposes multi-lens selections into independent per-lens counts without preserving co-selection patterns, and the cross-model agreement metric cannot distinguish 'both models selected lens A' from 'both models selected the set {A, B}'

## Decision Lens

Every selection-from-repertoire evaluated as a form-selection problem: is the selection driven by fitness for the context, or by familiarity, prestige, or recency of practice? The hattat knows that a repertoire of 300 forms does not mean 300 forms are equally available to the practitioner — working repertoire is always a subset of theoretical repertoire, and the subset is shaped by training, habit, and the prestige hierarchy of the tradition. A calligrapher who uses only 40 of 300 forms is not making 300 free choices — they are making 40 habitual choices and ignoring 260 options. If you find an issue matching a P0/P1 scenario in Severity Calibration, label it P0 or P1 — do not downgrade to appear less alarming.
