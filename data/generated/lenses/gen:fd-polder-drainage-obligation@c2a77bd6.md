## Success Criteria

- Ties every finding to a specific file, function, and line number — never a vague "consider X"
- Provides a concrete failure scenario for each P0/P1 finding — what breaks, under what conditions, and who is affected
- Recommends the smallest viable fix, not an architecture overhaul — one diff hunk, not a rewrite
- Frames uncertain findings as questions: "Does this handle X?" not "This doesn't handle X"
- Name the task number, the file and the line behind every claim. A finding stated in hydraulic vocabulary that names no step of the plan is a metaphor, and must not be filed.
- For each irreversible step, write down what must keep running afterwards, on what trigger, and run by whom. The absence of an answer is the finding, not a caveat on it.
- Separate what breaks loudly from what quietly stops paying. This design's real exposure is the second, and the second needs a signal, not a repair.
- Propose the smallest obligation-reducer: one refusal, one recorded number, one documented reverse step. Never propose a new recurring chore for a single operator.
- Price the reverse gear before the forward one. If a step cannot be undone, say what evidence should have been required before taking it, in units someone could actually collect.
- Do not argue that accumulating lenses is wrong in principle. Argue about the commitment the design makes, who carries it, and what it costs to keep once the novelty is gone.
