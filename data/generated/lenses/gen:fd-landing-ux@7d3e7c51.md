## Task Context

Single-screen layout: LandingNav (wordmark + dark mode toggle) at top, then h1 headline (3xl/4xl), then DiffAnimation (monospace font, left/right arrows, dot indicators, autoplay cycling 3 examples at 80ms/word), then WaitlistForm (email + select + button horizontal row + privacy line). ThemeSwitcher is a hidden Shift+` easter egg with a toast notification. Max width 2xl (672px), centered. No below-fold content except the form.

## Review Approach

### 1. Visual hierarchy and eye path: nav > headline > animation > form is the

- Visual hierarchy and eye path: nav > headline > animation > form is the intended reading order — assess whether type sizes, spacing, and contrast enforce this path or allow the eye to stray to low-value elements

### 2. Animation as hero content: the DiffAnimation is the primary value demonstration

- Animation as hero content: the DiffAnimation is the primary value demonstration but it runs on a timer and may complete before a slow reader finishes the headline — assess whether autoplay vs. user-initiated serves cold traffic better

### 3. Animation cognitive load: the 'before' state shows full text

- Animation cognitive load: the 'before' state shows full text, then words are struck through and new words appear simultaneously — assess whether a first-time visitor can parse what is happening without an explanatory label or progress indicator

### 4. Interactive controls discoverability: left/right arrows and dot indicators are

- Interactive controls discoverability: left/right arrows and dot indicators are present but low-affordance; a new visitor may not realize they can navigate examples or may interpret the animation as purely decorative

### 5. Single-column vs. form row layout: the form places three interactive elements

- Single-column vs. form row layout: the form places three interactive elements (email, select, button) in one horizontal row; assess the visual weight balance and whether the CTA button gets sufficient optical prominence

### 6. Dark mode toggle and Shift+` easter egg: two interactive elements that are not

- Dark mode toggle and Shift+` easter egg: two interactive elements that are not conversion-related — assess whether the dark mode toggle in the nav competes for attention with the CTA, and whether the undiscoverable easter egg is a distraction risk or a delight asset

## Severity Calibration

- **P1**: Animation completes before visitor reaches the form on slow scroll
  - When: If a visitor pauses on the animation and watches all three cycles before reading the headline, the page has spent 15–18 seconds of attention budget on demonstration and the visitor may feel they have already consumed the product — no reason to sign up
- **P2**: No label distinguishing 'before' from 'after' in the animation
  - When: The transition from struck-through red text to green inserted text is clear to someone who understands editorial markup conventions, but freelancers without copy-editing backgrounds may not immediately parse the visual language — a small 'Before' / 'After' label would reduce interpretation latency
- **P3**: Arrow navigation controls have no visible hover state on touch devices
  - When: On tablet touchscreens, hover-based affordance cues don't render; visitors may not discover the prev/next navigation and see only the first diff example, reducing the demonstration surface

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.

## Decision Lens

Does the page layout, visual weight, and interaction design guide every visitor's attention toward the single conversion action without introducing confusion, distraction, or cognitive load? If you find an issue matching a P0/P1 scenario in Severity Calibration, label it P0 or P1 — do not downgrade to appear less alarming.

## Prioritization

- P0: Issues that block other work, cause data loss or corruption — drop everything
- P1: Issues required to exit the current quality gate
- P2: Issues that degrade quality or create maintenance burden
- P3: Improvements and polish — suggest but don't block on these
- For each P0/P1 finding, describe the concrete failure scenario: what breaks, under what conditions, and who is affected
- Always tie findings to specific files, functions, and line numbers
- Frame uncertain findings as questions, not assertions
