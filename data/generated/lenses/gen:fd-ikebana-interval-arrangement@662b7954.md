## Task Context

Six open design questions for a dynamic flora system in an elven colony sim (Rust ECS, WASM). The flora system sits on a geology foundation (TileData with soil, fertility, erosion, water table). Questions cover: species count and generation method (fixed vs procgen, 8-12 vs 20-30), sprint scoping, terrain architecture (replace forest terrain with entities vs overlay), observer-dependent beauty, entity density (~600 entities on 40x30 map), and when gardens transition from utility to art form. Core philosophy: system complexity is the source of emergence, not a cost to minimize.

## Review Approach

### 1. Ma (interval/negative space)

- Ma (interval/negative space) and entity density (Q5): ikebana treats the space between elements as the primary compositional medium — the flower is the punctuation, the emptiness is the sentence. Does the flora density design consider that tiles WITHOUT flora entities carry meaning? A meadow tile surrounded by ancient trees is not 'empty' — it is the ma that makes the trees legible. Does density option B (trees as entities, groundcover as grid) accidentally destroy ma by filling every tile with something?

### 2. Shin

- Shin-soe-hikae (three-branch universe) and species count (Q1): the Ikenobo tradition creates infinite variation from three primary elements at different heights and angles. Option A's 8-12 species mirrors this constraint — few enough to know individually, numerous enough for compositional variety. But does the design specify the RELATIONSHIPS between species (which is the heaven-branch, which is the earth-branch)? A species list without a compositional grammar is ingredients without a recipe.

### 3. Observer cultivation

- Observer cultivation and beauty perception (Q4): ikebana's beauty is radically observer-dependent — the same arrangement communicates different things to a child, a casual viewer, and a 30-year practitioner. Option B's 'aesthetic axis modifier' maps to this, but ikebana suggests the modification should be qualitative, not just quantitative (+1 beauty). A high-Structure elf shouldn't just find geometric gardens 'more beautiful' — they should perceive compositional relationships that low-Structure elves literally cannot see.

### 4. Nageirebana (controlled wildness)

- Nageirebana (controlled wildness) and terrain architecture (Q3): the thrown-in style appears natural but is deeply intentional. Option A (full replacement) creates an architecture where every plant is a placed entity — but can the system express nageirebana, where placed entities appear unplaced? The distinction between 'wild meadow' and 'garden' should be a spectrum of intentionality, not a binary. Does the terrain architecture support graduated intentionality?

### 5. Seasonal impermanence (mono no aware)

- Seasonal impermanence (mono no aware) and garden-as-art timing (Q6): ikebana arrangements are temporary by design — they wilt, they are dismantled, the art is in the moment. Garden-as-art should not be a permanent upgrade from utility to art. Can a garden lose its art status? Can a composition fall apart as species die or seasons change? Option A (keep gardens simple) might accidentally treat gardens as permanent fixtures rather than living compositions that require continuous attention.

## Severity Calibration

- **P1**: The density design treats empty tiles as wasted space rather than compositional elements, leading to a world where every non-water tile has a flora entity and no visual breathing room exists
  - When: Q5 Option A is chosen without any design for how empty space between flora entities creates compositional meaning — density is treated as coverage percentage rather than arrangement quality
- **P2**: Observer-dependent beauty is implemented as a quantitative modifier (+1/-1) rather than a qualitative difference in what the observer perceives, collapsing ikebana's depth of cultivated seeing into a simple number
  - When: Q4 Option B is chosen but the aesthetic axis modifier only adjusts beauty magnitude, not what aspects of flora arrangement the elf can perceive
- **P2**: Species are generated as a flat list without compositional grammar — no specification of which species create contrast, which harmonize, which serve as 'heaven-branch' vs 'earth-branch' in arrangements
  - When: Q1 specifies species counts and per-species properties (beauty, soil preference) but not inter-species compositional relationships

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.
