## Severity Calibration

- **P1**: Active direction conflicts are not visible until merge attempt
  - When: If two directions are editing overlapping content and that conflict is not surfaced until one of them attempts to merge, the garden has reproduced the construction failure mode of 'discovered conflict on closeout' — expensive, disruptive, and preventable. The overlap should be visible during active work, not only at the terminal transition.
- **P2**: The garden has no persistent progress signal that communicates to Ryan what has been decided, what is in progress, and what needs his attention without him having to query each direction individually
  - When: A Ryan who must click into each of 4 active directions to understand total garden state is experiencing the site management failure of 'no lookahead schedule posted' — he cannot self-coordinate or prioritize without a synthesis view. The garden-level completion topology should be visible at the garden entry point.
- **P2**: Governance invariants appear as opaque system constraints without explanation
  - When: When a participant tries to delete an attribution record or re-open a merged direction and receives an error, that error should explain the platform invariant being enforced and why it exists — not just 'operation not permitted'. Building code violations come with code references and explanations; platform invariant enforcement should too.

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.

## Decision Lens

Every progress communication decision is evaluated by asking: can a participant who is responsible for one part of this project understand where the whole project is without a meeting, and can they see dependencies that will affect their work before those dependencies arrive? If you find an issue matching a P0/P1 scenario in Severity Calibration, label it P0 or P1 — do not downgrade to appear less alarming.

## Prioritization

- P0: Issues that block other work, cause data loss or corruption — drop everything
- P1: Issues required to exit the current quality gate
- P2: Issues that degrade quality or create maintenance burden
- P3: Improvements and polish — suggest but don't block on these
- For each P0/P1 finding, describe the concrete failure scenario: what breaks, under what conditions, and who is affected
- Always tie findings to specific files, functions, and line numbers
- Frame uncertain findings as questions, not assertions
