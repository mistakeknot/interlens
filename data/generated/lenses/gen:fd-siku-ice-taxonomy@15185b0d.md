## Severity Calibration

- **P1**: Species count is chosen by aesthetic preference for a 'learnable' number without analyzing the decision-space the categories must serve
  - When: The design picks 8 or 12 or 30 species based on what 'feels right' rather than by examining how many meaningfully distinct flora-related decisions elves and players actually make — resulting in either redundant species that collapse to the same gameplay effect or too few species to capture important ecological distinctions
- **P1**: Terrain and flora are treated as independent layers with no shared continuous substrate
  - When: The overlay architecture (Q3-B) creates a dual-representation where terrain says 'forest' and entities say 'three oaks and a birch' without acknowledging that these are the same information at different granularities — systems reading terrain and systems reading entities can reach contradictory conclusions about the same tile
- **P2**: All simulation systems read flora at the same granularity regardless of their decision needs
  - When: Pathfinding, beauty calculation, ecological dynamics, and garden composition all operate on the same species-level entities when some systems need only coarse categories (tree/shrub/groundcover) and others need fine distinctions (species, age, health) — either the coarse systems pay unnecessary computational cost or the fine systems lack the detail they need
