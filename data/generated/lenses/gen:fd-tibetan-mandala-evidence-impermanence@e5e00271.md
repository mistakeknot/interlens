## Task Context

Sylveste v5.0 builds its entire thesis around 'trust earned through observable evidence that compounds over time.' The four upstream systems (Interweave, Ockham, Interop, FluxBench) feed evidence into a flywheel that drives trust ratcheting. The mandala lens tests the thesis's hidden assumption: that compounding is always beneficial and that accumulated evidence remains valid. The specific concern is that the authority ratchet mechanism in Ockham may lack the demotion granularity needed when evidence ages out or environmental conditions shift.

## Severity Calibration

- **P0**: Irrevocable trust from stale evidence
  - When: The authority ratchet accumulates 6 months of positive evidence for a subsystem, then the underlying model or data source changes fundamentally — but the ratchet has no epoch mechanism, so the subsystem retains high authority based on evidence that no longer reflects its actual capability (like a mandala that should have been dissolved but is instead preserved under glass, preventing the construction of a new one that reflects current understanding)
- **P1**: Monotonic compounding blindspot
  - When: The vision's flywheel treats evidence accumulation as always positive — more sprints, more evidence, more trust — without acknowledging that evidence from a period of low environmental complexity may create false confidence that fails under novel conditions (like constructing a mandala in still air and assuming the technique works in wind)
- **P2**: No temporal weighting in mesh minimum
  - When: The capability mesh's weakest-link calculation treats a 90% gate pass rate from 6 months ago the same as a 90% rate from yesterday — no temporal decay, no concept of evidence freshness, leading to a mesh assessment that looks healthy but is actually describing a system that no longer exists

## Decision Lens

Dul-tson-kyil-khor layered construction and ritual dissolution — the mandala is built from center outward in concentric rings of increasing complexity, each ring depending on the stability of inner rings. But the tradition's deepest insight is that the structure MUST be periodically dissolved and rebuilt, because attachment to accumulated form causes rigidity. The question is always: does this accumulated evidence serve the current purpose, or has it become an obstacle to adaptation?
