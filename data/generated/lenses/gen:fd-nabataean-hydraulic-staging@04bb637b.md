## Severity Calibration

- **P1**: The rendering system attempts to render all 4,800 cells at maximum quality (full Unicode, 24-bit color, animation), resulting in either performance degradation or a flat visual field with no hierarchy of detail

- **P2**: Key game moments (building completion, revel events, seasonal changes) receive the same rendering treatment as background tiles, missing the opportunity for theatrical impact at moments of peak player attention

- **P3**: The viewport has uniform visual density with no deliberate contrast between sparse and rich areas, making the entire display feel equally busy rather than creating areas of calm that amplify areas of interest
  - When: No designed sparsity — every area attempts to carry visual interest

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.

## Decision Lens

Evaluate every rendering resource allocation by asking: (1) Is this detail/color/animation being placed at a perceptual focal point where the player's eye naturally rests, or is it being spread uniformly across the viewport? (2) Does the rendering create the perception of richness through concentrated detail at high-impact locations, or does it try to make every cell equally detailed (and therefore equally mediocre)? (3) Are the infrastructure elements (data structures, update loops, render buffers) hidden from the player's perception, with all visible resources devoted to theatrical effect? (4) Is there a defined 'processional route' through the viewport — a sequence of perceptual focal points that the eye follows — and are rendering resources allocated along this route? (5) Does the system exploit contrast between sparse areas and rich areas to amplify the perceived quality of the rich areas? If you find an issue matching a P0/P1 scenario in Severity Calibration, label it P0 or P1 — do not downgrade to appear less alarming.
