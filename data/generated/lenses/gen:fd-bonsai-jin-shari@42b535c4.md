## Severity Calibration

- **P0**: A player reloads three times over a long campaign, each time fleeing the worst thing that happened. Each time, one to three days later, the salon retells exactly that thing and nothing else. The wording is warm, third-person, attributed, and passes every §2.5 rule; the drone never adjudicates. By the third occurrence the player is no longer hearing the world talk about a night that never was — they have induced the rule, and the line reads as a receipt for an action they took at the menu. The grin becomes a flinch.
  - When: P0 because the success condition is tonal and absolute and the doc's entire defence of it is lexical: every guardrail in §2.5 governs words, and none governs whether the machinery's timing and content selection are predictable. A regularity cannot be worded away. Downgrade to P1 only if content selection can be shown to be uncorrelated with the player's reload motive by construction.
- **P1**: A player who wants a dramatic chronicle saves, deliberately dissolves a salon and drives two elves out, then loads. Three ghosts are minted on demand, exactly as if the night had been lost. Meanwhile the player in the next seat who has never reloaded reaches hour eighty with an empty apocrypha register and a visibly plainer history. Both outcomes are correct per §2.1, and the register can no longer be read as a record of anything survived.
  - When: P1 because it does not corrupt the mechanism's operation, but it hollows out the claim the mechanic is built on — that these were nights actually lived and lost — and it inverts the design's own intent by paying careful play less than reload-heavy play. Escalate to P0 if the intended emotional reading depends on the player trusting that ghosts are not authored.
- **P2**: At entry eight of a long campaign the register holds eight one-line hearsay retellings of eight bad things, all surfaced identically, one a day, all of equal apparent weight. The dissolution of the colony's founding salon and a premiere that got a cool reception occupy the same visual and rhetorical mass. The colony's ghost-history has no shape — no wound it is about, no quiet years — and reads as an evenly clipped hedge rather than a tree that survived something.
  - When: P2 rather than P1 because each individual line still works and the mechanism operates as specified; the loss is compositional. Escalate to P1 if the flattening is what causes players to stop reading the surface, since the mechanism then produces nothing at all.

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.
