## Review Approach

### 1. Heat-window dependency: Venetian glass knowledge is time-critical

- Heat-window dependency: Venetian glass knowledge is time-critical — the gather must be worked within seconds, and the master's intervention only transfers knowledge if the student is physically present during that window. Does the two-phase bond formation (Interest → Proximity) model the difference between a student who is intellectually interested but not physically present at the decisive moment, and one who is present but not yet formally bonded? Interest alone without proximity during critical events should be a failed transmission.

### 2. Thermal memory vs declarative knowledge: Murano masters could not write down

- Thermal memory vs declarative knowledge: Murano masters could not write down their formulas because the knowledge lived in color perception (judging temperature by flame color) and haptic feedback (feeling the gather's viscosity). The XP system models skill as a scalar — does it distinguish declarative knowledge (transmissible via demonstration) from embodied thermal intuition (only transmissible through repeated co-presence at the furnace moment)?

### 3. Forced proximity and the island model: Murano glassblowers were confined to the

- Forced proximity and the island model: Murano glassblowers were confined to the island to prevent knowledge leakage — proximity was enforced by external constraint, not by relationship quality. Does the Proximity phase model the difference between proximity maintained by genuine relationship (the student wants to be near the master) and proximity maintained by structural constraint (the student lives in the same settlement and has no choice)?

### 4. Defection and knowledge exile: when a Murano master left Venice

- Defection and knowledge exile: when a Murano master left Venice, they were hunted — but the knowledge they carried was genuine and complete. Does the lineage chain system model what happens when a graduated student leaves the colony? Their school membership should follow them as a live credential, not just a historical label.

### 5. Secret knowledge degradation: the lattimo (milk glass) formula was lost because

- Secret knowledge degradation: the lattimo (milk glass) formula was lost because the lineage broke — no written record existed and the last practitioner died without a student in proximity. Does the school dissolution scenario model permanent knowledge loss when all lineage members die, versus knowledge that can be partially reconstructed from artifacts (surviving glass pieces that the system already tracks as compositions)?

### 6. Master-as-gatekeeper dynamics: in Murano

- Master-as-gatekeeper dynamics: in Murano, the master controlled not just who learned but when the student was deemed ready to attempt independent work — readiness was a social judgment, not a test score. Does graduation at mentor's skill level create a purely mechanical gate that bypasses the mentor's social role as arbiter of readiness?

## Severity Calibration

- **P1**: Proximity phase is passive time accumulation rather than event co-presence
  - When: The transition from ApprenticeshipInterest to Apprenticeship fires after N ticks of proximity without requiring the student to be present during critical craft events — produces students who are geographically nearby but never actually present for the decisive moments of teaching; the bond forms but carries no embodied knowledge
- **P2**: Lineage chain tracks origin but not living access to practice
  - When: A student records 'trained by X, who trained by Y' but if X and Y are dead and no surviving practitioner uses the technique, the school is extinct in practice even though its label persists on compositions — no 'last living practitioner' flag or knowledge-at-risk signal exists
- **P3**: Knowledge departure follows the person, not the relationship
  - When: When a graduated student leaves or dies, their mentor-derived skills leave with them but the system has no mechanic for the mentor to observe the loss, grieve a broken lineage, or attempt emergency transmission to a second student before the window closes

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.
