## Task Context

The target pipeline runs 12 LLMs against 1,360 dilemmas with a 291-lens library, detecting position bias, computing agreement, identifying near-misses, and building regression anchors. The Polynesian navigation lens asks whether bias detection uses redundant independent methods, whether regression anchors have external ground truth or are self-referentially derived from the models being calibrated, whether near-misses are aggregated to reveal systematic lens-pair boundaries, and whether the calibration survives lens library updates.

- Multi-indicator cross-reference as bias detection redundancy: the navigator never trusts a single star — the bearing must be confirmed by wave patterns and zenith stars and wind direction — assess whether the pipeline uses multiple independent methods to detect position bias (not just frequency counting of early-index selection, but also semantic analysis of whether early-indexed lenses are systematically different in content, readability, or specificity from late-indexed lenses), because a single detection method might itself be biased

### 2. Seasonal drift as systematic bias evolution: the star compass must be

- Seasonal drift as systematic bias evolution: the star compass must be recalibrated seasonally because stellar positions shift relative to the horizon — assess whether the pipeline accounts for the possibility that position bias varies across models and across time (as models are updated, their position bias profiles may change), and whether the regression anchor suite is designed to detect calibration drift between pipeline runs rather than assuming a fixed bias profile

### 3. Reference voyage as regression anchor: the navigator's reference voyages are

- Reference voyage as regression anchor: the navigator's reference voyages are canonical passages where the correct bearing is known, used to verify the compass is reading true before trusting it on unknown passages — assess whether the regression anchors include dilemmas where the correct lens selection is established by expert consensus (not just high model agreement), providing a ground-truth calibration signal independent of the models being calibrated

### 4. Ambiguous horizon zones as near-miss boundaries: the navigator treats the

- Ambiguous horizon zones as near-miss boundaries: the navigator treats the boundaries between compass houses as the most critical calibration points because a star on the boundary could be assigned to either house — assess whether the pipeline identifies lens-pair boundaries (pairs of lenses that are most frequently confused with each other) and whether the near-miss analysis maps the full boundary topology of the 291-lens space rather than treating each near-miss as an isolated incident

### 5. Etak (moving reference island) as dynamic calibration frame: in etak navigation

- Etak (moving reference island) as dynamic calibration frame: in etak navigation, the reference island is conceptually 'moved' past the canoe rather than the canoe moving past the island, providing a dynamic reference frame — assess whether the pipeline has a mechanism for re-anchoring its calibration when the lens library itself is updated (lenses added, removed, or redescribed), or whether a library change invalidates all previous calibration and requires a full re-run

## Severity Calibration

- **P0**: The pipeline detects position bias using only selection frequency by index position, but early-indexed lenses happen to be more general/applicable than late-indexed lenses due to library construction order — the detected 'bias' is partially real selection preference for legitimately better-placed items, and the correction over-penalizes early lenses, degrading pipeline accuracy below uncorrected baseline
  - When: When the bias detection method cannot distinguish between positional bias (model selects item because it appears early) and content correlation (early items happen to be more applicable), and the correction is applied without validating that correction improves accuracy on known-good benchmarks
- **P1**: The regression anchor suite verifies pipeline consistency across runs but has no external ground truth — the anchors were selected by high cross-model agreement, which could reflect shared model bias rather than correct classification, meaning the pipeline is calibrating against a biased reference, analogous to a navigator calibrating against reference voyages that were themselves recorded with a miscalibrated compass
  - When: When the regression anchors are derived entirely from model outputs (high agreement = anchor) without any independent expert validation, and the anchor suite could systematically reflect the same biases being corrected
- **P2**: Near-miss analysis identifies individual dilemmas where models disagree but does not map the global boundary topology of the lens space — it finds that lens A and lens B are confused on dilemma 47 but does not discover that lenses A and B are systematically confused across 30 dilemmas, revealing that they are inadequately distinguished in the lens library itself
  - When: When near-miss analysis operates per-dilemma rather than aggregating across the full dataset to identify systematic lens-pair confusion patterns that indicate library taxonomy problems rather than model calibration problems

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.
