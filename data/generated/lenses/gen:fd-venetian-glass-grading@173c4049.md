## Review Approach

### 1. Grade-before-invest principle

- Grade-before-invest principle — Murano masters grade silica purity before deciding furnace temperature and artisan skill level. The brainstorm grades expansion confidence before assigning model tier. Is the grading itself reliable enough to warrant differential investment?

### 2. Thermal commitment

- Thermal commitment — once glass enters the furnace at a temperature, changing mid-process risks shattering. Once an agent starts at a tier, can it be changed? The brainstorm assumes one-shot assignment.

### 3. Batch contamination

- Batch contamination — in glassmaking, one impure batch contaminates the entire crucible. Can one mis-graded agent contaminate the synthesis (Phase 3) by injecting low-quality findings?

### 4. Maestro scarcity

- Maestro scarcity — the best glassblowers are scarce; you don't waste them on batch glass. Opus tokens are scarce (expensive). But maestros sometimes find hidden quality in 'batch' material. Does haiku ever surface findings that sonnet wouldn't?

## Severity Calibration

- **P1**: Grading error: high-purity signal (expansion score 3) assigned to genuinely low-value domain, wasting opus tokens
  - When: Expansion score inflated by P0 in adjacent domain that doesn't actually transfer
- **P2**: Batch contamination: haiku agent produces confused finding that misleads synthesis
  - When: Synthesis doesn't weight findings by agent tier quality

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.
