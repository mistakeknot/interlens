## Task Context

Reviewing encryption and privacy architecture options for Garden Salon. Key questions include: who holds encryption keys, how are keys distributed when new participants join, what happens when participants leave or become inactive, and whether encryption must be all-or-nothing or can support graduated access within a single garden.

## Review Approach

### 1. Whether the encryption model supports graduated access ra...

- Whether the encryption model supports graduated access rather than binary — in mandala construction, outer rings are visible to all attendees, middle rings to initiated monks, inner rings only to the Vajra Master and senior practitioners; does the encryption design allow different content zones within a garden to have different access levels, or is it all-encrypted or all-plaintext?

### 2. Whether key distribution follows a trust preparation path

- Whether key distribution follows a trust preparation path — mandala initiation requires progressive stages of teaching and practice before access is granted; is key distribution in the design a ceremony (with verification, confirmation, and revocation paths) or a mechanical handoff that could be intercepted or misapplied?

### 3. Whether the design handles ritual dissolution

- Whether the design handles ritual dissolution — the completed mandala is swept into a river, and this destruction is as important as creation; what happens when a garden is deleted, a participant leaves, or the encryption keys must be destroyed? Is there a clean dissolution protocol or do encrypted fragments persist as unrecoverable debris?

### 4. Whether the boundary between visible

- Whether the boundary between visible and hidden is principled — the mandala's access tiers follow doctrinal logic, not arbitrary lines; is the encryption boundary (what the server can vs. cannot see) drawn according to a coherent threat model, or is it an ad hoc list of 'encrypt this, leave that open'?

### 5. Whether collaborative creation can proceed when participa...

- Whether collaborative creation can proceed when participants have different access levels — monks working on different rings coordinate through spatial proximity and shared rhythm; can participants with different key access levels collaborate on the same garden without the encryption creating workflow barriers?

### 6. Whether the impermanence of the artifact is accounted for...

- Whether the impermanence of the artifact is accounted for in the key architecture — the mandala is inherently temporary; does the encryption design account for the fact that gardens, sessions, and keys all have finite lifespans, or does it assume permanent availability of decryption capability?

## Severity Calibration

- **P1**: Garden deletion leaves encrypted content fragments permanently irrecoverable but still stored
  - When: If key destruction is not synchronized with content deletion, the platform accumulates ciphertext debris that cannot be audited, recovered, or confirmed destroyed — a mandala that can neither be completed nor properly dissolved
- **P1**: The succession mechanism (owner goes inactive) has no key escrow or recovery path
  - When: If the garden owner holds the master key and becomes unreachable, the garden becomes a sealed tomb — all content encrypted, all participants locked out, with no ritual for transferring authority
- **P2**: All-or-nothing encryption forces AI features off for any garden that wants any privacy
  - When: If there is no graduated encryption (some content encrypted, some processable), gardens must choose between full privacy and full functionality — the mandala where either everyone sees everything or no one can work

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.
