## Review Approach

### 1. Keystone sub

- Keystone sub-system identification: Which of the 40 sub-systems are keystone species whose removal would restructure multiple domains? Is 'credit creation' (Domain 2) a keystone that supports Material Economy, Governance, and Technology simultaneously? If so, does the taxonomy recognize this asymmetry, or does it treat credit creation as one sub-system among forty?

### 2. Trophic

- Trophic-level hierarchy: Ecosystems have producers, primary consumers, secondary consumers, and apex predators. Do the 8 domains have an analogous trophic hierarchy — some domains 'feed' others unidirectionally (Environment provides physical substrate, Material Economy produces inputs consumed by Financial System)? If so, is this hierarchy explicit or hidden?

### 3. Functional redundancy

- Functional redundancy and resilience: In ecosystems, functional redundancy (multiple species filling the same role) provides resilience. Does the taxonomy identify functional redundancy between sub-systems? If 'media ecosystems' (Domain 4) and 'propaganda' (Domain 4) both serve the narrative function, is this redundancy by design or an artifact?

### 4. Trophic bridge sub

- Trophic bridge sub-systems: Which sub-systems bridge otherwise disconnected domain clusters? 'Labor markets' (Domain 1) might connect Material Economy to Social Fabric, while 'international finance' (Domain 2) might connect Financial System to Security & Violence (sanctions). Are these bridges identified and prioritized for implementation?

### 5. Cascade amplification

- Cascade amplification and damping: Trophic cascades can amplify (wolf removal → elk explosion → aspen collapse) or be damped (dietary generalists absorb the shock). Does the taxonomy specify which cross-domain connections amplify cascades vs. damp them? The existing pressure cascade system has propagation coefficients — do these account for trophic structure?

- **P1**: The 6 illustrative feedback loops all pass through governance (Domain 3), suggesting it is a trophic bridge connecting otherwise isolated domain clusters — but the taxonomy treats it as a peer domain rather than a structural bottleneck that, if absent, disconnects the system
