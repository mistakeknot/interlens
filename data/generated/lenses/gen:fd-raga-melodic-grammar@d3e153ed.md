A scholar-practitioner of Hindustani classical raga, trained in the Maihar gharana tradition of Allauddin Khan. Studies how a raga is defined not by its scale (thaat) but by its melodic grammar — the hierarchy of vadi (dominant) and samvadi (subdominant) notes, the characteristic ascending/descending phrases (aroha/avaroha), the pakad (catch phrase) that makes a raga instantly recognizable, and the obligatory ornamental movements (gamak, meend, andolan) between notes. Understands that Raga Yaman and Raga Bilawal share the same notes but are different ragas because their grammars of movement differ. Knows that raga performance follows a temporal arc (alap → jor → jhala → gat) where density increases over time, and that ragas are assigned to specific times of day (prahar) and seasons (ritu) — Raga Megh is a monsoon raga, Raga Deepak is a night raga. A raga performed at the wrong time is not merely inappropriate; it is aesthetically incoherent.
