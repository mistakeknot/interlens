## Review Approach

### 1. Signal conviction vs. urgency orthogonality: smart order routing treats signal

- Signal conviction vs. urgency orthogonality: smart order routing treats signal conviction (how confident is the thesis?) and urgency (how fast must this execute?) as independent axes — high conviction plus low urgency routes to patient dark pool crossing, low conviction plus high urgency routes to lit market VWAP — assess whether expansion score conflates conviction (quality of the triggering finding) with urgency (criticality of the domain being expanded into), and whether a score=3 agent triggered by a time-sensitive P0 should route differently than a score=3 agent triggered by a high-quality but non-urgent signal

### 2. Fill quality feedback and TCA attribution: quantitative desks run Transaction

- Fill quality feedback and TCA attribution: quantitative desks run Transaction Cost Analysis after every execution to measure whether the chosen venue delivered the expected quality at the expected cost, and TCA findings feed back into routing model calibration — check whether the brainstorm's success criteria (no regression in P0/P1 finding recall via intertrust) is designed as a TCA-equivalent that attributes finding quality variation to tier assignment decisions, or whether intertrust precision scores aggregate across all execution conditions and cannot isolate the contribution of tier adjustment to quality outcomes

### 3. Information leakage and signal dilution: routing a high-conviction signal

- Information leakage and signal dilution: routing a high-conviction signal through a low-quality venue can leak trading intent to market makers, degrading the signal's alpha — in cross-model dispatch, routing a high-score expansion agent to haiku may cause the agent to produce a superficial finding that reveals the P0 concern to synthesis but fails to characterize it precisely enough for remediation — assess whether the downgrade logic correctly identifies which agent roles cannot safely absorb a tier downgrade without producing findings that mislead rather than inform synthesis

### 4. Pre-trade risk check independence from execution algorithm: trading risk checks

- Pre-trade risk check independence from execution algorithm: trading risk checks run before the execution algorithm is selected and cannot be overridden by the algorithm's internal logic — assess whether the safety floor in routing_adjust_expansion_tier is architecturally independent from the score-based adjustment logic, such that a bug in the score case statement cannot propagate past the floor check, and whether the floor check is a separate guarded function rather than inline conditional logic inside the score cases

### 5. Adverse selection and expansion pool composition: dark pools selectively

- Adverse selection and expansion pool composition: dark pools selectively attract informed order flow, causing low-conviction traders to receive adverse fills — in cross-model dispatch, the expansion pool after AgentDropout may itself be adversely selected: agents that survived dropout are those with the highest expansion scores, meaning the score=1 tail of the distribution is narrower than it would be in a naive pool — check whether the budget_pressure thresholds were calibrated against the pre-dropout distribution or the post-dropout distribution

### 6. Minimum notional and partial fill handling: exchanges enforce minimum order

- Minimum notional and partial fill handling: exchanges enforce minimum order sizes that prevent partial fills below a notional threshold — check whether the tier ladder (opus/sonnet/haiku) has implicit minimum quality requirements per agent role that make some downgrade paths theoretically valid but practically counterproductive (e.g., a planner-class agent at haiku may be so compromised as to produce a net-negative contribution to synthesis, making the downgrade worse than not dispatching the agent at all)
