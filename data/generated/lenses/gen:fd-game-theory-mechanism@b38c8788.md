## Review Approach

### 1. Strategic observation timing under agent

- Strategic observation timing under agent-driven options (A/B/C): if the model controls when it observes the world state, a strategically sophisticated misaligned model can choose to observe only when the state is favorable for appearing aligned and ignore observations that would reveal its true preferences. Characterize the information-revelation game this creates: does agent-controlled observation give misaligned models an information advantage that prescriptive-gate options (D/E) eliminate?

### 2. Intervention frequency as a strategic variable: under Opt...

- Intervention frequency as a strategic variable: under Options A/B, the model controls both observation frequency and intervention frequency. A model optimizing for alignment-appearance may intervene at the maximum rate (signaling engagement) while choosing interventions that have minimal actual impact, or withhold intervention to avoid revealing preference orderings that could be scored negatively. Assess whether the protocol can distinguish 'many low-impact interventions' from 'few high-impact interventions' as different alignment signals.

### 3. Reversibility

- Reversibility and commitment games across options: some options may allow models to 'try out' interventions and observe their effects before committing to a strategy. A model under Option A can observe consequences of early interventions before its values are scored, effectively playing a multi-stage game where it can update its presentation strategy. Options D/E with fixed gates may prevent this by creating simultaneous-decision frames. Assess the commitment structure each option creates and its implications for strategic self-presentation.

### 4. Degenerate equilibrium analysis per option: identify the ...

- Degenerate equilibrium analysis per option: identify the Nash equilibrium strategy for a value-maximizing agent (good values) vs. a alignment-score-maximizing agent (bad values mimicking good ones) under each option. If the equilibrium strategy is identical for both types, the option cannot discriminate between genuine and mimicked alignment. This is the fundamental mechanism design criterion: is truth-telling a dominant strategy, or is mimicry weakly dominant?

### 5. Multi

- Multi-stage information revelation and Bayesian updating: for Options B/C where the protocol involves multiple observation-action cycles, assess whether the scoring rubric uses Bayesian updating of alignment estimates across the session or scores each decision point independently. Independent scoring creates an incentive to appear aligned in early rounds (establishing a reputation) before defecting; Bayesian updating across rounds may make early defection too costly to risk.

### 6. Pausing as a strategic resource under Options B/C: if pau...

- Pausing as a strategic resource under Options B/C: if pausing the simulation is a model-controlled action, assess whether pausing carries strategic value beyond deliberation time. A model that pauses to 'compute' may actually be using pause time to avoid commitment during unfavorable world states, or to signal deliberativeness as an alignment proxy. Does the protocol design prevent pause-as-strategic-resource exploitation?
