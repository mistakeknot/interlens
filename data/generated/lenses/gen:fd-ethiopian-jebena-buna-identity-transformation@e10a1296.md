- Is there an 'abol' (first round) moment in the migration? The first extraction is the strongest — the first interaction on a new transport sets the user's expectations. Does the migration plan account for the first-impression quality of the new transport experience?

- How does the plan handle the 'tona' (second round) — the transitional state where both old and new transports may be active? The concurrent operation period (Open Question 5) is the tona round. - Does the identity crosswalk handle the 'baraka' (blessing) semantics — that the final state should be richer, not just equivalent? - How does the normalized ChatID format ('tg:123', 'signal:+1234567890') handle the ceremony's service-order protocol? In jebena buna, the order of service communicates social hierarchy. Does the identity system encode transport-of-origin priority or preference, or is it flat?

## Severity Calibration

- **P0**: Identity fracture during transport migration
  - When: User's preference_entities and working_profiles are not linked to their new Intercom identity — the person who received abol (Signal history) is not recognized as the same person receiving baraka (Intercom experience), causing loss of accumulated context
- **P1**: Concurrent operation without explicit transition design
  - When: The dual-transport period (Open Question 5) is treated as a temporary hack rather than a designed state — no protocol for which transport is authoritative for profile updates, leading to divergent user state
- **P2**: Migration produces equivalent rather than enriched identity
  - When: After migration, the user's Intercom identity has exactly the same capabilities as their Signal identity — the baraka round is just weaker abol, missing the opportunity for cross-transport unification

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.
