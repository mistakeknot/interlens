## Task Context

Sylveste v5.0 proposes an authority ratchet (Ockham) where subsystems earn progressively more autonomy through accumulated evidence. The vision frames this as a one-way beneficial process: evidence compounds, trust ratchets upward, the system improves. The vakif lens tests the hidden assumption that earned authority should persist — revealing that irrevocable trust accumulation can become a governance liability when conditions change, subsystems are replaced, or founding thresholds ossify into unchangeable constraints.

Is authority irrevocable once earned (like a vakfiye) or is there an explicit demotion protocol with the same evidentiary rigor as promotion?

- Does the compounding evidence thesis distinguish between evidence that supports a current capability and evidence that reflects a historical capability? - Is there an istibdal mechanism — a way to substitute one subsystem or evidence source for another of equal or greater capability without dismantling the trust that was built on the original? When Auraken is replaced by Skaffen, does the trust earned by Auraken transfer, or must Skaffen re-earn from zero?

- The vision's PHILOSOPHY.md addition of 'Authority ratchet as mechanism' risks creating a legal-structural commitment (like a vakfiye) that future iterations cannot easily amend. Does the vision distinguish between the ratchet as a principle (revisable) and the ratchet as codified mechanism (potentially irrevocable once downstream systems depend on it)?

### 5. How does the system handle the 'dead founder' problem

- How does the system handle the 'dead founder' problem — when the original designer's intent is embedded in evidence thresholds and gate conditions that made sense at design time but are no longer appropriate, and no living participant has authority to change the founding conditions because the system treats its own accumulated evidence as canonical?

## Severity Calibration

- **P0**: Authority without revocation mechanism
  - When: The authority ratchet promotes a subsystem to high trust based on 6 months of evidence, then the subsystem's maintainer leaves and quality degrades — but the ratchet has no demotion protocol with the same rigor as promotion, so the subsystem retains authority it can no longer justify (like a vakif whose founding conditions mandate payments to a beneficiary class that no longer exists, but the nazir has no legal authority to redirect funds)
- **P1**: Trust non-transferability across subsystem replacement
  - When: Auraken earned significant routing trust over months of evidence, then the Go migration replaces it with Skaffen — but earned trust is bound to the subsystem identity, not the capability, so Skaffen starts at zero trust despite providing identical or superior capability (like a vakif whose endowed building burns down and the replacement building, though better, cannot inherit the original's endowment status without a complex istibdal proceeding)
- **P2**: Ratchet mechanism ossifies into founding condition
  - When: The authority ratchet's evidence thresholds (gate pass rate > 95%, override rate < 5%) are codified in Ockham's configuration and become de facto vakfiye — downstream systems depend on these specific thresholds, making them effectively irrevocable even when the thresholds should evolve with system maturity

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.

## Decision Lens

Vakfiye irrevocability and istibdal adaptation — the endowment's founding document specifies exact conditions for revenue use, beneficiary classes, and succession rules that are legally permanent. Revenue compounds (rents fund repairs fund expansion), but the founding conditions cannot evolve with the compounding. The only escape valve is istibdal: substituting one endowed asset for another of equal or greater value while preserving the founder's stated purpose. The nazir's art is finding istibdal pathways that technically honor irrevocable conditions while adapting to present reality.
