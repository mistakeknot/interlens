## Severity Calibration

- **P0**: Threshold-as-aspiration without threshold-as-measurement
  - When: F4b's acceptance criterion: 'A benchmark demonstrates the engine remains tractable at the North Star scale — at least one synthetic test with 10,000 Souls, simulated event accumulation across centuries, hitting compaction. Target acceptance bar to be set during planning, not in this PRD; "tractable" is named here as the goal.' This is the cellar equivalent of 'the wine will be ready when it's ready' — a measurement is named (10k Souls, centuries) but the pass condition is not. Winemakers know what happens when you bottle on schedule rather than on chemistry.
- **P1**: Reversibility claimed where bottling actually occurs
  - When: F1 chooses an interim extraction shape (B or C) with shape A deferred. The PRD frames this as reversible ('cutover trigger for the deferred shape'). But once the migration epic opens with shape B's git topology, every downstream commit assumes that topology. Is the racking-back cost (rewriting commit history? reorganising the workspace mid-migration?) named and accepted? Or is the reversibility nominal?

- **P2**: No triangle test — design validated by author alone
  - When: F2's playtest card 'exercises the design' but the acceptance criterion does not name who else exercises it. In tasting-room terms: the winemaker passes the wine because they pass it; no second palate is involved. The card-test is good craft, but its acceptance condition needs a panel.
- **P2**: Decision documents as pre-fermentation notes, not cellar log
  - When: The four artifacts gate the migration but the PRD does not specify whether they remain authoritative reference during the migration (the cellar log read every day) or whether they are filed at PRD close and not consulted again. - **P3**: Missing organoleptic check on the whole vintage
  - When: The PRD-as-whole acceptance lists four conditions but does not include a step where the four documents are read together by a single reviewer who pronounces them collectively ready. Wine equivalent: each tank is logged, but no one tastes the final blend before bottling.
