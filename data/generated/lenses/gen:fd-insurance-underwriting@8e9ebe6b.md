## Review Approach

### 1. Construct definition before signal extraction: the brainstorm names four

- Construct definition before signal extraction: the brainstorm names four constructs to extract (taste, brand affinity, spending patterns, discovery avoidance of already-owned items) — assess whether each construct has an explicit operational definition that maps observable email signals to the construct, or whether 'brand affinity' is implicitly defined as 'brands that appear in order confirmations' without accounting for gift purchases, work expenses, or one-time purchases that do not reflect genuine affinity

### 2. Observable proxy vs. claimed construct gap: 'spending rhythm' extracted from

- Observable proxy vs. claimed construct gap: 'spending rhythm' extracted from order confirmation timestamps is a proxy for purchase frequency, but order confirmation date lags purchase decision by hours-to-days and may reflect shipping batching rather than purchase intent — assess whether the pipeline's extraction logic is designed around what the email data actually timestamps (order placed vs. shipped vs. confirmed) and whether the preference model correctly labels the construct it is measuring

### 3. Feedback loop contamination: once Auraken uses purchase history to make

- Feedback loop contamination: once Auraken uses purchase history to make recommendations, future purchases may be influenced by those recommendations — assess whether the preference model is designed to distinguish pre-import baseline behavior from post-import Auraken-influenced behavior, and whether the feedback loop is acknowledged in the model design or treated as unproblematic

### 4. Gift purchase signal contamination: orders shipped to addresses other than the

- Gift purchase signal contamination: orders shipped to addresses other than the account holder's default — gifts — appear in order confirmations as purchases but do not reflect the buyer's own taste — assess whether the pipeline has any mechanism to flag or exclude gift orders from preference signal extraction, or whether it incorporates them as unweighted taste signals

### 5. Category distribution as revealed preference: the brainstorm mentions category

- Category distribution as revealed preference: the brainstorm mentions category distribution as a pattern — assess whether the category taxonomy used is derived from the email data itself (Amazon product categories embedded in order confirmation HTML) or imposed post-hoc, and whether the category assignment for digital goods (App Store, Steam, Kindle) is comparable across retailer parsers

### 6. Cold-start and sparse history calibration: for users with fewer than 10

- Cold-start and sparse history calibration: for users with fewer than 10 purchases across all categories, extracted constructs like 'brand affinity' and 'spending rhythm' have very low statistical support — assess whether the preference model expresses confidence or uncertainty on construct estimates based on signal density, or whether it treats a 3-purchase brand history identically to a 50-purchase brand history

## Severity Calibration

- **P1**: A user buys 15 items as gifts in December — the preference model extracts brand affinity and category distribution signals from these gift purchases and uses them to weight future recommendations — the user's taste profile is significantly distorted by gift-buying behavior that does not reflect their own preferences, and there is no correction path because the pipeline has no gift signal
  - When: When the parser does not attempt to detect gift orders (shipping address != billing address, or 'this is a gift' flag in Amazon order confirmation HTML) and the preference model does not discount or flag purchases as potentially non-self-directed
- **P1**: A user has 8 Amazon purchases over 3 years — the preference model extracts 'spending rhythm: infrequent, considered' and 'brand affinity: [brands from 8 orders]' with the same confidence representation as a user with 200 purchases — Auraken uses these signals to make recommendations that overfits to a sparse and potentially unrepresentative purchase history
  - When: When the preference model stores extracted constructs without a confidence or sample-size signal that downstream recommendation logic can use to discount low-support inferences
- **P2**: Amazon order confirmation timestamps are used to compute 'spending rhythm' — but the user's email client applies a filter that batches Amazon order confirmations into a weekly digest, so confirmed timestamps all cluster on Sunday mornings — the preference model infers 'Sunday purchase pattern' which is an artifact of email filtering, not purchase behavior
  - When: When the pipeline uses email receipt timestamp rather than order-placed date (embedded in the Amazon confirmation HTML) as the temporal signal for spending rhythm extraction

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.
