## Review Approach

### 1. Fixed-pitch constraint as craft

- Fixed-pitch constraint as craft: letterpress printers never fight the fixed grid — they work with it, using white space, weight contrast, and rhythmic repetition as the primary expressive vocabulary for TUI enrichment

### 2. Ink density as tone

- Ink density as tone: half-block and braille characters have distinct 'ink coverage' values (12.5% to 100%) that function as a continuous tone scale — this is the gray ramp that video-to-ASCII conversion must map against

### 3. Relief inversion

- Relief inversion: letterpress prints both relief (raised inked surface) and blind (uninked impression) — dark-on-light vs. light-on-dark ASCII art has fundamentally different perceptual weight and should not be mixed without intent

### 4. Leading and tracking

- Leading and tracking: the terminal's fixed line height and character width are the leading and set-width — procedural ASCII art generation must respect these as optical constants, not incidental

### 5. Typeface mixing risk

- Typeface mixing risk: using braille, half-block, box-drawing, and standard ASCII characters in the same cell region is equivalent to mixing six typefaces on one page — it reads as chaos unless each has a distinct semantic role

## Severity Calibration
