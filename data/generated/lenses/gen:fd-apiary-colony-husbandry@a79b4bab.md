## Task Context

Garden Salon has pivoted from git-repo-backed project gardens to an infinite-canvas chat space where topics are spatial regions; history is geography that cools in place, and concurrent topics are the default and a tangent with traction buds into a new region; Each participant's agent tends the canvas through the existing proposal path under a propose-not-move rule with three v1 actions — cluster unplaced messages, propose a summary card when a region cools, and post a private per-arrival digest — where low-stakes proposals resolve by silence-is-consent after a cooling period. Auth moves to atproto OAuth with DID as the user key. The launch gate is a specific ten-person X group chat cohort actually moving in and running three concurrent topics for a week without anyone asking 'wait, where was that thing'. Review goal: stress the design before build phases start — does propose-not-move hold under real group-chat dynamics, and what is missing or over-scoped for v1.

## Review Approach

### 1. Disturbance budget

- Disturbance budget — every cluster, summarize, and digest is a lid lifted. Ten members, ten agents, N regions, and no cap, no batching, and no purpose test before an agent may propose.

### 2. Silence-is-consent makes ignoring load-bearing

- Silence-is-consent makes ignoring load-bearing — a member cannot cheaply decline to look, which is the exact property that makes notification-heavy chat exhausting to live in.

### 3. Entrance diagnosis

- Entrance diagnosis — whether the zoomed-out view answers 'what happened, does anything need me' without opening a region, and what an agent may decide from entrance-legible signals alone.

### 4. Minimum viable density

- Minimum viable density — the scale question asked downward: ten async people across three-plus regions may be too sparse to keep any of them warm, and a cold room reads as a dead one.

### 5. Absconding versus swarming

- Absconding versus swarming — leaving quietly in week one is a different failure from outgrowing the canvas, with different early indicators and opposite countermeasures. Neither is named.

### 6. Seeding with drawn comb

- Seeding with drawn comb — v1 hives the cohort onto an empty plane rather than regions pre-drawn from their existing recurring topics, which is the classic absconding setup.

### 7. Mis-clustering as cross-comb

- Mis-clustering as cross-comb — the highest-frequency action, taken unilaterally, with no accuracy expectation, no cheap correction path, and no stated right to re-place your own message.

### 8. Bee space between regions

- Bee space between regions — no minimum gap, no maximum, no rule for interstitial content, and centers of mass that drift as regions accrete, so neighbors weld together over months.

### 9. Flow versus dearth

- Flow versus dearth — cooling and consent windows on wall-clock time misfire in both directions as the cohort's own rate swings, and nothing in v1 feeds the room during a dearth.

### 10. Robbing the weak region

- Robbing the weak region — attention and summarize-on-cool both act against small slow topics precisely when exposure makes them fragile; merges are specified as atomic, not gradual.

### 11. Drift and salience

- Drift and salience — placement is never neutral; content accretes toward whatever is central or recently warm, and nothing measures per-region intake or compensates for the skew.

### 12. Contamination in the comb

- Contamination in the comb — blocking removes the person, not what they wrote into shared geography; open question 5 has no equivalent of the burn-the-frames rule.

## Severity Calibration

- **P0**: Ten async people spread across concurrent regions cannot keep any of them warm
  - When: The success criterion is itself the risk. Three concurrent topics across ten async members is roughly three people per region, and async means those three are rarely present together. A linear group chat forces all ten people's attention through one channel — that forcing function is why a small group chat feels alive, because a message posted at 2am has ten readers by morning. Regions on a canvas deliberately destroy it: a message in a cool region has an audience of whoever happens to wander there. A colony below a critical cluster size in an oversized cavity cannot hold heat and dies with full stores, and the beekeeper's countermeasure is always to reduce the space — follower boards, drop to a nuc, tighten the entrance — never to add room. This design has no reducer anywhere: the canvas is infinite, budding is encouraged and free, and open question 6 asks only what breaks from above (Yjs doc size, relay fan-out, render density). The first failure will not be a ceiling. It will be the room reading dead at the zoom level where people decide whether to engage, around week two, and it is architectural rather than tunable: the countermeasures are a bounded canvas at launch, a hard cap on concurrent live regions, and a consolidation action that folds cold regions back into warm ones. All three are cheap to decide now and expensive once the geography carries meaning.
- **P0**: The tending vocabulary has no disturbance budget, so governance reintroduces the churn the cohort is fleeing
  - When: Propose-not-move settles who may move things; it never prices what proposing costs. Each of ten participants has an agent; agents cluster continuously, propose condensation whenever a region cools, and digest on every arrival. Each proposal is a demand on attention, and silence-is-consent makes ignoring one load-bearing — a member cannot cheaply decline to look, because not looking is a vote. That is the precise property that makes notification-heavy chat exhausting, reintroduced through the mechanism meant to make the canvas calm. The classic way a novice kills a colony is not neglect; it is a successful intervention followed by inspections to check whether it worked. Missing and settable only in the first build phase: a hard budget on proposals per member per day, batching into a single review at arrival rather than a live stream, a stated question each proposal must answer before an agent may raise it, and a rule that an unread proposal expires with no effect rather than resolving by consent. Note the interaction with the cooling period — lengthening the window to protect absent members increases the number of proposals in flight at once, so the two knobs fight.
- **P1**: The cohort is hived onto bare foundation in the one week the old home still costs nothing
  - When: A swarm hived onto drawn comb stays; hived onto bare foundation it absconds at a far higher rate, because the box demands weeks of work before returning anything. The launch gate is a specific ten-person cohort moving in, and v1 offers them an infinite empty plane plus agents eager to cluster — maximum work, maximum disturbance, minimum payoff — during the exact week the X group chat is still running, still warm, and free. The highest-leverage item absent from v1 scope is seeding: the cohort's actual recurring topics standing as named regions holding real prior material before anyone arrives, so the first visit is recognition rather than homework. Related and equally unscoped: a deliberately reduced-tending protocol for the first two weeks, since that is when disturbance tolerance is lowest and when every clustering decision is being made on a canvas with no established geography to cluster against.
- **P1**: Mis-clustered messages compound because correction is more expensive than the error
  - When: Cluster is the highest-frequency action in the system and the only one agents take unilaterally, justified because unplaced content had no place to lose. But placement is what later placement is relative to: a message filed into the wrong region becomes context for the next clustering decision and material for the summary that eventually condenses that region. Comb drawn crooked never stays a local defect — every frame after it is built against it and the hive welds into one mass that cannot be opened without cutting. The design states no accuracy expectation for clustering, gives a human no way to say 'this does not belong here' cheaper than the full proposal path, and does not establish that an author may always re-place their own message freely. That last rule alone contains the whole failure mode and costs one permission check; without it the cheapest correction is more expensive than the error, so errors accumulate.
- **P1**: Cooling thresholds on wall-clock time misfire in both directions
  - When: Cooling drives summarize proposals and silence-is-consent windows drive whether reorganization lands, and both assume a stable metabolic rate. The cohort's real rhythm swings hard: a shared-obsession week where every region runs hot, then ten days in August when nobody posts. A threshold tuned during a flow will, in a dearth, propose condensing live conversations into tombstones and declare the whole canvas cold; tuned for the dearth, it never fires during a flow, and the same wall-clock cooling period that is generous mid-week silently converts a weekend into consent. Beekeepers never set a calendar date for any of this — the schedule is relative to the flow, and the correct dearth action is to feed, not to harvest. Cooling and consent windows should be expressed against a rolling baseline of the salon's own recent rate, and the system needs a feeding action for weeks when nothing is coming in — a seeded prompt, the house agent resurfacing a cold thread. v1 has neither, and a canvas that only ever condenses has exactly one long-run state.

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.

- Provides a concrete failure scenario for each P0/P1 finding — what breaks, under what conditions, and who is affected

- Bounds the canvas and caps concurrent live regions at launch, and pairs budding with a consolidation action that folds cold regions back into warm ones
- Names week-one absconding indicators — return latency, one person doing all the placing, the old X chat still running in parallel — and a reduced-tending protocol for that window
- Seeds the canvas with the cohort's real recurring topics as named regions holding prior material, so the first visit is recognition rather than homework
- Gives every author an always-free right to re-place their own message, plus a clustering-accuracy expectation and a correction path cheaper than the proposal path
- Expresses cooling and consent windows against a rolling baseline of the salon's own activity rate rather than wall-clock time, and defines a feeding action for dearth weeks
- Makes region merges gradual rather than atomic: merged regions stay separable for a defined window during which either can be pulled back apart
- Defines the spacing invariant that keeps neighboring regions separable after months of accretion, enforced at write time rather than by periodic cleanup
- Measures per-region intake so drift toward salient regions is visible, and says what the layout does to compensate
