## Task Context

The brainstorm proposes mapping expansion scores to model tiers. Score 3 keeps model, score 1 downgrades. Safety floors protect critical agents.

## Review Approach

### 1. Triage scoring granularity

- Triage scoring granularity — ED uses 5-level ESI; expansion score has only 4 levels (0-3). Is this sufficient for meaningful differentiation?

### 2. Over-triage vs under-triage tradeoffs

- Over-triage vs under-triage tradeoffs — in ED, over-triage wastes resources but under-triage kills. What is the equivalent risk asymmetry here?

### 3. Reassessment protocol

- Reassessment protocol — ED patients get re-triaged on deterioration. Should agent tier be adjustable mid-review if early findings change the picture?

### 4. Resource ceiling effects

- Resource ceiling effects — when ED is at capacity, even high-acuity patients get degraded care. The budget pressure mechanism mirrors this, but does it degrade gracefully?

### 5. Triage nurse expertise

- Triage nurse expertise — the triage decision itself requires capability. Who validates that the expansion scoring is accurate enough to drive tier decisions?

## Severity Calibration

- **P1**: Under-triage: a genuinely critical finding domain gets haiku because the expansion signal was weak
  - When: Expansion score fails to capture actual domain criticality
- **P2**: No reassessment: agent locked to haiku even after its partial findings suggest it needs more capability
  - When: Tier assignment is one-shot with no upgrade path

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.
