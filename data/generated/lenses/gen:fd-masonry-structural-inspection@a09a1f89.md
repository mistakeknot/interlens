## Task Context

Reviewing encryption and privacy architecture options for Garden Salon. The governance system uses content hashing, snapshot captures, and state machines that currently assume server-side plaintext access. The design must determine which governance operations can work on encrypted content and which require the encryption boundary to be drawn around them.

## Review Approach

### 1. Whether governance can operate on structural properties r...

- Whether governance can operate on structural properties rather than content — a master mason inspects a wall by checking plumb, level, and bond pattern without needing to see every stone's interior; can governance operations (merge approval, staleness detection, succession triggers) work from encrypted-content metadata (hashes, timestamps, participation records) rather than plaintext?

### 2. Whether quality assurance happens before opacity

- Whether quality assurance happens before opacity — stones were inspected before being set in mortar, because afterward they were inaccessible; does the encryption design ensure content is validated (hashed, signed, governance-checked) at the client before encryption, rather than requiring the server to decrypt for verification?

### 3. Whether the mason's mark problem is solved

- Whether the mason's mark problem is solved — individual marks hidden inside walls served attribution but were unverifiable after construction; can content attribution survive encryption without requiring decryption to verify, or does encryption make attribution claims unfalsifiable?

### 4. Whether the system handles the 'flying buttress' case

- Whether the system handles the 'flying buttress' case — structural elements that are visible and load-bearing must remain inspectable even when the interior is sealed; which governance artifacts (proposals, votes, rationale) must remain in plaintext for the governance system to function, and is that boundary clearly defined?

### 5. Whether the decades

- Whether the decades-long construction timeline is accounted for — cathedrals took generations, and inspection standards had to persist across master mason successions; does the encryption design account for the fact that governance rules, inspection capabilities, and key holders will change over the garden's lifetime?

## Severity Calibration

- **P1**: Encryption makes governance proposals opaque to the governance engine, breaking the state machine
  - When: If the server cannot read governance proposals (direction merges, ownership transfers, policy changes) because they are encrypted, the governance system cannot enforce its own rules — the master mason who cannot inspect the wall before it bears load
- **P1**: Content hashing moves to the client but clients can submit fraudulent hashes with no server-side verification
  - When: If encryption pushes hash computation to the client and the server cannot verify hashes against content, the entire attribution and merge-integrity system rests on client honesty — mason's marks that cannot be inspected
- **P2**: No clear boundary between governance metadata (must be server-readable) and content (should be encrypted)
  - When: If the design does not explicitly enumerate which fields are governance-structural vs. content-private, implementation will either over-encrypt (breaking governance) or under-encrypt (leaking content through governance fields)

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.
