## Review Approach

### 1. Graduation as threshold vs. phase change

- Graduation as threshold vs. phase change: the brainstorm fires graduation when student skill matches mentor skill — this is a continuous threshold crossing, not a phase change. In chrysopoeia, the rubedo (completion) cannot occur until the citrinitas (dawning) is fully stabilized, and the citrinitas cannot begin until the albedo (purification) is complete — you cannot skip or rush a stage by accumulating more of the previous stage's currency. Does the apprenticeship arc have stages that must be qualitatively completed before the next begins, or is it purely XP accumulation?

### 2. Nigredo requirement

- Nigredo requirement: chrysopoeia begins with the nigredo — the dissolution of the initial form into an undifferentiated black mass. The student must first 'die' as an unformed practitioner before they can be refined. Does the apprenticeship arc have an initial dissolution phase where the student's prior aesthetic certainties are temporarily destabilized — a period of active confusion that is a necessary precursor to genuine formation, not a failure state?

### 3. Mutual transformation

- Mutual transformation: in chrysopoeia, the alchemist's own spiritual state affects the transmutation — a practitioner who has not undergone their own nigredo cannot successfully transmute another substance. Does the mentor change through the teaching relationship? A mentor whose personality axes or aesthetic position are completely unchanged by having taught for years is modeling instruction-as-service, not transmission-as-transformation.

### 4. Calcination vs. sublimation

- Calcination vs. sublimation: alchemical processes have different instruments — calcination (burning to ash) and sublimation (driving volatile essence upward) produce different intermediate products even from identical starting materials. Does the mentor's personality axis (patience, pride, boldness) determine which transformation process the student undergoes, or do all apprenticeships follow the same instrumental sequence regardless of the specific dyad?

### 5. The failed chrysopoeia

- The failed chrysopoeia: most alchemical attempts fail — not randomly, but at characteristic failure points. The most common failures are 'fixation failure' (the volatile essence won't stabilize) and 'tincture failure' (the coloring agent won't penetrate the base). Do apprenticeship arcs have characteristic failure modes beyond mood degradation — structural incompatibilities between student and mentor personality that produce a recognizable pattern of stuck or reversed development?

### 6. Philosopher's Stone as lineage artifact

- Philosopher's Stone as lineage artifact: in the tradition, a practitioner who achieves the rubedo possesses the Philosopher's Stone — a transmuting agent that can initiate the process in other substances without the Stone itself being depleted. Does a founder-mentor who creates a school become a transmuting agent — able to catalyze more students more efficiently than non-school mentors, with their completed aesthetic as the Stone that initiates the student's own transformation?
