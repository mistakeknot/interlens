## Task Context

Reviewing a first-principles civilizational taxonomy (8 domains, 40 sub-systems) for a simulation. The taxonomy must prioritize development effort toward sub-systems that are actual throughput bottlenecks, not just academically interesting.

## Review Approach

### 1. Rate

- Rate-limiting step identification: In a chemical plant, one unit operation determines total throughput. Which sub-systems are the rate-limiting steps for each domain? Is 'state capacity' (Domain 3) the rate-limiting step for policy implementation across all domains? Is 'semiconductor supply chains' (Domain 7) the bottleneck for AI development? Does the taxonomy identify these constraints?

### 2. Recycle stream accounting: Chemical processes with recycl...

- Recycle stream accounting: Chemical processes with recycle streams have different effective capacities than once-through processes. The taxonomy's feedback loops (e.g., credit cycle → unemployment → policy → credit cycle) are recycle streams. Does the taxonomy account for how recycle streams change the effective capacity of each domain — a financial system with a fast recycle loop has higher effective throughput than one without?

### 3. Heat integration

- Heat integration and energy recovery: Process plants recover waste heat from exothermic reactions to drive endothermic ones. Does the taxonomy identify analogous energy recovery between domains — where one domain's waste output is another domain's input? (E.g., military R&D waste → civilian technology, criminal network intelligence → security system improvement)

### 4. Process control

- Process control and instrumentation: Every chemical plant has a control system that maintains setpoints by adjusting control valves. Does the taxonomy specify control mechanisms for each domain — what variables are measured, what setpoints exist, what actuators adjust throughput? Or does it assume domains self-regulate?

### 5. Startup sequencing

- Startup sequencing and steady-state assumptions: Chemical plants have complex startup procedures where units must be brought online in specific order. The Open Questions section asks about implementation ordering — does the taxonomy specify a startup sequence, or does it assume all domains can be activated simultaneously?

### 6. Upset conditions

- Upset conditions and emergency shutdown: Process plants have emergency shutdown systems for upset conditions. Does the taxonomy model what happens when a domain enters an upset state (financial crisis, governance collapse) — is there an emergency shutdown that isolates the failing domain, or does upset propagate uncontrolled through all connected domains?

## Severity Calibration

- **P0**: The taxonomy lists 40 sub-systems as development targets without identifying which are rate-limiting bottlenecks — development effort is distributed uniformly when the system's behavior is determined by 5-8 bottleneck sub-systems
  - When: No throughput analysis or bottleneck identification exists in the taxonomy or implementation plan
- **P1**: Feedback loops (recycle streams) are listed as illustrative examples but their effect on domain capacity is not analyzed — a financial system with a 2-year credit cycle operates fundamentally differently from one with a 20-year cycle, but both would have identical parameterizations
  - When: Recycle stream cycle time is not a parameterized variable in the domain model
- **P2**: No upset-condition or emergency-shutdown mechanics exist — when a domain enters crisis, disruption propagates at the same rate as normal operations rather than triggering emergency containment or circuit-breaker behavior
  - When: The pressure cascade system has propagation but no circuit-breaker mechanics

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.

- Check whether feedback loop cycle times are treated as parameters that affect system behavior, not just topological features.
- Verify that the taxonomy includes upset-condition mechanics — what happens when a domain fails, and how is failure contained or propagated.

## Decision Lens

Prioritizes findings where the taxonomy fails to identify rate-limiting sub-systems that constrain the throughput of entire domain clusters. A taxonomy that lists 40 sub-systems without identifying which are bottlenecks is like a process flow diagram without capacity annotations — it shows the topology but not where the system will choke. Secondary priority: whether recycle streams (feedback loops that return output to earlier process stages) are properly accounted for, since they change the effective capacity of every upstream unit. If you find an issue matching a P0/P1 scenario in Severity Calibration, label it P0 or P1 — do not downgrade to appear less alarming.
