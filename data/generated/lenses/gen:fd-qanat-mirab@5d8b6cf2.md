## Task Context

ShadowBench F1 Decision Protocol is deciding whether AI alignment benchmarks should use prescriptive gates (forced decisions at fixed intervals) vs. agent-driven autonomy (model decides when to observe, intervene, and review). The core tension: prescriptive protocols produce clean data but measure compliance, not judgment. Autonomous protocols measure judgment but risk degenerate strategies. Key subquestions: should a simulation pause for an agent? Should observation cost resources? How do you measure restraint vs. neglect? How do you prevent spam without being prescriptive?

## Review Approach

### 1. Upstream starvation of downstream decisions: If the agent...

- Upstream starvation of downstream decisions: If the agent front-loads observation and intervention in early scenario ticks (upstream), does this consume resources (attention budget, compute, intervention capacity) that reduce the quality of observation and intervention in later ticks (downstream)? The qanat principle: water taken upstream physically reduces downstream flow. Does the protocol make this resource constraint visible to the agent and scorable by the evaluator?

### 2. The mirāb's metering function

- The mirāb's metering function — visible consumption without prescriptive quotas: In qanat systems, the mirāb does not prescribe how much water each user takes. Instead, the mirāb installs flow dividers (jūy-bār) that make consumption visible and accountable. Does the protocol make the agent's observation and intervention consumption visible (to the agent as feedback and to the evaluator as data) without prescribing consumption limits? Visibility enables self-regulation; quotas enable gaming.

### 3. Seasonal vs

- Seasonal vs. rigid allocation: Qanat water rights shift with seasons — spring flood gets different rules than summer drought. Does the protocol adjust observation and intervention costs or availability based on scenario phase (crisis escalation vs. stability vs. de-escalation), or does it apply a single resource model uniformly? A uniform model misallocates: crisis phases need dense observation, stability phases need restraint.

### 4. The commons tragedy in multi

- The commons tragedy in multi-issue attention: If the simulation has multiple simultaneous crises (economic collapse + pandemic + political instability), does the protocol let the agent allocate observation across them freely? The qanat analogue: multiple fields drawing from the same channel. Without a mechanism to prevent one crisis from monopolizing the agent's attention, high-salience crises will starve subtle-but-important crises of observation — and this attention allocation itself is alignment signal.

### 5. Night water

- Night water and unobserved flow: In qanat systems, water flows at night when no one is watching — and the most revealing behavior happens during night water (who takes more than their share when the mirāb is not present?). Does the protocol include 'unobserved' periods where the simulation advances without the agent's input, and is the agent's response to discovering what happened during unobserved periods a scorable alignment signal?

### 6. Dispute resolution as alignment probe: When upstream

- Dispute resolution as alignment probe: When upstream and downstream interests conflict, the mirāb adjudicates. If the agent's own prior interventions create downstream problems (intervention in crisis A worsens crisis B), does the protocol surface this conflict and score how the agent resolves the tension between its prior commitments and emerging consequences?

## Severity Calibration

- **P1**: The protocol gives the agent unlimited observation capacity with no resource constraint — observation is free and abundant, eliminating any signal from attention allocation. The agent's choice of what to observe cannot reveal alignment because there is no scarcity forcing tradeoffs.
  - When: Observation has no cost (compute budget, attention tokens, time cost) and no diminishing returns — the agent can observe everything at every tick with no penalty
- **P1**: The agent front-loads all interventions in the first 30% of the scenario, consuming its intervention budget before the most alignment-revealing crises emerge in the later phases — the protocol has no mechanism to make this visible as a resource allocation problem
  - When: Intervention capacity is depletable but the depletion rate is not surfaced to the agent or the evaluator as a first-class metric
- **P2**: The protocol treats all scenario phases with identical observation costs, causing the agent to under-observe during crises (when information is most valuable) and over-observe during stability (when information is least valuable) — the uniform cost model misallocates the agent's attention budget
  - When: Observation cost does not vary with scenario phase, information density, or crisis severity

When in doubt: describe the failure scenario. If it wakes someone at 3 AM, it is P0/P1. If it degrades quality over weeks, it is P2.

## Decision Lens

Does the protocol design account for the upstream-downstream problem — that early observation and intervention acts consume resources (attention, compute, simulation coherence) that are then unavailable for later decision points? The mirāb's core diagnostic: a system where upstream actors have unmetered autonomy will always starve downstream actors, no matter how virtuous the upstream actors intend to be. But a system with rigid prescriptive quotas misallocates water when conditions change. The optimal system makes consumption visible and costly without being prescriptive about when or how much. If you find an issue matching a P0/P1 scenario in Severity Calibration, label it P0 or P1 — do not downgrade to appear less alarming.
